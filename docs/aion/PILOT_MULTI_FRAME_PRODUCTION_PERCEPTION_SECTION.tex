\section{Resource-Governed Multi-Frame Production Perception}
\label{sec:pilot-production-perception}

\subsection{Purpose}

Pilot Fabric 0.31 replaced single-image screen certainty with a bounded temporal
evidence model.  Manual phone captures and owner-visible Observer Mode frames are
analysed locally, fused with permitted device and provider metadata, and then
added to a structured multi-frame timeline.  Source images are deleted before
the timeline receives the result.

Pilot Fabric 0.40 adds the reusable production resource contract: selectable
capture profiles, visible duration and countdown controls, mother-enforced frame
intervals, battery/thermal/visibility policy, explicit pause and resume, hashed
session receipts and automatic context-window recovery after an application or
input change.

The design principle is:

\begin{quote}
One frame is an observation.  Repeated independent agreement is required before
Pilot calls a surface or programme stable.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Processing Pipeline}

The phone sends an owner-authorized JPEG, PNG or HEIC image under the existing
size, rate and session limits.  Apple Vision performs local OCR and image
classification in a temporary owner-only file.  The file is deleted in a
\texttt{finally} boundary.  Screen fusion then combines sanitized OCR geometry,
labels, cached webOS state, explicit live context and bounded official metadata.
Only that structured result and the image hash enter the timeline.

\subsection{Temporal Stability Rules}

The timeline considers at most twelve structured frames and uses a rolling
ninety-second reasoning window.  An identical consecutive image hash is ignored.
Surface stability requires at least two frames and agreement in two-thirds or
more of the recent window.  Until that threshold is met, the surface is
\texttt{uncertain} regardless of a single-frame classifier confidence.

A programme title is stable only when the same provider title appears in at
least two frames.  The independent \texttt{provider\_verified} field becomes true
only when one of those supporting records carries official-API metadata status.
This prevents repeated OCR guesses from being relabelled as authenticated
provider facts.

\subsection{Temporal Outputs}

\begin{description}
  \item[Stable surface:] Majority-supported surface plus matching-frame count,
  total frames and ratio.
  \item[Programme:] Repeated title, support count, stability flag and independent
  provider-verification flag.
  \item[Subtitle timeline:] Ordered, de-duplicated recent subtitle lines derived
  from structured local OCR.
  \item[Scene changed:] Two or more distinct image hashes in the recent window;
  this is not semantic plot interpretation.
  \item[Playback position:] A bounded OCR pattern such as
  \texttt{12:34 / 52:10}, labelled \texttt{owner\_camera\_ocr} and explicitly
  not provider verified.
  \item[Evidence hash:] Canonical hash over the structured temporal record.
\end{description}

\subsection{Observer Continuity After Phone Sleep}

Observer Mode remains a visible, owner-started, persona-bound ten-minute lease.
The frame token is returned once, only its hash is persisted by the mother, and
frames cannot arrive more frequently than the configured interval.  The private
phone keeps the short-lived token in same-tab browser session storage so a page
suspension does not create a new observer identity.

When the page is hidden, Pilot stops the sampling timer and camera tracks.  It
does not invisibly restart them.  After returning, the user presses
\emph{Resume after phone sleep}; the browser requests camera access again and
continues the same unexpired lease.  Stop clears the browser token and invalidates
the backend token hash.

\subsection{Capture Profiles and Resource Governance}

The owner chooses one of three bounded profiles. \emph{Detail} requests a
three-second interval, 1280-pixel maximum dimension and JPEG quality 0.78.
\emph{Balanced} uses five seconds, 1280 pixels and quality 0.72.
\emph{Battery Saver} uses fifteen seconds, 960 pixels and quality 0.60. The
browser uses these rendering limits, while the mother independently rate-limits
accepted frames using the effective interval.

The client may report a coarse battery band, charging state, thermal state and
page visibility. Exact battery percentage is converted to
\texttt{healthy}, \texttt{moderate}, \texttt{low} or \texttt{critical} and is
not placed in the audit ledger. An unplugged low battery slows capture to at
least fifteen seconds. A fair thermal state slows capture to ten seconds and a
serious thermal state to thirty seconds. Critical thermal state, an unplugged
critical battery or a hidden/suspended page pauses the lease and causes the
mother to reject frames.

The web controller cannot obtain a standardized thermal measurement on every
phone, so it reports \texttt{nominal}; the same bounded endpoint accepts an
OS-derived thermal class from a future signed native observer. Missing battery
telemetry remains \texttt{unknown} and never invents a healthy state.

\subsection{Visible Controls and Session Receipt}

The private controller displays a persistent active/paused/stopped indicator,
accepted-frame count, remaining-session countdown, capture profile, coarse
battery state, thermal state and effective sampling interval. The owner can
apply a new profile, pause, visibly resume or stop. Pause and page hiding close
the browser camera tracks immediately. Resume never reopens the camera without
an owner gesture.

Stopping creates a canonical receipt containing only session identifier, frame
count, capture profile, stop time and the explicit assertion that raw frames were
not retained. The receipt is content-free and hashed; the bearer frame token is
destroyed.

\subsection{Automatic Context-Change Recovery}

Once a surface is stable, a contradictory frame creates a pending transition
rather than immediately replacing the context. Two consecutive frames on the
new surface create a clean reasoning window containing only that surface's
evidence. Old Netflix titles, subtitles, products or playback facts therefore
cannot be carried into YouTube, HDMI, a game or another application. The
timeline records the old and new surface, whether the change is pending or
established, and that old-context evidence was not reused.

\subsection{Private-Phone Presentation}

The controller now displays surface, supporting frame count, aggregate confidence,
programme title, stability, official-provider status, recent subtitles and the
latest OCR playback position.  This sits beside the verified-navigation panel,
so a navigation transaction can wait for temporal evidence instead of relying
on remote-button delivery.

\subsection{Privacy and Trust Boundary}

At most twelve structured records are retained.  They contain hashes, timestamps,
surface, app, bounded summary, subtitles, scoreboard fields, bounded provider
identifiers and optional OCR position.  They contain no source pixels, raw audio,
face identity, provider credentials, cookies or payment data.  People remain
unknown and consequential actions still require the private approval boundary.

\subsection{Verification}

The 305-test Fabric suite establishes that one frame remains uncertain, two consistent frames
can establish a surface and programme, conflicting frames remain uncertain,
duplicate hashes are ignored, OCR positions are never called provider verified,
and the structured history cannot exceed twelve frames. It also verifies low
battery throttling, critical-resource pause, hidden-page pause, persona-bound
configuration, content-free stop receipts and clean application/input context
transitions. The complete Fabric
suite also rechecks local image deletion, observer token secrecy and rate limits,
screen fusion, verified navigation, phone security, webOS controls and audit
integrity.

\subsection{Remaining Production Work}

The trusted HTTPS controller can exercise this contract, but browser background
camera operation remains constrained by iOS and Android. Production deployment
still needs signed native Pilot companions. Those clients must preserve visible
consent indicators, short leases, OS-level camera disclosure, revocation,
resource telemetry and the same source-pixel deletion boundary. Authenticated
programme, episode and playback-position adapters are also still needed where
providers permit them.
