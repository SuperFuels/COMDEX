\section{Long-Document Semantic Memory and Concept Composition}
\label{sec:long-document-semantic-memory}

AION completed a governed semantic-memory cycle over three complete
public-domain books comprising 143,164 words.  A replaceable language substrate
proposed answers and exact quotations from retrieved sentence windows.  HexCore
retained a proposal only when its quotation was recoverable from the immutable
source:
\[
  \text{book}
  \rightarrow \text{retrieved evidence}
  \rightarrow \text{proposal}
  \rightarrow \text{span verification}
  \rightarrow \text{concept--relation memory}.
\]
Reported content was not automatically marked as independently verified.

The retained graph preserved source-bound relations including
\(\texttt{white}\rightarrow\texttt{rabbit}\),
\(\texttt{pink}\rightarrow\texttt{eyes}\), and
\(\texttt{blue}\rightarrow\texttt{caterpillar}\).  A first proximity rule
incorrectly proposed \(\texttt{pink}\rightarrow\texttt{rabbit}\); delayed
structural evaluation detected the error, and the binding rule was restricted
to adjective-to-immediately-modified-noun relations.

The system constructed novel combinations such as \texttt{blue rabbit} and
\texttt{yellow machine}, but retained them only as hypothetical compositions.
No generated combination was converted into a factual claim.  Contradictory
reader notes were likewise retained with provenance rather than silently
resolved.

The promoted procedure is
\texttt{procedure\_long\_document\_semantic\_memory\_6d1736d5906a}.
The result remains bounded: the sources and questions are public development
material, retrieval and semantic types are engineered, and no independent
evaluator authored the cohort.
