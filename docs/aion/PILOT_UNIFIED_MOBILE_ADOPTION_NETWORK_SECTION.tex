\section{Pilot Trusted Network and Useful Adoption Loop}
\label{sec:pilot-mobile-adoption-network}

Pilot's adoption mechanism begins with useful coordination rather than an empty
social-network invitation.  A recipient sees who invited them, the exact bounded
request and the role or relationship being proposed.  Joining continues that
request and does not reveal unrelated sender, household or business information.

\subsection{Contacts and relationship scope}

The trusted-network authority supports household, family, friend and professional
relationships.  A high-entropy, seven-day invitation token is stored only as a
hash.  Inspection returns the sender's bounded display name, relationship, request
kind and summary.  Acceptance requires the intended recipient route and creates a
specific bilateral relationship rather than general access to either person's
Pilot.

Employer, client and Boardroom invitations additionally require an exact workspace
reference and one of participant, reviewer, approver or administrator roles.  The
accepted result retains the original useful request, such as a particular document
review, so onboarding has immediate value.

\subsection{Private contact discovery}

Address-book discovery is disabled without explicit consent.  When enabled, email
addresses and telephone numbers are normalized locally and converted into
epoch-bound HMAC tokens.  Unsupported entries are discarded, duplicates collapse,
and the request states that no readable contacts were uploaded.  The rotating
discovery key remains mother-private.  This is a candidate-discovery mechanism, not
permission to contact or invite every match.

\subsection{Activation evidence}

The authority records bounded creation and acceptance events for invitations.
Its activation view is aggregate-only, split by contact, employer, client and
Boardroom invitation types.  It contains no persona identifiers, addresses or
message content.  This allows Pilot to measure whether useful TV, task and workspace
entry points lead to adoption without centralizing conversation data.

\subsection{Export, deletion and revocation}

A person can export their invitations and relationship records with a canonical
export hash.  Either party can revoke an active relationship.  The relationship
becomes inactive immediately, future authorization checks fail, and a minimal
revocation tombstone preserves the audit fact.  Person deletion removes private
invitations and revokes every affected relationship; it does not silently leave an
agent-to-agent route open.

\subsection{Verification and remaining work}

Automated tests cover useful contact invitations, exact-request continuation,
employer/client/Boardroom roles, wrong-recipient denial, one-time acceptance,
consent-gated contact discovery, absence of readable address-book data, aggregate
metrics, export, revocation and deletion.  The composed handoff API spanning
messages, tasks, reminders, files and Moments is documented separately in
Section~\ref{sec:pilot-universal-handoffs}.  Remaining work comprises household,
freelancer, employee and Boardroom field pilots and independent security, privacy
and legal review.  Network growth does not require Tessaris to own the underlying
conversations or business records.
