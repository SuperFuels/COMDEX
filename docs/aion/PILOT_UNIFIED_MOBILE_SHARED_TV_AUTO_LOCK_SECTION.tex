\section{Trusted-Phone Presence and Automatic Shared-Television Locking}
\label{sec:pilot-mobile-shared-tv-auto-lock}

Pilot treats a television as a shared surface rather than as a trusted personal
computer.  A person's private television workspace is therefore a short-lived,
exclusive lease held by a separately trusted phone.  The implementation now closes
the remaining gap between explicit logout and passive abandonment of the room.

\subsection{Two independent expiry conditions}

The mother maintains two deadlines for the active shared-screen identity.  The
\emph{inactivity deadline} is renewed only by a real accepted interaction, such as a
directional television command or an authorized experience action.  The
\emph{presence deadline} is renewed by a possession-signed heartbeat received
directly from the paired phone on a private, loopback or link-local network address.
Both deadlines are bounded to five minutes in the current consumer configuration.
The session is valid only while both conditions remain true.

This separation prevents a background heartbeat from disguising an abandoned and
unused television as active.  It also prevents television speech or another remote
surface from keeping private tiles unlocked after the trusted phone has left the
local boundary.  A direct remote route or opaque relay may carry an authorized
command, but it is deliberately insufficient evidence that the possession device is
still in the room or household.

\subsection{Acquisition, renewal and release}

Acquisition continues to require the existing Ed25519 possession signature, active
persona, trusted device record and mobile capability lease.  A second person cannot
silently displace a live session.  The phone sends a local presence proof every
thirty seconds while the application is visible and online.  Passive presence
updates only \texttt{last\_presence\_at} and
\texttt{presence\_expires\_at}; it never changes
\texttt{last\_activity\_at} or the inactivity deadline.

Explicit logout remains immediate and is signed by the controlling phone.  Spoken
logout uses the existing governed television route.  Device revocation and account
recovery also clear the active identity.  If either deadline expires, the next
authoritative read atomically removes the active identity and records whether the
cause was \texttt{inactivity\_timeout} or
\texttt{trusted\_phone\_departed}.  A revoked or unknown phone cannot renew
presence.

\subsection{Interface and privacy behaviour}

The unified phone displays whether the television is locked, controlled by this
person or controlled by another household member.  It explains the inactivity and
nearby-phone boundary beside the takeover control.  When an expiry is observed, the
controller returns to the locked state and requires a new signed takeover.

The shared-surface authority already issues only public tiles without an active
identity and purges private presentation records before issuing a locked manifest.
Automatic expiry therefore reaches the same privacy state as explicit logout:
private task, calendar, message, approval, file and Boardroom material is not retained
as television presentation data.  Authoritative personal and business records remain
on the customer-controlled mother and are not deleted merely because a screen locks.

\subsection{Protocol boundary}

The phone uses the dedicated
\texttt{/v1/personal/tv/presence} operation.  Its request is covered by the normal
phone certificate, capability lease and request signature and requires the
\texttt{tv.control} scope.  The service additionally derives locality from the
observed client address; a caller-provided ``local'' flag is not trusted.  The
response contains deadlines and policy state but no private content, device key or
network address.

\subsection{Verification and remaining field qualification}

Automated tests prove separate activity and presence deadlines, departure expiry,
lock-reason recording, signed phone authority, rejection of non-local presence,
revoked-device denial and the mobile interface contract.  The complete AION Fabric
suite contains 567 tests, and the optimized mobile production build compiles.

A real two-person household exercise remains deliberately open.  It must demonstrate
takeover, ordinary use, five minutes without interaction, trusted-phone departure,
explicit logout and reacquisition on the physical television.  Automated time and
network-address tests are not represented as proof that a particular home router or
mobile operating system delivered every field transition.
