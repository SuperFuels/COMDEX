\section{Boardroom at Medium-Business Scale}
\label{sec:aion-medium-business-boardroom}

The small-business Boardroom remains the operating foundation.  The medium-business
extension does not create another Boardroom, another memory or another Business Map.
Instead, it allows one customer-owned AION brain to describe a group of companies and to
bind every legal entity back to its existing Boardroom workspace and canonical Business
Map.  This preserves one source of business truth while supporting the organisational
complexity that appears as a company grows.

\subsection{Why a Medium Business Is Structurally Different}

A larger organisation is not merely a small organisation with more users.  It may contain
several legal entities, brands, operating companies, countries, physical locations and
business units.  A food group, for example, may contain Fresh, Frozen, Produce, Biscuits
and Restaurants divisions, with different currencies, managers and regulatory constraints.
Its actual organisation may also bear little resemblance to a standard Sales--Marketing--
Finance chart.  Positions such as Head of Experience, Programme Controller or Regional
Transformation Lead may cross conventional departments and may have both a primary manager
and dotted-line accountability to a project or regional director.

The implemented model therefore treats job titles as descriptive facts, never as access
authority.  Authority comes only from an authenticated person, assigned roles, explicit
capabilities, bounded organisational scopes and the policy governing the proposed action.

\subsection{Requirements-Based Onboarding}

The onboarding flow does not ask the customer to declare that it is a small, medium or large
business.  Those labels differ between countries and do not predict technical or governance
needs.  It asks instead about legal entities, locations, countries, currencies, users, projects,
external collaborators, approval levels, separation of duties, enterprise identity, regulated
data and private compute.  A local requirements profiler recommends modules such as Core
Boardroom, Project Operations, Multi-Location, Multi-Entity Group, Delegated Authority, Bounded
External Workspaces, Enterprise Identity, Regulated Data Controls and Private Compute.

The recommendation explicitly records that no business-size label was used and that commercial
entitlement is decided separately.  A one-person regulated consultancy can therefore receive
sovereign controls while a much larger but operationally simple organisation can retain a simpler
interface.  Capabilities are progressively revealed from actual requirements rather than cluttering
every customer's starting screen.

\subsection{Canonical Group and Operating Model}

The medium-business operating record contains the following linked structures:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a group identity and multiple legal entities;
  \item nested divisions, business units, functions and teams;
  \item countries, regions and operating locations;
  \item people with arbitrary position titles, engagement types, primary managers and
        optional dotted-line managers;
  \item roles containing named capabilities and financial approval ceilings;
  \item projects containing teams, allocations, objectives, metrics, work items,
        milestones, risks and financial positions;
  \item organisation-specific workflows and escalation routes; and
  \item residency, retention, provider-region and separation-of-duty policies.
\end{itemize}

Identifiers are unique and references are validated before a revision can be committed.
Cycles in business-unit or primary reporting hierarchies are rejected.  Unknown people,
roles, projects, locations and escalation owners fail closed.  Concurrent edits use an
expected revision so that a stale browser cannot overwrite a newer organisation model.

Every entity must declare both a \texttt{boardroom\_workspace\_id} and a
\texttt{business\_map\_ref}.  When a department task is routed, AION emits a bounded
envelope containing the authenticated person, entity, unit, department, task class,
Boardroom workspace and Business Map reference.  The existing Boardroom department router
then performs the work.  The extension therefore composes with the already-built Boardroom
rather than duplicating its company knowledge or department agents.

\subsection{Projects, Teams and Financial Control}

Projects are first-class operating objects rather than notes attached to a department.
A project can draw a matrix team from several functions and can include employees,
freelancers, advisers, accountants or directors.  Allocation percentages are bounded from
zero to one hundred, and the project records the role each person performs on that project.

For each project, AION calculates:

\[
R=B-C,\qquad V=B-F,\qquad O=(F>B).
\]

where $B$ is approved budget, $C$ is committed expenditure, $F$ is forecast cost at
completion, $R$ is the remaining amount after commitments, $V$ is forecast variance and
$O$ is the over-budget indicator.  Actual expenditure is retained separately from both
commitments and forecast.

Portfolio totals are grouped by currency.  A GBP project and a EUR project are never added
together into a false group total.  If several currencies are present, the response explicitly
states that its cross-currency total was suppressed.  Model and inference expenditure is also
reported by currency.  Currency conversion may be added only through a dated, evidenced FX rate
with the original amounts retained.

The viewer-scoped portfolio includes authorised entities and projects, open risks, milestones,
objectives, metrics, work items and model expenditure.  A regional manager can therefore see
their own portfolio while the group director can see the wider group; neither view changes the
underlying authority.

\subsection{Identity, Matrix Work and Bounded Workspaces}

An enterprise identity-provider record declares an approved directory or single-sign-on
source.  An external provider subject resolves to exactly one active AION person.  Unknown,
disabled or duplicated subjects are denied.  The resulting person receives a workspace
projection containing only their allowed entities, units, locations and projects.

This supports conventional employees and directors as well as temporary and specialist work:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a freelancer can be restricted to one project and no general company files;
  \item an external accountant can receive the required finance scope without access to
        marketing, people or unrelated Boardroom material;
  \item a regional leader can span several locations without gaining group-wide authority;
  \item a project specialist can work across departments through an explicit project scope;
  \item a director can hold group scope while consequential actions remain approval gated.
\end{itemize}

The production deployment still requires the customer's chosen SAML, OIDC or directory
provider to be connected and qualified.  The binding contract is implemented, but no vendor
identity is silently trusted merely because an email address or job title matches.

\subsection{Delegation, Approval and Separation of Duties}

For every proposed action, AION evaluates the person, capability, entity, business unit,
location, project and amount.  A role may grant a capability but still be denied because the
requested project is outside the person's scope or the amount exceeds their limit.

Organisation workflows bind a request capability to a distinct approval capability.  The
requester is recorded in the action history; a configured separation-of-duty rule prevents
that same person from supplying the corresponding approval.  Amounts above the workflow
limit return a named escalation target instead of being treated as approved.  A model,
department agent or senior-sounding title cannot override this decision.

The operational sequence is:

\begin{quote}
authenticated person $\rightarrow$ scoped request $\rightarrow$ policy decision
$\rightarrow$ distinct approval or escalation $\rightarrow$ authorised capability
$\rightarrow$ verification $\rightarrow$ receipt.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Residency, Retention and Configurable Intelligence}

The routing policy is evaluated before business context is supplied to local or remote
compute.  Each data classification has an allowed residency set and a retention period;
each customer-approved provider has an allowed region set.  Missing classification policy,
an unknown provider, a disallowed residency or a disallowed provider region denies the route.
This applies equally to a public model API and to a model running in the customer's own cloud.

This policy connects directly to AION's configurable intelligence stack.  A group may permit
one local model for ordinary work, an EU-hosted private model for confidential analysis and a
separately approved specialist model for a bounded task.  Models remain replaceable compute:
none owns the Business Map, organisational memory, user authority or execution rights.

\subsection{Support, Audit and Privacy}

Support access is itself a named capability.  A redacted support bundle contains schema and
revision information, object counts, model and scope hashes, and a generation time.  It states
that customer content and secrets are excluded.  Customer-readable audit export similarly
requires explicit authority and redacts password, secret, token, private-key, payment-card,
salary and medical fields.  These controls allow a medium business to obtain diagnostics
without sending its operating model or confidential records to Tessaris.

\subsection{Implemented Interfaces and Verification}

The operating authority is available as a reusable Python service and through the local
Boardroom API.  The API supports revisioned configuration, state inspection, access and
workflow decisions, viewer-scoped portfolio and workspace views, and authorised support and
audit exports.  The same service is included in both the Sovereign Brain bootstrap and Pilot
Fabric distribution.

Automated qualification covers multi-entity structures, nested units, reporting-cycle
rejection, arbitrary job titles, project budgets and teams, currency separation, model spend,
scope and amount denial, separation of duties, escalation, department routing, enterprise
identity resolution, regional policy, redaction and revision conflicts.  Production release
still requires field qualification with a real multi-unit organisation, its identity provider,
regional infrastructure and retention/legal-hold procedures.

\subsection{Result}

Boardroom can now grow from an owner-operated company into a multi-entity operating system
without surrendering the customer's brain or flattening the organisation into generic
departments.  The medium-business advantage is not merely a larger dashboard: it is the ability
to give every person and every replaceable intelligence component precisely the business
context and authority required for their work, and no more.
