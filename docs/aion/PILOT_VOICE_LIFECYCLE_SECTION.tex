\section{Local Voice Lifecycle, Privacy History and Quiet Mode}
\label{sec:pilot-voice-lifecycle}

Pilot Fabric 0.36 introduced the visible voice lifecycle. Pilot Fabric 0.39
extends it with governed cancellation, start-anchored wake recognition,
scheduled quiet hours and explicit private-phone push-to-talk. The system
remains auditable without converting household speech into a behavioural log. The
microphone node runs locally on the mother brain using the cached speech model;
raw audio remains in a bounded ephemeral ring and is never written to the Fabric
ledger.

\subsection{Explicit State Machine}

The service publishes the states \texttt{listening}, \texttt{hearing},
\texttt{thinking}, \texttt{acting}, \texttt{speaking}, \texttt{completed},
\texttt{error}, \texttt{sleeping} and \texttt{off}.  \texttt{Hearing} means
that local energy detection has begun an utterance; it does not imply that Pilot
accepted a command.  After transcription, speech without an accepted wake phrase
returns to \texttt{listening} with the outcome class
\texttt{background\_rejected}.  An accepted intent advances through reasoning,
governed execution and response states.

This distinction is presented live on the trusted dashboard.  It prevents an
ambiguous ``online'' indicator from concealing whether Pilot is listening,
reasoning, operating a capability or delivering a result.

\subsection{Content-Free Activity History}

A bounded in-memory deque retains recent state transitions.  An entry contains a
state, a local timestamp, a short outcome class and two explicit false flags:
\texttt{contains\_transcript} and \texttt{contains\_audio}.  It therefore
supports privacy inspection and debugging without retaining what a person said.
The diagnostic transcript window remains volatile and limited; it is not copied
into the activity history, capability ledger or preference file.

\subsection{Sleep and Microphone Closure}

``Pilot, sleep'' closes the input stream, clears the audio ring, resets measured
audio level and marks the service \texttt{sleeping}.  Because capture is closed,
the service cannot hear a wake command while asleep.  Resumption requires the
local dashboard or another separately authorized private control.  This is a
physical privacy boundary rather than a software promise to ignore captured
audio.

\subsection{Quiet Mode and Local Voice Preferences}

Quiet mode suppresses synthesized replies while leaving command governance,
visual status and private-phone results active.  The owner can switch back to
spoken replies from the local dashboard.  Voice names are restricted to a local
allowlist and speech rate is clamped to 120--240 words per minute.  Preferences
are saved under the mother runtime, never projected to the television or device
nodes, and create a metadata-only audit event.

Pilot Fabric 0.39 adds local quiet-hour schedules expressed as a bounded pair of
24-hour wall-clock values. Schedules may cross midnight. An equal or incomplete
pair is inactive, malformed values fail closed, and the schedule is never
distributed to television nodes.

\subsection{Wake Boundary and Background Rejection}

The accepted wake phrase is anchored to the beginning of an utterance after an
optional short greeting such as ``Hey''. A television sentence that mentions
Pilot later in its dialogue therefore cannot activate a command. Bounded repairs
remain for established local-recognition errors, but there is no general fuzzy
match that can transform arbitrary programme audio into a wake event.

\subsection{Governed Cancellation}

``Pilot, cancel'' now propagates through the active television-agent task,
conversation objective, autopilot plan, verified-navigation transaction and all
reversible service proposals owned by the active persona. Waiting approvals are
marked cancelled, pending steps are skipped and active identifiers are cleared.
An external adapter in an uncertain in-flight state is deliberately not reported
as cancelled; Pilot instead requires reconciliation. The audit entry contains
layer names and counts rather than the raw utterance.

\subsection{Private-Phone Push-to-Talk}

The trusted HTTPS controller provides a hold-to-speak control. Pressing it is an
explicit activation boundary, so the owner need not repeat the room wake word.
The browser captures audio only while held and sends at most 3 MiB to the mother
brain. The local Whisper model transcribes an owner-only temporary file; a
\texttt{finally} boundary deletes it after both success and failure. Requests use
the private-phone conversation channel and active persona rather than shared-TV
memory.

Ordinary unencrypted LAN pages cannot request the iPhone microphone. Push-to-talk
therefore fails closed unless the owner opens and trusts the household HTTPS
controller. A future signed native companion should replace manual browser
certificate trust in the consumer release.

\subsection{Verification and Remaining Production Boundary}

The implementation is covered by 301 automated Fabric tests, including stateful
cancellation across five subsystems, overnight quiet schedules, start-anchored
wake rejection and immediate phone-audio deletion. This does not constitute
real-room acoustic qualification. Full-duplex barge-in, platform-native echo
cancellation, household preference profiles and accent, distance and
accessibility matrices remain open until measured on supported hardware.
