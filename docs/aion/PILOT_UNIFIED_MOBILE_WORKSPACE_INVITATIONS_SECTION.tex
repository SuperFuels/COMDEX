\section{Governed Workspace Invitations}
\label{sec:pilot-mobile-workspace-invitations}
\raggedright

A visible or discoverable business workspace does not grant access.  Pilot now creates a
separate governed invitation containing the organization, inviter, intended recipient,
role, requested scopes, operating constraints, expiry, and explicit revocation route.
This is the only path by which the later phone-acceptance flow may propose a new
membership.

The inviter must hold an active membership with \texttt{workspace.invite}.  Every scope
offered to the recipient must also be held by the inviter; possession of invitation
authority alone cannot manufacture Finance, People, file, approval, or administrative
access.  Constraints are limited to an allowlist covering departments, regions, project
references, approval limits, working hours, and bounded explanatory notes.  Unknown or
oversized constraints fail closed.

Invitations expire between five minutes and 30 days, begin in the pending state, and
include an opaque revocation route.  The original inviter may revoke a pending invitation;
another administrator requires the separate \texttt{workspace.invite.revoke} scope in
the same workspace.  Cross-workspace revocation is denied.  Revocation is idempotent and
cannot be used to rewrite an accepted or otherwise terminal invitation.

Creation is protected by a content-bound idempotency key.  A transport retry returns the
same invitation, while reuse of that key with a different person, role, scope, constraint,
or lifetime is rejected.  No membership is created at invitation time, and neither
discovery nor an upstream provider response can bypass private recipient acceptance.

Automated tests prove exact metadata, constraint and expiry construction, replay-safe
creation, explicit revocation, denial of delegated privilege expansion, and rejection of
changed-scope replay.  This closes \texttt{MOB-0603}.  The next task presents this exact
invitation privately on the intended phone and records its acceptance or refusal.
