\section{Signed Mobile Workspace Manifests}
\label{sec:pilot-mobile-signed-workspace-manifest}

Pilot does not construct its Workspace or Boardroom interface from an unrestricted API
response.  The mother brain now publishes a short-lived, cryptographically signed
manifest describing the exact business surfaces and actions that one paired phone may
present for one person, membership, lease, and workspace.

\subsection{Manifest contents}

The manifest contains canonical references for departments, agents, dashboards, files,
packages, and decisions.  Each entry contains only a bounded identifier and display
label; the business record remains in its authoritative provider.  The envelope also
binds the provider identifier, membership identifier, person, mobile surface, workspace,
capability lease, redaction declarations, issue time, and expiry time.

Permitted actions are the intersection of two independently controlled declarations:
the active membership scopes and the actions advertised by the workspace provider.
Consequently a permissive provider cannot expand a restricted membership, while a broad
membership cannot manufacture an action that the provider did not declare.  Empty,
excessive, unknown, or out-of-membership capability sets are rejected.

\subsection{Signing and validation}

The mother serializes the complete manifest envelope as deterministic canonical JSON,
calculates its SHA-256 payload hash, and signs the same bytes with its Ed25519 device
identity.  The receiving surface verifies the hash, signature, schema version, expected
person, expected phone surface, and expiry before rendering it.  Altering even a label or
file reference invalidates the signature.  A manifest lives for between 30 seconds and
15 minutes and therefore cannot act as permanent authority.

The signed manifest is a presentation and routing boundary, not a provider token and not
a replacement for the underlying capability lease.  Every subsequent read or action
must still pass the live membership and lease checks.  Provider credentials and raw
Boardroom records never enter the manifest.

\subsection{Verification}

Automated tests issue and verify a signed manifest, inspect its bounded catalogue,
detect post-signature tampering, reject capabilities outside the membership, and reject
an excessive lifetime.  The existing gateway tests continue to prove wrong-person,
expired-membership, scope, surface, and cross-client isolation.  This closes
\texttt{MOB-0602}; the next build stage adds governed invitations rather than silently
creating membership.
