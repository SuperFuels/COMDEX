\section{Continuation Record: Franka Cube-Lift Attempts}
\label{sec:cube-lift-continuation-20260808}

\subsection{Purpose and exact claim boundary}

This section records the complete continuation state of the NVIDIA manipulation
programme as of 8 August 2026.  Its purpose is operational as well as
scientific: a future development cycle should be able to resume from this
point without reconstructing the controller history, repeating rejected
experiments, or confusing contact evidence with a completed lift.

The current direct precision controller has \emph{not} completed the strict
cube-lift task.  The independent success authority is a post-action cube
height strictly greater than $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$.  The final fresh-world trial
scored 0/1, and no controller from this continuation cycle was promoted.  The
programme nevertheless established four narrower physical capabilities:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item the Franka can be brought safely into the correct object region by a
        bounded direct controller;
  \item table and self-contact can be excluded from grasp evidence;
  \item genuine object-only opposed fingertip contact can be produced; and
  \item one sealed trajectory produced a genuine $7.9\,\mathrm{mm}$ physical
        cube micro-lift.
\end{enumerate}

These are enabling results, not substitutes for the required lift.  A
historical protected controller also retains two genuine PhysX lifts in twelve
trials, which proves that the robot, environment, and lift authority admit the
task.  It does not prove that the newer direct controller is reliable.

All paid experiments described here used NVIDIA Isaac Lab and PhysX on an
NVIDIA L40S.  NVIDIA Cosmos was not yet used to train or evaluate the grasp
controller.  Cosmos-based representation or world-model training remains a
later stage, to begin only after a reproducible cube-lift baseline exists.

\subsection{Runtime authority and success contract}

The deployed proposal policy was deliberately denied privileged simulator
state.  At runtime it could use RGB, bounded robot proprioception, the episode
step, and object-filtered fingertip touch.  It could not use the true cube
position, reward, teacher action, private contact geometry, or the evaluator's
success bit.  True post-action cube position and policy mode were recorded
privately for diagnosis after execution; they were never returned to the
policy as input.

The causal task contract was
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{safe approach}
&\rightarrow \text{object-only opposed contact}
\rightarrow \text{secure closure}\\
&\rightarrow \text{attachment evidence}
\rightarrow \text{verification lift}\\
&\rightarrow h_{\mathrm{cube}}>0.10\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:direct-franka-lift-contract}
\end{equation}

The controller was required to fail closed.  One-finger touch, table contact,
high simulator reward, commanded upward hand motion, or an unverified grasp
could not be counted as a lift.  This prevented the system from learning from
false-positive contact or promoting a controller merely because the hand moved
upward while the cube remained on the table.

\subsection{Why the evaluation design was changed}

Early matched searches revealed a simulator-authority problem.  A retained
$15\,\mathrm{mm}$ search controller changed from 2/6 to 1/6 under the same
nominal seed, image, GPU, environment, and frozen policy.  A deterministic
audit seeded Python, NumPy, Torch, CUDA, and Warp; requested deterministic RTX;
enabled enhanced PhysX determinism; and compared initial observations plus
complete observation, action, and outcome chains.  The first reset image was
reconstructed exactly, but later reset images and causal chains diverged.

The likely boundary was sequential world reuse: later arms or episodes
inherited solver, rendering, or contact history despite reset calls.  Small
1/12 versus 2/12 comparisons were therefore too noisy to guide paid
development.  Subsequent work used one bounded fresh-world episode at a time
and treated a nominal seed as insufficient proof of identical late-contact
conditions.  Future tournaments must use either a newly reconstructed world
for every arm and episode or a precommitted replicated statistical design with
confidence bounds.

The object-filtered contact run failed the deliberately strict attachment

default promoted controller retained its stricter sealed threshold.  A fresh
repeat did not reacquire contact, so the relaxed probe was not credited with a
full lift.

\subsection{Final attempt and terminal state}

The last authorized paid attempt used a 350-step fresh world with symmetric
tactile bracketing.  If the first lateral correction failed, a second
correction was prepared to test the opposite side of the original approach
rather than continuing farther in the same direction.  Verified grasp and
lift phases were also assigned to the stable Cartesian controller.

The sealed outcome was:
\begin{align*}
N_{\mathrm{lift}} &= 0/1, &
h_{\mathrm{start}} &= 0.052057\,\mathrm{m},\\
h_{\mathrm{max}} &= 0.052057\,\mathrm{m}, &
\Delta h_{\mathrm{cube}} &= 0,\\
N_{\mathrm{dual}} &= 0, &
N_{\mathrm{one\mbox{-}sided}} &= 3,\\
N_{\mathrm{unsafe}} &= 0. &&
\end{align*}

The controller observed persistent one-sided contact, issued one causal reopen
and re-centre action, and then failed to regain contact.  The second side of
the bracket was never reached because persistent tactile evidence did not
recur.  The fingers never closed, no attachment signature was earned, and no
lift command was authorized.  This is a safe and correctly rejected episode.

All 33 focused controller, tactile, and evaluator-contract tests passed before
the final run.  The trajectory archive contains 350 RGB frames, bounded
proprioception, actions, fingertip touch, rewards for audit only, true
post-action cube position for private evaluation, and the per-step policy
mode.  Both NVIDIA workspaces were stopped after evidence retrieval.  No
additional paid retry was launched, no new controller was promoted, and no Git
commit was made for the experimental working state.

\subsection{Current diagnosis}

The principal blocker is \textbf{repeatable acquisition of a balanced
two-finger grasp in a fresh world}.  It is no longer accurate to describe the
problem as simply ``the arm cannot reach the cube'' or ``the lift command is
missing.''  The arm has reached the object, generated opposed contact, closed
around it, and physically begun lifting it.  What remains unreliable is the
last-centimetre sequence that converts approach into the same balanced contact
state on demand.

Three effects must not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{world variance:} nominally identical resets can diverge after
        contact history accumulates;
  \item \textbf{residual Cartesian error and compliance:} the commanded target
        is not necessarily the measured loaded hand pose; and
  \item \textbf{discrete tactile correction:} a one-sided signal can disappear
        after a large re-centering move before the controller gathers enough
        evidence to test the opposite side.
\end{enumerate}

The upward Cartesian path is prepared but remains weakly tested because grasp
acquisition rarely authorizes it.  The immediate research problem is therefore
to make attachment entry repeatable, then test whether attachment survives a
small vertical displacement before attempting the full task lift.

\subsection{Required restart protocol}
\label{subsec:cube-lift-restart-protocol}

When this work resumes, the following order should be preserved.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Begin from the retained evidence, not a broad search.}
        Reanalyse the 19-frame object-filtered opposed-contact window and the
        $7.9\,\mathrm{mm}$ micro-lift.  Record measured hand pose, finger width,
        left/right force, velocity, and policy phase at entry, peak balance,
        closure, and separation.

  \item \textbf{Reconstruct a fresh simulator world for every episode.}
        Record the initial RGB and bounded-observation hashes.  Never compare
        arms sequentially in one reused world and call the result matched.

  \item \textbf{Replace the large discrete re-centering jump with a bounded
        local contact search.}  Around the final measured contact pose, test a
        precommitted symmetric set of millimetre-scale lateral offsets.  Each
        move must settle before new touch evidence is interpreted.  The search
        must use fingertip touch and proprioception only; true cube pose remains
        evaluator-owned.

  \item \textbf{Separate acquisition from attachment qualification.}
        First require repeatable opposed object-only contact and a realistic
        finger-width band.  Then close gradually while the tactile balance
        servo minimizes left/right force asymmetry.  Log why each candidate
        window fails the signature rather than changing several thresholds at
        once.

  \item \textbf{Use a $3$--$4\,\mathrm{cm}$ verification lift.}
        Once attachment is earned, command a slow Cartesian rise while holding
        closure.  Attachment is verified only if the cube follows the hand.
        If it slips, capture the exact force, width, hand acceleration, and
        object-height frame at separation.

  \item \textbf{Attempt the full lift only after verification.}
        Continue beyond the independent $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ cube-height
        threshold.  Simulator reward must remain diagnostic and cannot replace
        this authority.

  \item \textbf{Demand replication before promotion.}
        A single lift is a development breakthrough, not a reliable skill.
        The initial reliability target should be at least 4/6 safe lifts in
        independently reconstructed worlds, followed by held-out seeds.
\end{enumerate}

Each paid cycle should change one causal mechanism, run the smallest experiment
that can falsify it, retrieve immutable evidence, and stop both GPUs.  If a
trial does not reach object-only contact, attachment thresholds are irrelevant;
if it reaches balanced attachment but slips during the verification lift, the
next change belongs to closure, friction, wrist stability, or acceleration---
not approach geometry.

\subsection{Path from one cube to general grasp competence}

The present controller is object-generic only in a limited architectural
sense: contact filtering binds to the active object prim rather than a
cube-specific collision path, and the runtime contract does not expose the
cube pose.  Empirical competence has been demonstrated only on the cube.

After the cube gate is repeatable, the curriculum should expand one factor at a
time across object width, height, aspect ratio, mass, friction, orientation,
occlusion, and initial lateral offset.  Training should distinguish at least
four reusable skills:
\begin{equation}
\text{visual grounding},\quad
\text{pre-grasp placement},\quad
\text{tactile acquisition},\quad
\text{load-preserving transport}.
\end{equation}

Only after transfer succeeds across held-out objects should NVIDIA Cosmos be
introduced for predictive visual or world-model learning.  Cosmos should be
evaluated as a challenger representation under the same physical authority;
it must not receive private cube state or convert imagined success into
competence credit.

\subsection{Handover verdict}

The cube-lift task remains open.  The programme progressed from unstable
approach and false contact to object-filtered opposed contact, a genuine
$7.9\,\mathrm{mm}$ micro-lift, stable 19-frame grasp geometry, and a fail-closed
Cartesian lift architecture.  It did not convert those advances into a fresh
post-action cube height above $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ before the bounded development
cycle ended.

The shortest credible route on resumption is not another broad policy search.
It is a fresh-world, millimetre-scale acquisition experiment around the proven
opposed-contact pose, followed by a slow $3$--$4\,\mathrm{cm}$ attachment test
and then the full lift.  Until that sequence is replicated, 
\AION{} should be described as having learned important components of grasping
without yet mastering reliable cube pickup.
