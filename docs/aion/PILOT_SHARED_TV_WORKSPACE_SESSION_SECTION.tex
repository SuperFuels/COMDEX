\section{Persona-Bound Shared Television Workspaces}
\label{sec:pilot-shared-tv-workspace}

Pilot Fabric 0.48 converts the television from a permanently shared household
surface into a temporarily acquired personal workspace.  A person's trusted
phone acts as the possession factor.  The television never stores the phone's
private key, raw private memory, service credentials, document contents, or
payment information.

\subsection{Workspace Acquisition}

The user selects a Pilot identity on the private phone and signs a one-time
Ed25519 acquisition payload containing the persona identifier, trusted-device
identifier, purpose, and nonce.  The mother brain verifies the signature
against the previously enrolled public key and rejects reused nonces.  A
successful acquisition creates one exclusive shared-screen lease containing:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the active persona and trusted-device identifiers;
  \item the display name permitted on the shared screen;
  \item activation and last-activity timestamps;
  \item a five-minute inactivity deadline; and
  \item an explicit prohibition on displaying private details by default.
\end{itemize}

Only one live lease may exist for a television workspace.  A second household
member cannot silently replace the current user.  They must wait for expiry or
for the current user to release the screen.  Once free, the next trusted phone
may acquire it with its own signed payload.

\subsection{Inactivity Lock and Explicit Release}

Accepted television speech and private-controller interactions renew the
lease for five minutes.  Passive video playback, ambient speech, page polling,
and mere proximity do not constitute authority.  When the deadline expires,
the mother brain returns a locked snapshot and withholds persona-specific
workspace data.

The user may say ``Pilot, log me out of the TV'' or press the phone control
labelled ``Log this person out of the TV''.  Phone release uses a separately
signed Ed25519 payload.  Release removes only the shared-screen lease; it does
not delete the identity, tasks, messages, memories, consents, or device key.
Lost-device revocation and account recovery also invalidate relevant active
sessions.

\subsection{Locked and Acquired Home Surfaces}

The locked television retains public household experiences such as Games, AI
TV, AI Learning, AI Shopping, and God View.  It displays no personal task
titles, calendar entries, files, messages, or Boardroom material.  It instead
instructs the user to acquire the screen from a trusted phone.

After acquisition, the home surface clearly names the active person and adds
five principal workspace tiles:

\begin{description}
  \item[Tasks] Persona-bound lists, reminders, delegated requests, and safe
  aggregate counts.
  \item[Calendar] The active person's schedule and separately approved event
  proposals.
  \item[Business] The AION Boardroom and business workspace, subject to an
  additional business-container login and its own authorization policy.
  \item[Files] Persona-authorized documents and saved assets; discovery of a
  filename does not imply permission to open its contents.
  \item[IoT] Household device discovery, connection state, permissions, rooms,
  and governed controls.
\end{description}

The television shows only bounded summaries until the user deliberately opens
a tile.  Business authentication, provider OAuth, document authority, and IoT
control leases remain separate capabilities; acquiring the screen cannot
silently grant them.

\subsection{Enforced Privacy Boundary}

The phone interface previously allowed a persona name to be selected before
performing an inbox operation.  Version 0.48 makes selection insufficient:
the selected persona must also hold the current signed television lease.
Private memory inspection, correction, export, deletion, service-consent
changes, task creation, task completion, reminders, and agent messages fail
closed when the requesting persona is not active.  When no lease exists, the
companion snapshot no longer defaults to the first household identity's
private export.

Shared-screen task enquiries disclose counts rather than private titles.  A
spoken request such as ``Pilot, show my task list'' may state the number of open
or awaiting tasks, while the detailed list remains on the trusted phone unless
the product later provides a deliberate, short-lived reveal control.

\subsection{Current Boundary and Next Work}

The exclusive lease, timeout, signed release, privacy gates, home tiles, and
voice logout are locally operational.  The Calendar, Business, Files, and IoT
tiles are capability entry points rather than claims that every external
provider is connected.  Production completion still requires native phone
biometric attestation, per-tile authorization screens, real Boardroom session
authentication, authorized file-provider adapters, calendar OAuth, and
multi-television field qualification.
