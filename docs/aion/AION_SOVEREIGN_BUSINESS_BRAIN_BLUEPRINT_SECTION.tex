\section{AION Sovereign Business Brain Blueprint}
\label{sec:aion-sovereign-business-brain}

\subsection{Product decision}

Tessaris shall not depend upon ownership of a single foundation model.  The
product is a customer-controlled AION brain which is useful before a premium
model is connected and becomes progressively more valuable as it learns the
customer's organisation.  The first installation is therefore an
\emph{empty governed brain}, not an empty chatbot.  It already provides
identity, memory, learning, planning, policy, permissions, capability control,
evidence, approvals, and audit machinery.  It does not initially contain the
customer's private institutional context.  That context is earned through
explicit onboarding, authorised connectors, observed work, corrections, and
verified outcomes.

Models, inference engines, agent harnesses, evidence providers, business
connectors, and compute backends are replaceable modules.  AION remains the
enduring system of record for what the customer knows, who may do what, which
routes work, and what happened.  The governing path is
\[
\begin{split}
\text{request} &\rightarrow \text{active person and space}
\rightarrow \text{business context} \\
&\rightarrow \text{policy and authority}
\rightarrow \text{capability plan}
\rightarrow \text{intelligence route} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Pilot presentation or execution}
\rightarrow \text{verification}
\rightarrow \text{receipt}
\rightarrow \text{bounded learning}.
\end{split}
\]

\subsection{Layered architecture}

The experience layer contains Personal Pilot, bounded professional Workspaces,
Boardroom, and the television, mobile, desktop, vehicle, and device surfaces.
These surfaces do not become independent sources of authority.  They project
the active identity and permitted capabilities of a customer-owned brain.

The brain layer owns people, organisations, roles, locations, goals, policies,
decisions, processes, terminology, canonical entities, memory boundaries,
capabilities, tasks, plans, evidence, outcomes, receipts, and measured route
performance.  A separate provider-neutral router selects deterministic local
functions, AION knowledge, local models, public evidence, customer-configured
cloud models, specialist providers, or optional frontier providers according
to sensitivity, residency, authority, quality, latency, context, cost, energy,
hardware, and measured performance.  Failure of a cloud provider must not
remove core intelligence.

The execution layer exposes versioned adapters for local runtimes, private
inference endpoints, customer-authorised cloud providers, NVIDIA infrastructure,
retrieval and evidence services, business systems, communications, files, and
device capabilities.  An adapter may not own canonical identity, memory,
policy, or the business map.

Four deployment profiles use the same logical brain: a personal computer or
home server; a dedicated small-business appliance; infrastructure within the
customer's chosen private-cloud account; and an enterprise VPC, on-premise,
air-gapped, or approved confidential-compute environment.  A signed export must
move the brain between these profiles without changing its logical identity or
copying unapproved provider secrets.

\subsection{The permission-aware business map}

The business map is a versioned graph rather than a folder of embeddings.  Its
ontology covers organisations, legal entities, brands, business units,
departments, teams, locations, people, roles, delegations, approval limits,
customers, suppliers, products, services, assets, systems, objectives, metrics,
risks, obligations, processes, tasks, documents, claims, evidence,
capabilities, models, providers, compute targets, and execution receipts.

Every relationship records its source, confidence, visibility scope, owner,
observation time, and review state.  Inference is visibly distinct from an
authorised record.  A model cannot convert a hypothesis into organisational
truth without sufficient evidence or the relevant human authority.  Customers
must be able to inspect what AION knows, how it knows it, what remains uncertain,
and who may change it.

Onboarding creates the organisation boundary and possession-bound owner,
conducts a short adaptive interview, produces a proposed map and an explicit
unknowns and conflicts queue, connects selected sources read-only first, and
observes ordinary work before proposing automation.  Repeated corrections and
verified outcomes may promote a procedure into a governed reusable capability;
observation alone may not silently expand authority.

\subsection{Learning without unsafe continuous retraining}

AION separates four learning loops.  Memory learning retains facts,
relationships, decisions, preferences, and corrections.  Operational learning
measures procedures, tools, recovery routes, and failure modes.  Routing
learning selects models, tools, and compute according to task-specific evidence.
Model adaptation performs optional fine-tuning or distillation only after
sufficient licensed evidence and formal evaluation.  The first three loops do
not modify model weights.  No live conversation may trigger unreviewed training,
and private information may enter a training corpus only under an explicit and
revocable data-use policy.

\subsection{Independent model catalogue}

AION shall maintain signed manifests containing the exact model version,
immutable hashes, trusted mirrors, source, licence, attribution, commercial-use
terms, supported modalities and languages, context behavior, hardware and
inference compatibility, quantisation, task and safety evaluations, tool-use
reliability, latency, cost, energy, provenance, vulnerability notices, permitted
data classifications, and last qualification date.  Model hubs, hardware
vendors, and model developers are sources rather than roots of trust.  A
customer may pin qualified versions and retain licence-permitted packages.

\subsection{Build programme and closure gates}

\paragraph{Phase 0: sovereign boundary.}
Freeze provider-independent identity, memory, map, policy, capability, and
receipt contracts; inventory existing COMDEX components; prohibit adapters from
granting authority or writing canonical truth; define brain export and routing
receipts; and prove that provider removal preserves customer state.  Closure
requires a no-cloud boot and a successful provider-removal restart exercise.

\paragraph{Phase 1: downloadable empty brain.}
Provide signed Mac, Windows, and Linux/server installation; hardware profiling;
an appropriate local runtime; possession-bound owner identity; encrypted vault;
empty personal, workspace, and organisation stores; guided deployment choices;
automatic health recovery; signed rollback; encrypted backup; restore; and
complete export.  Closure requires a non-technical clean installation with no
paid API and a verified restoration of the same logical brain.

\paragraph{Phase 2: business-map bootstrap.}
Implement the canonical ontology, adaptive interview, proposed-map review,
confidence and provenance, unknown and contradiction queues, source-preserving
document ingestion, initial read-only connectors, scoped graph queries,
retention, legal hold, and source revocation.  Closure requires reconstructing
a representative company while preventing a restricted user from enumerating
inaccessible graph regions.

\paragraph{Phase 3: unified intelligence contract.}
Define one request, structured response, and streaming contract across
deterministic capabilities, models, retrieval, evidence, and agent harnesses.
Require declared disclosure and residency; enforce cost, latency, energy,
quality, and context budgets; issue privacy-preserving route receipts; and add
health-aware fallback which cannot cross privacy or cost boundaries.  Closure
requires equivalent execution through two local models and one optional remote
provider with an inspectable selection reason.

\paragraph{Phase 4: catalogue and evaluation fabric.}
Implement signed model manifests, compatibility checks, domain, safety,
tool-use, latency, cost, and energy benchmarks, separated public and private
scores, champion--challenger routing, bounded canaries, rollback, drift
detection, and licence-aware mirrors.  A candidate cannot become default until
it passes customer-defined acceptance thresholds.

\paragraph{Phase 5: compute plug-ins.}
Qualify local inference, private OpenAI-compatible endpoints, replaceable NVIDIA
NIM and NeMo adapters, private-cloud and VPC recipes, Kubernetes quotas and
observability, optional confidential-compute attestation, and CPU, Apple
Silicon, NVIDIA, and later accelerator profiles.  Prove air-gapped,
no-paid-AI, and premium-enabled operation.  The brain's identity, map, policy,
and application contracts must survive migration between compute targets.

\paragraph{Phase 6: harness and capability packages.}
Define a signed package manifest for tools, scopes, tests, and limits; sandbox
undeclared access; require dry-run, adversarial permission, and outcome tests;
version and roll back prompts, procedures, tools, and policy independently;
and support curated department and industry packs.  Packages must be
inspectable, constrainable, revocable, and removable without corrupting memory.

\paragraph{Phase 7: medium-business Boardroom.}
Extend the map across legal entities, locations, business units, and regions;
connect Boardroom departments and agents; integrate enterprise identity and
separation of duties; add organisation-specific workflows, approval limits,
portfolio dashboards, professional workspaces, residency rules, and customer-
readable audit and export.  A multi-unit trial must enforce every user's map
and work boundary across mobile and desktop.

\paragraph{Phase 8: governed improvement.}
Record corrections and verified outcomes independently from chat history;
learn procedures and routes by task class; expire stale evidence; propose
automation only after repeated success and explicit approval; and permit model
adaptation only through licensed datasets, held-out tests, reversible releases,
and retention checks.  Customers must see what changed, why, and how to reverse
it.

\paragraph{Phase 9: commercial assurance.}
Add licensing for free Pilot, Boardroom, and Sovereign Fabric editions; meter
optional compute without monetising private content; provide customer-controlled
telemetry; complete security, privacy, supply-chain, regulatory, incident,
recovery, and support preparation; and run small- and medium-business pilots.
Commercial operation must not require centralised customer brains, a token,
blockchain, or a premium model.

\subsection{Relationship to existing Tessaris systems}

The unified mobile checklist remains authoritative for the Personal, Workspace,
Boardroom, and communication experience.  Boardroom is the first rich business
application over the brain.  Pilot Fabric supplies governed device and surface
execution.  GlyphNet supplies private person-to-person and agent-to-agent
communication.  GlyphChain may later anchor selective non-identifying proofs,
but is not required for intelligence routing.  PHO, wallets, and speculative
economic functions remain hidden until separately governed and reviewed.

\subsection{Commercial packaging and immediate order}

Pilot Free supplies a customer-owned personal brain and local core capabilities.
Boardroom supplies paid business agents, connectors, workflows, evaluations,
and approvals.  Tessaris Sovereign Fabric supplies customer-controlled private
deployment, enterprise governance, model and compute administration, support,
and service levels.  Premium models, managed compute, specialist capabilities,
and verified transactions remain optional consumption products.

Implementation begins by closing the sovereign boundary, building the clean
empty-brain installer and export format, implementing the business-map core,
consolidating current routers behind the intelligence contract, introducing
signed model manifests and customer-specific evaluations, qualifying local,
private, and NVIDIA-backed compute as interchangeable targets, and connecting
the resulting brain to Personal, Workspace, and Boardroom.  The decisive proof
is not the number of models AION can call.  It is that a customer can remove
every provider, preserve the brain, connect a replacement, and continue to
operate.
