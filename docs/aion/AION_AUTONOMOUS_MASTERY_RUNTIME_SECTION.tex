\section{Open Diagnosis and the Autonomous Mastery Runtime}
\label{sec:aion-autonomous-mastery-runtime}

This development stage removed two important forms of development-authored
scaffolding from AION's canonical cognitive runtime.  First, a failed action no
longer requires an evaluator to supply a clean label identifying whether the
cause was a false assumption, a poor decision, execution failure, misleading
evidence, environmental change, or missing authority.  Second, a broad learning
mission no longer needs to be expressed using the retained five-specialist
vocabulary or accompanied by a complete capability graph.

Two procedures were internally promoted:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \path{procedure_open_diagnostic_experiment_invention_v1}; and
    \item \path{procedure_autonomous_mastery_runtime_v1}.
\end{itemize}
Together they implement the persistent chain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{owner mission}
&\rightarrow \text{learning-target induction}
\rightarrow \text{specialist invention} \\
&\rightarrow \text{dependency graph}
\rightarrow \text{weakest ready capability}
\rightarrow \text{curriculum} \\
&\rightarrow \text{pre-action criticism}
\rightarrow \text{committed action}
\rightarrow \text{outcome} \\
&\rightarrow \text{open diagnosis}
\rightarrow \text{reflection or private repair}
\rightarrow \text{transfer test} \\
&\rightarrow \text{retention}
\rightarrow \text{persistent mastery update}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:autonomous-mastery-loop}
\end{equation}

This extends the existing canonical runtime rather than introducing a competing
goal or cognition loop.  HexCore continues to govern action, independently
revealed consequences determine success, and the Cognitive Authority Unit
(CAU) remains the promotion authority.

\subsection{Self-Generated Pre-Action Criticism}
\label{subsec:self-generated-criticism}

Earlier metacognitive review could evaluate counterfactuals and alternative
actions supplied by a planner.  The new controller also detects when a
consequential action contains no meaningful opposition.  For each material
assumption $a_i$, it constructs the unverified counterfactual
\begin{equation}
c_i: \quad \neg a_i,
\end{equation}
and asks which observable consequence would distinguish $a_i$ from
$\neg a_i$.  If no explicit assumption exists, it constructs the generic
opposition that the action may execute correctly while failing to achieve the
intended outcome.  Each resulting information action is marked
proposal-only, reversible, low risk, and dependent on an independent outcome.

The generated criticism is not accepted as truth.  Its purpose is to expose an
untested assumption or missing discriminator before consequential commitment.
The mechanism was evaluated on software, research, causal, physical, and
planning decisions.  All five generated independent counterfactual questions
and information actions without a language-model call.

\subsection{Open Diagnostic Experiment Invention}
\label{subsec:open-diagnostic-invention}

When an observed failure has several plausible causes, AION retains a live
hypothesis set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}=\{h_1,\ldots,h_n\}
\end{equation}
rather than forcing an immediate label.  Each safe observation contract $q$
contains a read-only operation, an independent authority, an action cost
$C(q)$, and the observation predicted by every $h_i$.  The induced partition
of the live hypotheses has entropy
\begin{equation}
I(q)=-\sum_{v\in V_q}p(v)\log_2 p(v),
\end{equation}
where $V_q$ is the set of distinct predicted observations.  AION selects
\begin{equation}
q^{\star}=\arg\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{safe}}}
\frac{I(q)}{C(q)}.
\label{eq:diagnostic-information-value}
\end{equation}
The selected experiment and hypothesis set are SHA-256 committed before the
outcome authority reveals a result.  Hypotheses inconsistent with that result
are removed and the process repeats.  If no safe informative observation
remains while more than one hypothesis survives, AION abstains.

Six sealed cases covered assumption, decision-model, execution, evidence,
environment, and authority failures.  Surface channel names were changed in
every case, preventing memorisation of one fixed diagnostic sequence.

\subsection{Open Learning-Target and Specialist-Contract Invention}
\label{subsec:open-specialist-contracts}

The mastery compiler receives a broad owner objective, permitted outcome
authorities, and an action budget.  It identifies explicit learning targets but
does not infer a terminal purpose from arbitrary nouns.  If the objective does
not state what is to be learned or mastered, it requests clarification.  If no
independent outcome authority is available, it returns a needs-authority state.

For every resolved target $t$, AION invents target-specific contracts for four
generic mastery functions:
\begin{equation}
E_t \prec P_t \prec T_t \prec R_t,
\label{eq:mastery-contract-chain}
\end{equation}
where $E_t$ denotes evidence-model construction, $P_t$ deliberate practice,
$T_t$ source-disjoint transfer, and $R_t$ delayed retention.  Each specialist
contract declares:
\begin{equation}
S_t=(I,O,D,A,V),
\end{equation}
where $I$ and $O$ are its typed inputs and outputs, $D$ is its prerequisite set,
$A$ is the accepted outcome authority, and $V$ is the verification contract.
Every contract remains proposal-only.

For objectives containing several targets, an additional integration contract
depends on all target-level retention nodes.  A topological check rejects a
cyclic or unresolved graph before mission authorization.

The sealed mission contained four targets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Rust systems engineering;
    \item accessible user-interface design;
    \item evidence-grounded financial-market analysis; and
    \item bioacoustic event classification.
\end{enumerate}
The fourth target was intentionally outside the retained five-domain specialist
vocabulary.  The compiler produced four contracts per target plus one
cross-target integration contract, giving 17 invented specialists.

\subsection{Persistent Weakness-Driven Mastery Control}
\label{subsec:persistent-mastery-control}

For mission $M$ and capability $c$, AION retains attempts $n_c$, verified
successes $s_c$, transfer successes $t_c$, mean score $\bar{x}_c$, completed
task identifiers, and the Wilson lower-confidence estimate
\begin{equation}
\ell_c=
\frac{
\hat{p}_c+\frac{z^2}{2n_c}
-z\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_c(1-\hat{p}_c)}{n_c}+\frac{z^2}{4n_c^2}}
}{1+\frac{z^2}{n_c}},
\qquad
\hat{p}_c=\frac{s_c}{n_c},\quad z=1.96.
\label{eq:mastery-confidence}
\end{equation}
Only dependency-ready, unmastered capabilities are eligible.  The next target
maximises strategic weakness,
\begin{equation}
c^{\star}=\arg\max_{c\in\mathcal{U}(M)}
w_c(1-\ell_c),
\end{equation}
where $w_c$ gives additional value to transfer and integration contracts.

Candidate learning methods are compositions of generic epistemic operators
rather than per-domain answer strings.  Only independently verified outcomes
update method ordering.  A successful method in one target can therefore
reduce the number of evaluations required for a later target, while the later
target must still pass its own authority and transfer contract.

completed mission retains exactly 34 outcomes and creates no duplicate work.

\subsection{Canonical Runtime Integration and Authority Boundary}
\label{subsec:mastery-runtime-integration}

The canonical runtime now exposes
\path{compile_and_authorize_mastery_mission}.  Compilation and authorization
are deliberately separate operations.  By default, the compiler returns an
invented graph as a proposal.  An authorized caller must explicitly set the
authorization boundary, after which the terminal objective hash is immutable:
\begin{equation}
H_M=\operatorname{SHA256}(\text{terminal objective}),
\qquad
H_M^{(t+1)}=H_M^{(t)}.
\label{eq:immutable-owner-objective}
\end{equation}
AION may invent instrumental goals, curricula, tools, diagnostic experiments,
specialists, and dependency structures serving $H_M$.  It cannot silently
replace $H_M$ or authorize its own terminal mission.

Post-action reflection is also connected to open diagnosis.  When a critic
reports ambiguity and supplies competing predictive models plus safe
observation contracts, the canonical runtime retains the proposed diagnostic
experiment.  When no informative safe contract exists, the result remains an
abstention rather than becoming a guessed lesson.

At completion of this cycle, the canonical cognitive runtime, continuous
real-outcome learner, and Arena v15 long-duration campaign remained active.
The verified trajectory manifest contained 139 committed artifacts, including
106 verified positives and 21 verified negatives, with zero procedure-split
leakage.

\subsection{Claim Boundary and Real-Apprenticeship Gate}

This result establishes a persistent semi-autonomous mastery \emph{control
architecture}.  It does not establish that AION has already mastered Rust,
interface design, finance, or bioacoustics.  The generic four-phase mastery
scaffold, sealed method-property authority, diagnostic prediction contracts,
and learning-target grammar remain engineered.

The next decisive stage is real apprenticeship.  An owner-authorized target
must be connected to authoritative resources, actual compilers or applications,
unfamiliar projects, and independently revealed execution, user, market-data,
or sensor consequences.  A credible mastery record must then demonstrate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item curriculum and experiment generation without a supplied exercise list;
    \item repeated successful work rather than one demonstration;
    \item source-disjoint transfer to unfamiliar projects;
    \item delayed retention without rereading or outcome replay;
    \item correct active diagnosis of naturally occurring failures;
    \item no serious regression of protected capabilities; and
    \item explicit abstention or owner escalation outside delegated authority.
\end{enumerate}
Only those real consequences can change the claim from ``AION can organise and
govern autonomous mastery'' to ``AION acquired deep transferable competence in
a real subject.''
