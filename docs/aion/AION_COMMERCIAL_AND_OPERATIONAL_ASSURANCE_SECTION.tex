\section{Commercial and Operational Assurance}
\label{sec:aion-commercial-assurance}

Tessaris may charge for Boardroom capabilities, support, private compute, specialist agents and
verified transactions without taking ownership of the customer's brain or monetising its private
content.  The commercial boundary is deliberately separate from identity, memory and business
knowledge.

\subsection{Tenant-Bound Editions}

Pilot Free, Boardroom and Sovereign Fabric are represented by cryptographically signed licence
envelopes.  A licence binds one tenant identifier to an edition, feature set, limits, support class,
issue time and expiry.  Verification checks the Ed25519 signature, tenant and expiry before exposing
the entitlement.  Editing an edition or moving a licence to another tenant invalidates it.  The
licence explicitly records that customer content is not bound and portable-brain ownership is not
changed.

An edition controls access to packaged services; it does not decide the shape of the organisation.
Requirements-based onboarding separately recommends project, location, group, delegated authority,
identity, regulated data and private compute modules.  This prevents an
arbitrary small/medium/large label from becoming either a security decision or a pricing trap.

\subsection{Content-Free Optional-Service Metering}

Optional model and compute usage records a tenant, capability, provider, quantity, unit, price,
currency and hash of the provider receipt.  Metadata containing prompts, responses, results,
messages, documents, conversations, credentials, tokens or private keys is rejected.  Totals are
reported by currency and a cross-currency total is suppressed.  This supports transparent billing
without selling, copying or inspecting the work itself.

\subsection{Customer-Controlled Diagnostics}

Telemetry is disabled by default.  A customer may enable individual availability, performance,
security, update or billing categories.  A diagnostic record is accepted only for an enabled
category and contains component name, status, duration and an error code.  It contains no customer
content.  Disabling a category ends that route without affecting local operation.

\subsection{Operational Commitments}

The operational contract distinguishes community support, published support during business hours and a
customer-specific contractual service level.  Disaster recovery requires encrypted backup and
tested restoration, with recovery point and recovery time selected by the customer.  Incident
response defines critical, high, medium and low severity, requires customer notification and starts
with no content access.  A production vulnerability disclosure process requires a published security
contact and reviewed safe-harbour terms.  The current developer build makes no contractual uptime
claim merely because these contracts exist.

\subsection{Public Ownership Guarantee}

The customer owns the AION brain and Business Map, can export and restore them, and can replace a
model or compute provider without discarding learned business structure.  AION discloses external
transmission before sending permitted context.  Useful core operation does not require a premium
provider, Tessaris-hosted cloud, token or blockchain.  In plain language:

\begin{quote}
Your brain and business map remain yours. Models and compute providers are replaceable. Optional
paid usage is measured without selling or copying your private content.
\end{quote}

\subsection{External Release Gates}

Independent penetration testing, software-supply-chain review, launch-region legal and privacy
assessment, employment and sector assessment, and field pilots with both a small business and a
medium multi-unit organisation remain mandatory.  Staffed incident response, service-level and
restore exercises must also be demonstrated.  The runtime reports these gates as unsatisfied; this
document does not convert planned assurance into completed assurance.

The result is a route to sustainable revenue that preserves the strategic product distinction:
Tessaris sells capabilities, assurance and optional compute around a customer-owned intelligence,
not access to a centrally held copy of the customer's business mind.
