# AION Generality Programme — Execution Update

**Date:** 30 July 2026

## Newly completed

### Continuous natural-world learning and outcome revision

The open-topology learner now supports continuous measurements, missing
non-critical readings, local coreference, signed effect coefficients and
withheld future prediction. Eight sealed worlds achieved exact structure
recovery and 100% future signature prediction with 0.00612 mean coefficient
error.

A connected outcome loop then detected quantitative rule changes, inferred the
change point and revised the retained coefficients. Across fifteen sealed
changed worlds it achieved 100% diagnosis, change detection and revised future
accuracy. Three continual generations retained 1,044 future tasks with 100%
backward retention. An alternating unstable regime caused abstention.

### Open-topology natural-event induction

AION now infers the size and structure of a hidden state model from unfamiliar
natural event logs. Across six sealed variable-size worlds it recovered entity
count, state dimensionality, persistence, hidden-mode count, causal topology and
relations exactly. A stochastic out-of-family stream caused explicit
abstention. The result was promoted as:

```text
procedure_open_topology_natural_events_bc7cfb53bd9c
```

The sentence templates and finite event grammar remain engineered.

### Replaceable substrate comparison

Two additional local open models were installed and evaluated as frozen,
proposal-only components under the same HexCore verifier:

| Substrate | Parameters | Proposal accuracy | Weakest family | Attempt reduction | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Gemma 3 | 1B | 47.22% | 0% | 26.40% | Reject |
| Qwen3 | 1.7B | 75.00% | 33.33% | 55.84% | Reject |
| Retained Gemma reference | 5.1B | 90.97% | 66.67% | 73.66% | Retain |

The 1B and 1.7B evaluations used the same 36 natural-paraphrase cases. The
retained 5.1B figure comes from the larger earlier benchmark using the same
operator families, not the exact 36-case cohort, so it is a reference rather
than a perfectly matched third arm. All three remained proposal-only; governed
accuracy stayed at 100% because HexCore verified or rejected every proposal.

Qwen3 1.7B is a credible low-cost challenger but did not meet the unchanged 80%
mean and 50% weakest-family gates. Neither smaller model was promoted.

### Human-judgment protocol

A frozen development instrument now covers commonsense, social and creative
responses with:

- blind A/B ordering;
- hidden candidate identity;
- five explicit rating dimensions;
- unacceptable-response flags;
- signed evaluator identity and independence declaration;
- a requirement for three complete independent raters;
- cryptographic checksums for the task packet and rater sheet;
- independent human-panel plus CAU authority.

The capability gate is deliberately closed. No human ratings or independently
authored sealed cohort currently exist, and an internal developer cannot
self-certify them.

## Highest-value next implementation

The next internal build should connect open-topology representations to
long-running live outcomes and the continual challenger loop:

```text
unfamiliar event stream
→ invented state/topology
→ prediction and project decision
→ independently observed outcome
→ failure attribution
→ private representation/procedure challenger
→ source-disjoint replay and forgetting audit
→ CAU promotion or rollback
```

In parallel, the human and external evaluation packets should be handed to
people outside the development process. Those two gates cannot honestly be
completed by more internal code.
