\section{Cross-Domain Open Semantic Transfer}
\label{sec:cross-domain-semantic-transfer}

The successor to open literary relation memory tested whether retained semantic
operators transferred to unfamiliar technical, scientific, and historical
material.  The frozen sources were official Python documentation, NASA climate
questions, and the United States National Archives' constitutional questions.
The task supplied no domain ontology or relation vocabulary.  Four questions
per source were authored by the corresponding publisher rather than by the
benchmark implementation.

The governed pipeline was
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{official source}
&\rightarrow \text{open semantic proposals}
\rightarrow \text{span/hash verification} \\
&\rightarrow \text{canonical transfer audit}
\rightarrow \text{persistent memory} \\
&\rightarrow \text{restart}
\rightarrow \text{memory-only answer}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Language-model output remained proposal-only.  HexCore verified every retained
span, preserved the source checksum, distinguished reported statements from
verified truth, rejected admitted near-match ontology mappings, and retained
sole promotion authority through CAU.

The transferred operators included causal, containment, applicability, and
general association relations.  Reuse occurred in two unfamiliar domains.  No
historical predicate was forced into the retained ontology merely to satisfy a
metric.  One historical answer cited the correct memory but failed the declared
lexical-overlap threshold and remained counted as an error.

A bounded software-documentation bridge also used retained Python evidence to
explain why a proposed repair requires executable tests: an interface
description cannot establish that an implementation satisfies its behavioural
contract.  This output did not authorize a patch; hidden execution, adversarial
security checks, and CAU remained authoritative.

The procedure
\texttt{procedure\_cross\_domain\_semantic\_transfer\_58eae51b7d20}
was promoted and survived process reconstruction with zero relearning.  Fourteen
focused semantic-memory and repair tests passed.  Public questions and ingested
answers do not constitute contamination-proof independent evaluation.  The
next gate requires independently authored delayed questions and documentation-
guided, menu-free multi-file repair on hidden repositories under a matched
no-memory control.
