# AION Experience-Compiled Project Intelligence

## Result

`procedure_experience_compiled_project_intelligence_v1` replaces one retained
executor per project family with a compact compiler learned from AION's actual
consequence-confirmed project history.

The source ledger contained 267 projects.  A chronological 60/40 boundary
placed 160 earlier projects in development and 107 later projects in the
sealed cohort.  Development family labels were not retained.  Instead, AION
compressed observable project affordances and verified outcome authority into
six method prototypes.  Development artifacts were then closed.

For every sealed project AION received only the objective, success criterion
and artifact structure.  It selected an authority program, executed the
independent check and generated a property-level counterexample.  The original
family and authority label were revealed only after selection.

Relative to the first open-family result, which demonstrated five

\[

\]

This is a coverage multiplier, not a claim that general intelligence itself
improved by a precisely measurable factor.

## Retained object

The retained state contains no sealed project identifiers or answers.  It is a
small mapping from observable semantic and structural affordances to governed
authority programs:

\[
\text{objective + success contract + artifact shape}
\rightarrow
\text{method prototype}
\rightarrow
\text{compiled verification program}.
\]

The six learned programs cover later public technical change, fresh executable
discrimination, deterministic mathematical recomputation, immutable source-span
recovery, later environmental envelopes and multi-authority availability.

Situated projects use a composed program rather than a single verifier:

\[
\text{validate schema}
\rightarrow
\text{compare revisions}
\rightarrow
\text{separate origin}
\rightarrow
\text{rank information actions}
\rightarrow
\text{preserve provenance}
\rightarrow
\text{return competency evidence}.
\]

All four completed situated projects reconstructed and passed this composition.

## Architectural significance

Earlier phases retained answers, procedures, adapters or one invented project
family.  This phase retains a compiler capable of selecting and composing those
methods over a much larger project stream.  Consequently, adding verified
experience can expand coverage without requiring a new bespoke phase module for
every project instance.

The compiler is integrated into the persistent mastery service and reported by
the development dashboard.  It is proposal-only: independent authorities,
counterexamples, CAU and abstention retain final control.

## Boundary

The historical project stream is diverse in authority type but still strongly
structured by AION's existing useful-work generators.  Authority executors and
the observable feature vocabulary remain engineered.  The result establishes
experience compilation across a large chronological internal cohort; it does
not establish unrestricted project invention, general real-world mastery, AGA
completion or AGI.

The next gate is prospective use: newly invented objective families must be
compiled by this retained machinery before their outcomes exist, then succeed
against genuinely new authorities.  Repeated success there would show the
compiler is growing beyond the distribution that created it.

## First prospective tier-six closure

The autonomous difficulty ladder was extended from five to six. Tier six
cannot close from retrospective reconstruction: it requires five distinct
receipt roles, the fifth being a compiler selection committed while the
specialist project is still waiting for its outcome.

The first live project exposed and corrected two integrity defects before any
credit was awarded. Goal identity originally omitted difficulty, allowing a
higher tier to reuse a lower-tier identifier. A second defect allowed two goals
to attach to one specialist outcome. Goal identity now includes difficulty,
prospective commitments are restricted to the current active portfolio and
specialist project identifiers are one-to-one. The stale commitment remains in
the rejected audit ledger.

After those corrections, AION precommitted the multi-authority acquisition
program, observed the next public cycle and earned the first complete tier-six
receipt. At the resulting checkpoint:

## Learned-method executive

The final fixed-family dependency was removed from portfolio generation. When
the experience compiler is promoted, the executive no longer begins with the
five labels `research_investigation`, `software_tool`, `data_decision`,
`mathematical_reasoning` and `document_evidence`. It constructs useful-work
goals directly from retained method prototypes and binds completion to the
observable authority semantics of the later specialist project.

The live portfolio contains five learned-method contracts and zero fixed-family
contracts. Six retained methods rotate across generations according to the
consequence count. The first method-driven goal has precommitted
`fresh_subprocess_plus_later_public_row` while its specialist outcome remains
hidden.

Two additional audit failures were corrected during live rotation. Completed
prospective receipts are now archived rather than deleted when a goal leaves
the active portfolio, while inactive unresolved goals cannot recreate new
precommitments. The one receipt affected before correction was not regenerated
or re-awarded; its original digest was recovered as a reference from the
immutable autonomous-arbiter artifact.

The executive transition is therefore:

\[
\text{fixed task-family menu}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{retained method library}
\rightarrow
\text{authority-bound objective}
\rightarrow
\text{prospective compiled program}.
\]

The first learned-method goal subsequently completed all five tier-six receipt
roles. It selected `fresh_subprocess_plus_later_public_row`, committed that
program before the specialist result existed, and closed through the later
executable consequence. At this checkpoint the chronological sealed cohort has
grown to 110/110, exactly 22 times the original five-transfer cohort, and the
autonomous consequence ledger contains 30 confirmed goals.
