\section{Q2 Error Correction and Contention-Aware Expert Rotation}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-q2-correction-pairwise-rotation}

\subsection{Purpose and integrity boundary}

Two distinct attempts were evaluated after the stopped three-original-plus-Q2
bridge.  The first asked whether a tiny learned correction could repair the
remaining Q2 approximation error.  This was a separately labelled
quality-track compiler and did not qualify as exact inference.  The second
retained all four original MXFP4 experts, original gates, unrestricted router
and left-associated accumulation order, but scheduled two expert calculations
at a time before rotating to the second pair.  The second experiment therefore
remained eligible for the exact track if every output, hidden state and logit
matched bit for bit.

\subsection{Low-rank Q2 error-correction gate}

The compiler fitted a small low-rank correction to the difference between an
original fourth expert and its Q2 approximation.  Arithmetic captures were
used for fitting, extraction captures for selection and unseen reasoning
captures for development evaluation.  The resulting cartridge occupied only
293,931 bytes and its correction calculation required a median 0.0165~ms.
The size and speed objectives therefore passed comfortably.

Accuracy did not pass.  Selection p95 relative layer error was 0.13972 and
development p95 was 0.12538, versus the predeclared 0.02 ceiling.  No evaluated
row met the 0.02 threshold.  The decision was consequently
\texttt{STOP\_Q2\_ERROR\_CORRECTION}.  This establishes that a global low-rank
patch is too small to reconstruct the omitted expert's activation-dependent
nonlinear contribution, despite being cheap enough for the required runtime.

\subsection{Why unrestricted four-way swarming was replaced}

Prior four-way expert calculation had produced an attractive isolated
observation, from 0.448~s to 0.244~s, but sustained generation fell into the
1.77--2.27~tokens/s range.  All four workers were simultaneously reading large
weight arrays from the same unified-memory fabric.  Their calculations were
independent, but their data supply was not.  The experiment therefore tested a
two-lane rotation: experts zero and one execute concurrently, then experts two
and three; the four results are accumulated in the original order.

\subsection{Real-layer screening result}

The first screening used sixteen real layer-12 routes from each of reasoning,
arithmetic and extraction captures at a total ten-thread budget.  Across the
three families, all 192 paired candidate outputs were bitwise identical to the
serial calculation.  Median reported layer speedups were 1.9534, 2.6897 and
1.8479 respectively, and 14/16, 14/16 and 15/16 routes were faster.  This was a
valid narrow signal and authorised complete-transformer integration; it was
not generated-token evidence.

\subsection{Complete-model falsification}

The candidate was integrated behind a fixed five-pass \texttt{00110} pattern:
warm-up control, settled control, two pairwise candidates and a final control.
The first gate evaluated the 15-position arithmetic prompt plus one genuine
continuation position through all 36 attention/MoE layers and the vocabulary
head.  Every route, generated token, final hidden-state hash and step-logit hash
was identical across all five passes.  Exactness therefore passed.

The performance result failed decisively.  Aggregate native MoE calculation
time was 0.8005--1.0020~s on the serial passes, but 9.2549 and 15.3560~s on the
pairwise passes.  Total wall time and delivered-L2 bytes were also affected by
unequal SD fallbacks, so those wall figures are not used as a balanced speed
claim.  The native calculation counters alone are sufficient to reject the
candidate.

A second full-36-layer BOS gate replaced repeated graph construction with two
persistent reusable lane graphs.  It again remained completely bitwise exact.
Serial MoE time was 37.997--40.419~ms across the comparable early controls;
the two pairwise passes required 945.058 and 2218.189~ms.  Reusing the graphs
therefore did not cure the physical contention.  The fused serial graph is able
to keep the machine's optimized packed kernel supplied more efficiently than
two concurrent half-thread graphs.

\subsection{Decision and reusable lesson}

Decision: \texttt{STOP\_PAIRWISE\_EXPERT\_CALCULATION}.  The narrow
microbenchmark remains preserved as evidence that rotation can win under a
particular residency and page state, but it is not representative of the
complete transformer.  The full path demonstrates that multiple packed expert
calculations still fight for the same unified-memory bandwidth and incur
thread-pool overhead.

The surviving scheduling rule is asymmetric.  Required expert \emph{delivery}
may proceed concurrently, and a missing expert may be read while an already
resident expert is calculated.  Actual packed expert arithmetic remains on one
fused serial calculation path.  In warehouse language, several couriers may
prepare or fetch the next tools, but only one optimized workbench consumes the
shared machine-memory supply at a time.  This is the already promoted exact
I/O--compute overlap architecture, not a new tokens/s promotion.

\subsection{Corrected residency gate and selective-layer question}

A subsequent audit found that the initial isolated gate did not explicitly
warm both implementations before its measured ABBA sequence.  Because the
ordinary capture store returns fragmented Python-owned component buffers, its
first calculation can include lazy page faults.  The gate was corrected to run
one unmeasured serial and one unmeasured pairwise calculation for every route
before timing.  On eight layer-12 routes the corrected fragmented-buffer gate
remained exact and reported a median 5.3840-fold pairwise advantage.  This does
not reverse the full-model decision: it identifies the condition under which
rotation helps---unprepared or fragmented expert pages.

The optimized generation runtime does not use that representation.  Its
reusable route arena places expert components in bounded, prepared slots before
the fused calculation.  Per-layer inspection of the persistent full-model BOS
gate found that pairwise execution was faster on zero of 36 layers.  The best
pairwise layer achieved only 0.7245 times serial speed; the median layer ratio
was 0.02563.  Therefore a selective ``pairwise only on winning layers'' policy
has no admissible layers in the real optimized path and was not integrated.

This also defines the useful AtomSheet boundary.  Four-dimensional cells can
represent layer, expert, token and residency state, and can coordinate SD read,
decode, verification, arena placement and calculation.  They cannot create a
new physical memory channel.  The existing reusable arena is the concrete
memory-cell mechanism: it converts fragmented expert objects into prepared
calculation slots.  Once those slots are ready, a single fused calculation is
faster than concurrent expert calculations on this unified-memory host.
