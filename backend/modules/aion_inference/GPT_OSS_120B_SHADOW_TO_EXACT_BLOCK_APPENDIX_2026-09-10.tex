\section{From Governed Shadow Experts to Exact Multi-Token Block Execution}
\label{sec:shadow-to-exact-block-20260910}

\subsection{Scope, authorization, and unchanged claim boundary}

This section records the work performed after the frozen Shadow Expert
readiness corpus was established.  The user authorized local training of
derived numerical weights using model-generated captures that contain no
prompt text and no personal or customer data.  Promotion was divided into
staged traffic-reduction rungs: greater than 1.10$\times$ for an experimental
advance, at least 2$\times$ for a useful result, and at least 10$\times$ for a
breakthrough result.  Numerical accuracy, confidence calibration, exact SD
fallback, evidence hashing, and changed-arithmetic labelling were not relaxed.

The honest sustained unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B generation rate remains
approximately 0.103 tokens per second until an end-to-end candidate passes.
No layer microbenchmark in this section is reported as a generated-token rate.

\subsection{First authorized affine candidate}

The first trained candidate was deliberately minimal: a 2,860-byte per-layer
affine/polynomial cartridge.  It calculated in 0.0182~ms p50 and 0.0186~ms p95,
demonstrating that a compact replacement could have negligible arithmetic and
storage cost.  Accuracy failed decisively.  No layer passed calibration;
numerical and semantic holdout p95 relative $L_2$ errors were 20.02 and 21.41,
and projected traffic reduction remained 1.0$\times$.  Exact SD fallback was
therefore required for every layer.  The decision was
\texttt{STOP\_AFFINE\_BASELINE}.

The report canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{c7ff59cb0b96bdb3ae3eeea67d5536f5c08f251113a9c81b8dbf5aaee0c1ee32};
its identical local/SD file SHA-256 is
\texttt{e443ba15b37dbfd273c0801ccb43b3314339de7e161dcf5f4659e51a6423aea5}.
The isolated implementation commit is \texttt{85ebcc79}.  This result
falsified the simple affine shortcut but did not falsify higher-capacity or
route-conditioned alternatives.

\subsection{Nonlinear, residual-subspace, and route-aware investigation}

Four additional independent first-token families and two genuine eight-token
KV-linked trajectories were added to training while the calibration and two
holdout families remained frozen.  Both long trajectories repeated routes,
final hidden states, and logits bitwise.  The resulting manifest binds 288
verified targets, including 21 training states at every captured layer.  Its
canonical/file SHA-256 values are
\texttt{a2dfe446585f12466dba6decd210e195974759872410516e48f2240c960d5f3a}
and
\texttt{03b9384eafebd632bb7bb784454db2444918af8d28e1864d97b4736923e1a1e8}.

Nonlinear RBF and multikernel models reduced the worst holdout p95 error from
21.41 to 0.918, but no layer passed.  An impossible oracle projection, allowed
to select the best combination from the complete training-residual span using
the holdout target itself, still produced overall numerical and semantic p95
errors of 0.663 and 0.808.  Global residual dictionaries were therefore
falsified and stopped.  Layers 6 and 9 were narrow exceptions: their individual
oracle errors were approximately 0.015--0.017, proving that their residuals
could lie in the available span even though a safe coefficient predictor had
not yet been found.  Oracle canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{f997d884eb821774e63004735b761734dd2a49df3f63cdd000f18507e7a53b28}.

Adding the four unrestricted expert identities and gate strengths as an AION
route Glyph materially improved layer 6.  Calibration relative $L_2$ reached
0.0192; the two frozen unseen-family errors were 0.0282 and 0.0312.  Calculation
cost was 0.0850~ms p50 and 0.1070~ms p95.  The hard 0.05 ceiling was preserved,
but the 0.02 p95 gate was missed.  Replacing one of the 12 measured layers also
projected only 1.0909$\times$ traffic reduction, below the strict
$>1.10\times$ rung.  The decision was \texttt{STOP\_KERNEL\_BASELINE}, not
promotion.  Canonical/file SHA-256 values are
\texttt{9f1b12cf4ca4660d9031abd51b2f1124c2b362a16ac7cbc142f0293ac9d153fb}
and
\texttt{ef31a56cfac110d2fefabbdf1a8fe7e137e47cff26b8fa0a123e3238b37dcfa8}.

A route-coverage audit identified the blocker.  The median held-out route
shared only two of four experts with its closest training route; one of 24
holdout rows had zero overlap, and only eight of 24 contained four experts that
had all appeared somewhere in training.  Route-coverage canonical/file
SHA-256 values are
\texttt{6f0d2b505b88b3bcb6dff2012d9f253cabc115f1251775edc44233ccca38028c}
and
\texttt{89b00377375d69e6efbb384fc721fd6d8052ae35e11710eb4e9459727e5cd37a}.
The isolated near-signal commit is \texttt{4f08bce4}.  Random corpus expansion
and global residual dictionaries were stopped.

\subsection{Assessment of the hologram, GHX, and QQC material}

The earlier hologram material was re-examined against the physical 120B
bottleneck.  A GHX crystal is presently a compact symbolic graph and metadata
snapshot.  It can compress a reusable meaning or workflow motif, but it is not
a lossless compressor for dense, high-entropy MXFP4 neural weights.  The QFC is
primarily a visualization surface, while QQC supplies orchestration,
verification, rollback, and repair.  The documented ``Hologram CPU'' is a small
interpreter with synthetic beams rather than a replacement for the matrix
arithmetic performed by GPT-OSS 120B.

The useful hypothesis was therefore narrower: represent a layer route as a GHX
crystal whose nodes are experts and whose values contain gates and a normalized
router activation.  The crystal would recognize a route family, select a
resident calculator, expose coverage and margin, and leave final authorization
to a confidence gate with exact SD fallback.

\subsection{GHX route-crystal falsification and retained selector}

Because the earlier holdouts contributed to hypothesis formation, they were
treated as development-only for this experiment.  The configuration was frozen
before two new unrestricted synthetic-token families, 85001 and 95001, were
captured.  Both complete two-pass 120B executions repeated their routes, final
hidden states, and logits bitwise.

The layer-6 numerical candidate calculated in 0.0190~ms p50.  It did not
generalize: the two new relative $L_2$ errors were 0.0560 and 0.0736.  These
exceeded the 0.02 p95 target and the 0.05 maximum.  A single substituted layer
would also yield only 1.0909$\times$ projected traffic reduction.  The decision
was \texttt{STOP\_GHX\_ROUTE\_CRYSTAL}.  Report canonical/file SHA-256 values
are
\texttt{562ed9cad0fb2c9a3f6fd6bbc0a7531c6a4a4d9e01efca532dc19c8a8ac43b87}
and
\texttt{207cdb9f1059ed517798a2fc4f0245ba2bdb2411f0d952dc24e2d07df303ea0b}.
The SD evidence inventory SHA-256 is
\texttt{f6c3ad2c2e83f195a27b78a0d7a1007f0cff5050c1c81012cff1ca509b31062c}.

The inaccurate expert-output substitute is permanently ineligible.  The fast
matching primitive was retained as a separate, hash-verified, non-authorizing
selector.  It ranks candidate calculators and reports route coverage and score
margin, but it cannot approve a neural substitution.  A 1,000-query check

\subsection{Structured per-expert low-rank closure}

The next candidate assigned a separate gate-weighted affine feature block to
each of the 128 experts and represented the combined four-expert residual in a
learned low-rank basis.  Hyperparameters were selected using grouped
training-family cross-validation only; the calibration and holdout families
did not participate in selection.  The candidate calculated in 0.0305~ms p50
and 0.0343~ms p95, but accuracy failed: calibration relative $L_2$ was 0.1922
at layer 6 and 0.3463 at layer 9.  No layer was accepted and projected traffic
reduction remained 1.0$\times$.  The decision was
\texttt{STOP\_PER\_EXPERT\_LOW\_RANK}.

and
\texttt{246c18e1e558b70d9f5c4f8d725fb72d3428a8b5abb01650b527d0e521a4a2e6}.
The SD evidence inventory SHA-256 is

\subsection{Exact multi-token block hypothesis}

The programme then returned to the exact track.  Instead of approximating an
expert, several token positions are processed layer-major so that each exact
expert is fetched once and serves every position that routes to it.  For a
block of $B$ positions and $L$ layers, each selecting four experts, the ideal
load-amortization factor measured from an exact route trace is
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{load}} =
\frac{4BL}{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}
\left|\bigcup_{b=1}^{B}\mathcal{E}_{b,\ell}\right|},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E}_{b,\ell}$ is the four-expert route at position $b$ and
layer $\ell$.

Across two independent, bitwise-repeatable eight-position traces, the observed
all-layer analytical bounds were 1.8611$\times$ and 1.9136$\times$.  At four
positions the corresponding bounds were 1.3682$\times$ and 1.4656$\times$;
at two positions they were 1.1755$\times$ and 1.2361$\times$.  The eight-token
analytical report received
\texttt{ADVANCE\_EXACT\_BLOCK\_PROTOTYPE}.  Its canonical/file SHA-256 values
are
\texttt{6b9a801146fbb15feaa8b41ba9e465d8923c56723f79fb1305bb05ae25481c45}
and
\texttt{e198aa323cb1fcca0fdbfb0afe2af0e902d5157bd8d4df3122c3dff1d42df420}.
This was an analytical bound, not a measured runtime speedup.

\paragraph{Measurement boundary.}
This is a genuine exact layer-level speed and traffic result, but it is not yet
an end-to-end generation result.  It excludes draft generation, speculative
rejection, batched causal attention, output projection, and the remaining 35
layers.  The filesystem cache was not independently purged and classified for
this microgate; the balanced paired ratios are therefore evidence for reuse
within this execution path, not a universal cold-SD latency claim.

\subsection{Current architecture and next promotion gates}

The resulting direction is an exact speculative block runtime:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an already-resident smaller model drafts a bounded token block;
  \item GPT-OSS 120B processes those positions layer-major with causal masking;
  \item each layer loads the union of required exact experts once;
  \item expert work is grouped across all assigned positions;
  \item exact 120B logits accept matching draft tokens up to the first mismatch;
  \item rejected or incomplete positions return to the authoritative exact path.
\end{enumerate}

The next gate is a complete two-position layer-major transformer pass including
causal attention, all 36 MoE layers, final normalization, and teacher logits.
It must preserve exact token and step-logit behaviour and remain inside the
declared memory ceiling.  Only after two positions pass will the block expand
to four and eight positions.  End-to-end reporting must include draft
acceptance rate, accepted tokens per 120B verification pass, SD bytes per
accepted token, time to first token, p50/p95 latency, peak memory, and genuine
generated tokens per second.

The strategic conclusion is precise: low-capacity learned substitutes have not
yet generalized, whereas exact expert reuse across token positions has already
produced a measured 2.3461$\times$ layer speedup.  Consequently, exact
layer-major block execution is the promoted implementation path; the fast GHX
selector remains available as non-authorizing control infrastructure only.
