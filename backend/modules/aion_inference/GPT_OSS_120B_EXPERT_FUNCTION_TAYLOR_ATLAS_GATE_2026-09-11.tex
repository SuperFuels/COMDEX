\section{Local Taylor Expert Function Atlas: Held-Out Layer-12 Gate}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-taylor-atlas-gate}

\subsection{Purpose and claim boundary}

This experiment tested whether an omitted GPT-OSS 120B expert could be replaced by a
small, resident, expert-specific local function without repeatedly moving its full
MXFP4 weights.  It belongs exclusively to the quality-gated track.  The unrestricted
four-expert model remains the teacher and exact fallback.  This section reports a
single-layer numerical gate; it is not a full-model quality result and it makes no
generated-token throughput claim.

The business and writing blind cohorts remained sealed.  Arithmetic activations were
used to fit candidate geometries, extraction activations selected the configuration,
and reasoning activations were opened only after selection as the unseen development
family.

\subsection{Compiler design}

For each of all 128 experts in layer 12, AION queried the original SD-backed expert at
real activation anchors and along positive and negative principal activation
directions.  Central finite differences produced a local Jacobian.  At runtime the
nearest anchor supplied an expert-specific first-order Taylor approximation,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{E}_e(x) = E_e(a_j) + J_{e,j}\,(z(x)-z(a_j)),
\end{equation}
where $e$ identifies the original expert, $a_j$ is the nearest activation anchor,
and $z$ is the normalized low-dimensional activation coordinate.  Six bounded
configurations spanning one to four anchors and rank four to sixteen were selected
without access to the reasoning outputs.

The numerical source was the already-validated bounded GGML batch primitive at batch
size one.  Three preliminary invocations exposed a scratch-arena construction defect
in an experimental reusable-graph helper: the helper repeatedly consumed the whole
declared arena and then requested one final metadata object.  Those invocations
produced no atlas evidence and were excluded.  Increasing the arena was stopped; the
gate was rerun using the validated primitive instead.

The atlas reduced the unseen reasoning p95 error by approximately 17.5\% relative to
omitting the fourth expert entirely.  It was more than four times smaller than one
original expert and comfortably faster than the 0.4 ms correction-time ceiling.
Nevertheless, its 0.085561 p95 error was more than four times the predeclared 0.02
acceptance limit.

A post-gate certification audit strengthened the stopping decision.  None of the 21
unseen reasoning positions achieved relative layer-output error at or below 0.02;
none achieved 0.03; only one achieved 0.04.  Consequently, a certification mask at
the declared numerical boundary would accelerate zero observed positions.

\subsection{Decision and interpretation}

The status is \texttt{STOP\_TAYLOR\_ATLAS}.  The result is scientifically useful:
expert-specific local derivatives transfer better than deleting the expert, while
remaining exceptionally cheap.  They do not encode enough of the omitted nonlinear
expert function to preserve the layer output at the required accuracy.

This also closes a family of closely related candidates.  A prior direct randomized-
SVD factorization of the original gate, up, and down matrices produced approximately
1.06--1.09 relative expert-output error at ranks 32--128.  Prior shared MXFP4 product
dictionaries and activation-addressed residual-block oracles also failed their
frozen numerical gates.  Expanding the present atlas across layers would therefore
consume memory and engineering time without a certified operating region.

The programme returns to the measured frontier: exact four-expert execution with
demand-admitted storage and overlap, plus separately labelled quality-gated adaptive
expert selection, exact fallback, and multi-token reuse only where the reduced route
union makes reuse physically beneficial.  Context Atlases remain useful for reducing
prompt and retrieval work, but they must not be presented as a replacement for neural
expert computation or as evidence of higher decode tokens per second.
