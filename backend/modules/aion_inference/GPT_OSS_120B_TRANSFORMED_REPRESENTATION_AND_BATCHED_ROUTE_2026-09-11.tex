\section{Transformed Expert Representations and a Virtual Batched Route}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-transformed-representation-batched-route}

\subsection{Purpose and evidence boundary}

The exact unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B frontier remained approximately
$1.725$ token/s on the frozen arithmetic family and $1.829$ token/s on the
frozen extraction family.  Reaching 30 token/s therefore required a further
factor of approximately $16.4$ over the faster exact result.  Earlier work had
already shown that ordinary cache enlargement, conventional Metal execution,
component staging, activation sparsity, and unverified route restriction could
not supply that factor.  The experiments in this section consequently asked
two different questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Can the actual expert weights be represented with substantially fewer
        bytes while preserving their action on unseen activations?
  \item Can the four original experts be evaluated more efficiently without
        changing their identities or values?
\end{enumerate}

All transformed-weight experiments are explicitly quality-track experiments.
No transformed representation is called exact, and no microbenchmark rate is
reported as generated-token throughput.

\subsection{Shared MXFP4 product dictionary}

A 256-entry dictionary was trained over actual MXFP4 code subvectors from a
training-only set of expert weights.  Held-out routed experts and held-out real
activations were then reconstructed and evaluated through the original packed
expert arithmetic.  Three subvector widths were tested.  The dictionary index
stream provided strong projected compression, but the functional error was far
above the predeclared gates.

All three candidates were stopped.  Reducing the subvector width improved
fidelity monotonically, but the best tested case still exceeded the output
limit by $9.0\times$ at p95 and the contribution limit by $2.9\times$.
The result rejects this shared dictionary as a sufficient standalone encoding;
it does not reject dictionaries combined with a separately verified residual.

\subsection{Direct factorisation of an actual routed expert}

The gate, up, and down matrices of layer 15, expert 33 were dequantised from
their original MXFP4 representation and independently approximated using
truncated randomised singular-value decomposition.  Factorisation was performed
without reference to the evaluation activations.  Float16 factors, the original
biases, and the original SwiGLU-OAI non-linearity were evaluated on two frozen
real activations on which that expert had actually been selected.

The singular spectra explain the failure.  At rank 128, only 28.60\% of the
gate matrix energy, 23.12\% of the up matrix energy, and 32.07\% of the down
matrix energy were captured.  The matrices are not sufficiently low-rank in
their original basis for this representation to be useful at the required
compression.  The branch was stopped.  Its canonical evidence digest is
\texttt{cd70271586b1ffd8e7055f791c309068f5302284a6d468a9d2e2d8fd6818346f}.

\subsection{Four-expert batched CPU execution}

The next probe retained all four selected experts and their original MXFP4
weights.  Instead of constructing twelve independent matrix operations---gate,
up, and down for each of four experts---the candidate stacked the four experts
along a batch dimension and issued three batched matrix operations.  It then
applied the original gates and combined the four outputs.

Under the actual 8~GiB protected-pool pressure, a contiguous pre-staged route
reduced median four-expert arithmetic from 1.0921~ms to 0.6701~ms, a
$1.630\times$ kernel speedup.  Relative output error was
$4.35\times10^{-8}$ and the output argmax was unchanged.  The outputs were not
bitwise identical because the batched graph changed floating-point accumulation
order.  This is therefore a high-fidelity quality-track result, not an exact
result.  The canonical evidence digest is
\texttt{2f732a778f6acb2662f45d98316d1900124f280a9ea8aeed6069bc0b4f1fa39a}.

\subsection{Copy-free virtual route arena}

Pre-staging a contiguous route by copying approximately 52.9~MB per layer would
reintroduce the resident-memory traffic removed by earlier AION work.  A second
probe therefore reserved one virtual address range and mapped the independently
stored component files into page-padded expert slots.  GGML was presented with
the resulting strided three-dimensional tensor; no user-space expert-weight
copy was performed.

The mapping operation cost 1.373~ms for the complete four-expert route.  Once
mapped, median arithmetic was 0.7125~ms versus 0.7862~ms for the serial control,
a $1.104\times$ p50 improvement.  More importantly, p95 fell from 4.2497~ms to
1.2648~ms, a $3.36\times$ tail-latency improvement.  Relative output error was
again $4.35\times10^{-8}$ with equal argmax, but not bitwise equality.  The
canonical evidence digest is
\texttt{8b6b3b9434f30c3fdda951a83d33b09b924223ab85b6284922c38988c34298be}.

This proves a useful systems mechanism: independently addressable warehouse
objects can be exposed to a batched compute kernel as one logical tensor without
physically copying the weights.  The one-route mapping cost is small relative
to the current full-token time, but the measured p50 arithmetic gain is not yet
large enough to imply an order-of-magnitude generated-token improvement.

\subsection{Decision and next gate}

The product dictionary and direct low-rank factorisation branches are stopped.
The virtual batched route is retained as a composable quality-track candidate.
It must next be integrated behind an explicit runtime switch and tested over
all 36 layers and multiple generated tokens.  Promotion requires frozen semantic
quality, internal repeatability, bounded memory, lower p50 and p95 token latency,
and measured end-to-end improvement after charging route mapping.  The exact
unrestricted implementation remains the control and is unchanged.

The central physical conclusion remains unchanged: batching can reduce
arithmetic overhead and tail latency, but the 30 token/s North Star still
requires a large reduction in expert bytes delivered per accepted token.  The
next representation candidate must therefore combine a compact transformed
expert form with a directly executable kernel, or eliminate expert evaluations
through a quality-gated mechanism whose failures fall back safely to the
unrestricted warehouse path.
