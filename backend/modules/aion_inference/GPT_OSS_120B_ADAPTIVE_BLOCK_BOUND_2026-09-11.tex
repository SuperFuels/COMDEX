\section{Adaptive Multi-Position Reuse Bound}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-adaptive-block-bound-20260911}

The preserved-mass adaptive continuation policy and multi-position expert
union were promising separately, so their maximum compositional opportunity
was measured before constructing another full transformer runtime.  The audit
used the already verified 0.85 preserved-mass arithmetic and extraction traces.
For every non-overlapping two-, four-, and eight-position response window, it
counted the original active expert demands and the unique layer--expert union
that a teacher-forced block could fetch once.  No prompt text, hidden state, or
quality decision was used to select a favourable window.

For arithmetic, median delivery reductions were 1.1169, 1.3605, and 1.6718
times for blocks of two, four, and eight positions.  Extraction produced
1.1494, 1.3484, and 1.6047 times.  The best individual window in either family
reached 1.9943 times.  Thus adaptive expert counts do improve the union density,
but even a known eight-token teacher block cannot remove more than roughly half
the expert delivery in the best observed window.

The 30-token/s target is 16.406 times the promoted 1.82858-token/s exact
extraction frontier.  Under the intentionally favourable and physically
unrealistic assumption that all elapsed time scales linearly with expert
delivery, the best observed 1.9943-times window would yield only 3.6467
tokens/s.  Actual gain must be lower because attention, routing, output,
scheduling, draft generation, rejection and recovery remain.

A separate trajectory check compared the existing fast top-two arithmetic
draft with the preserved-mass target.  They shared only the first five greedy
response tokens before diverging.  This is a single initial-prefix observation,
not a post-rejection acceptance rate, but it confirms that known teacher tokens
cannot be assumed free.  Autoregressive generation still requires a draft and
verification/recovery mechanism.

Decision:
\texttt{DO\_NOT\_BUILD\_FULL\_COMPOSITE\_FROM\_ROUTE\_REUSE\_ALONE}.
Multi-position reuse remains a legitimate supporting optimization, but its
measured physical ceiling is too small to be the missing order-of-magnitude
mechanism.  It should be composed only after a future candidate independently
reduces active expert bytes or supplies a high-acceptance cheap draft.  The
canonical receipt SHA-256 is
\texttt{aace38fa5d0bb09ef94549aa0f89a49171843490cde855f8adc2ea1aa59a1aba}.
