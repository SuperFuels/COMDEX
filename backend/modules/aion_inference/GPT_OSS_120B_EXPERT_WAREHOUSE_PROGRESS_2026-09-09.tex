\section{Quality-Gated 120B Expert Warehouse on the Existing 64~GB Card}
\label{sec:gpt-oss-120b-expert-warehouse-progress}
\begingroup
\sloppy

\subsection{Objective and claim boundary}

This stage tests whether the complete selected representation of
\texttt{gpt-oss-120b} can be retained on the existing nominal 64~GB SD card
and exposed as a bounded, expert-addressed AION warehouse.  The selected
source is the two-shard \texttt{Q4\_K\_M} GGUF conversion at revision
\texttt{9480dc9fd99da05bca65e263301fce0223af6797}.  The source is the
\href{https://huggingface.co/bartowski/openai_gpt-oss-120b-GGUF}%
{Bartowski gpt-oss-120b GGUF repository}.

Three distinct integrity claims must not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The outer AION Zstandard representation is intended to be bit-exact
        to every byte of the selected \texttt{Q4\_K\_M} GGUF shards.
  \item \texttt{Q4\_K\_M} is itself a changed-weight representation of the
        official model.  It therefore remains on a separately labelled,
        quality-gated track.  It is not an exact-original-weight claim.
  \item No model-quality, generation-speed, time-to-first-token or usable
        120B-inference claim is made until the full import, source-hash
        verification and integrated generation gates have completed.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Exact capacity gate}

The selected source shards contain 62,841,713,344 bytes.  The prepared ExFAT
volume provided 62,180,294,656 free bytes at selection time.  A conventional
copy was therefore short by 661,418,688 bytes.  The minimum required reduction
was
\begin{equation}
 r_{\min} = 100\left(1-\frac{62{,}180{,}294{,}656}
 {62{,}841{,}713{,}344}\right) = 1.0525\%.
\end{equation}
This narrow deficit motivated a lossless outer-storage experiment before any
additional neural quantization, pruning or expert removal was considered.

Representative 64~MiB ranges sampled from both real GGUF shards compressed
losslessly by 4.0395--5.1618\% at Zstandard level~3.  Every sample was decoded
again and compared to the source bytes.  This exceeded the 1.0525\% capacity
threshold, but sampling alone was not treated as proof that the complete model
would fit.

\subsection{Rejected fixed-block layout}

The first resumable importer divided each logical GGUF shard into independent
64~MiB frames.  It completed 43 verified chunks, representing 2,885,681,152
source bytes and 2,686,027,077 compressed bytes, for a 6.9188\% reduction.
The source-capacity result was promising, but live inspection of the GGUF
header exposed a serious execution defect in this layout.

Each MoE weight tensor has dimensions $2880\times2880\times128$ and occupies
564,019,200 bytes.  One expert projection occupies 4,406,400 bytes; its
associated bias occupies 11,520 bytes.  A fixed 64~MiB frame could consequently
force the runtime to read and decode substantial neighboring-expert data to
obtain a single 4.4~MB projection.  The layout would fit the card while
undermining the address-selective execution objective.

The fixed-block import was stopped before completion.  Its immutable
invalidation sidecar has SHA-256
\texttt{7d316e5e7f4579474b7e662a8066dfb}%
\allowbreak\texttt{e461c0697be542e30a1ac36f82528b69f}.
The incomplete 2.7~GB staging directory was moved to the macOS Trash and can be
recovered; it was not represented as completed evidence.  This is an example
of the programme's falsification rule operating correctly: capacity alone was
insufficient for promotion.

\subsection{Expert-aligned replacement geometry}

Both GGUF headers were parsed without downloading or materialising the complete
files.  Shard~1 contains 438 tensor records and covers layers 0--22, with
layer~22 continuing into shard~2.  Shard~2 contains 249 tensor records and
covers layers 22--35.  The resulting source-bound plan covers every byte from
the beginning of each GGUF header to the end of each shard with neither gaps
nor overlap.

Every expert weight and bias tensor is divided along its 128-expert dimension.
Shared tensors and headers use independent frames capped at 64~MiB.  The frozen
plan contains:

Transactional regions & 689 \\
Expert-aligned frames & 27,648 \\
Shared/header frames & 487 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The plan's canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{379913c699fae493a66ef72d9664c071}%
\allowbreak\texttt{9458da6db6fce2cf360bc3ec64ec96e6};
the plan file SHA-256 is
\texttt{ff34f7b601c728b67d127c7ff75deb1a}%
\allowbreak\texttt{d149e399694a80c850681b2195818cd2}.

A complete expert comprises the gate, up and down weights and biases, totalling
13,253,760 raw bytes.  Four completely cold active experts at one layer would
therefore represent 53,015,040 raw bytes before reuse or coalescing.  The new
geometry allows AION to fetch precisely those expert frames and provides the
foundation for bounded LRU retention, route-aware coalescing and layer-ahead
overlap.  It does not assert that those later mechanisms have already won.

\subsection{Crash-safe import and proof receipts}

The replacement importer downloads one planned tensor region at a time to
bounded internal staging, emits independently compressed frames directly into
the SD warehouse, decodes every frame immediately, and requires the decoded
SHA-256 to match the downloaded bytes.  Only then is the pack atomically
promoted and its receipt written.  Interrupted regions are discarded or
repeated; completed region packs remain resumable.

Every frame receipt records the logical source offset, raw byte count, expert
identity where applicable, encoded pack offset, compressed byte count, raw
SHA-256 and compressed SHA-256.  The importer reserves 1~GiB of card headroom
and fails closed if the next transaction could cross that boundary.  When all
regions are present, it must reconstruct each logical shard through the frame
decoder and match the upstream full-shard hashes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item shard~1:
  \texttt{48b6bea561b52aaa3c10ca7681f35a92}%
  \allowbreak\texttt{3196cc348098e55ffc6ee3d184eb0949};
  \item shard~2:
  \texttt{06ab40ac3a9e2d7a77b6491b4cf76af}%
  \allowbreak\texttt{152bee88ce493331a1f5147a0207abcad}.
\end{itemize}
No \texttt{COMPLETE\_VERIFIED} manifest is emitted unless both checks pass.

\subsection{Bounded readers and runtime preparation}

Two fail-closed reader components were implemented.  The generic compressed
GGUF reader presents a seekable logical byte stream while retaining only a
declared number of decoded frames.  It checks both compressed and decoded
hashes on every cache fault.  The gpt-oss expert store constructs Glyph
addresses of the form
\texttt{layer/expert/projection/kind}, reads the six frames belonging to one
expert, and retains complete experts under a strict raw-byte LRU ceiling.
It records faults, hits, evictions, compressed bytes read, raw bytes
materialised and peak resident bytes.  Both readers reject incomplete or
unverified warehouse manifests.

\subsection{Bound progress snapshot}

At 2026-09-09 13:25:56 UTC, the expert-aligned import remained

\subsection{Required next gates}

The following gates remain mandatory, in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item finish all 689 transactions without breaching the declared reserve;
  \item reconstruct and verify both complete upstream shard hashes;
  \item verify complete 36-layer, 128-expert address coverage, including the
        layer~22 cross-shard boundary;
  \item measure real cold and retained expert reads, decompression amplification,
        cache reuse and p50/p95 latency from the SD card;
  \item integrate shared tensors, routing and expert materialisation under the
        18~GB unified-memory ceiling;
  \item obtain the first valid one-token and short-generation controls before
        attempting longer generation;
  \item run frozen and unseen quality cohorts against an independently defined
        control, reporting failures and regressions; and
  \item only then report 120B time to first token, tokens per second, peak
        memory, SD traffic and completed-task performance.
\end{enumerate}

The result recorded here is therefore a substantial storage-architecture and
integrity advance, not yet proof that the 120B model runs successfully or at a
commercially useful speed on the 18~GB Mac.
