\section{Balanced Route-Conditioned Student and Prepared-Slab Reassessment}
\label{sec:gptoss-balanced-student-slab-20260912}
\begingroup
\setlength{\emergencystretch}{3em}

\subsection{Scope and invariant boundaries}

This section records a fresh iteration of two previously promising mechanisms:
(i) a route-conditioned AtomSheet residual student intended to replace only a
certified omitted-expert contribution, and (ii) demand-admitted prepared route
slabs intended to amortise a previously measured batched four-expert kernel.
The authoritative unrestricted track was not changed.  Its promoted short-window
frontier remains approximately 1.72--1.83 tokens/s, while the fixed top--1
28.6174 tokens/s result remains a physical ceiling with failed semantic quality.
All learned and compact-weight candidates in this section are quality-track
experiments with mandatory unrestricted fallback.

\subsection{SD-native public teacher corpus}

The previously recovered SD-native GPT-OSS tokenizer was used to prepare four
public, non-personal prompt families: explanation, business, reasoning, and
writing.  No model or tokenizer was downloaded.  Each initial teacher capture
was executed twice through the existing compressed SD warehouse.  Every family
reproduced its router selections and achieved bit-for-bit identical final hidden
state and logits on the two passes.

\subsection{Route-conditioned residual students}

For every record, three original routed experts were calculated from the
compressed warehouse.  Their exact gated contribution was subtracted from the
unrestricted four-expert contribution, making the fourth expert's combined
effect the supervised target.  The resulting 1,416-row dataset is 74,991,988
bytes and has canonical hash
\texttt{078aed556b0cc275500d3697d3b52616}\allowbreak
\texttt{00c90b11391e3d06c2b58f50d5540619}.

Two student representations were selected by holding out complete training
families.  The compact version used 96 signed bins for each activation vector;
the full version retained all 2,880 coordinates of the FFN input, router input,
and retained-expert contribution.  Both also received the four expert identities,
gates, layer identity, omitted gate mass, and gate entropy.

\subsection{Prepared route-slab reuse bound}

The earlier batched four-expert kernel avoided repeated orchestration and was
approximately 1.63-fold faster after preparation, but copying 52.9 MB into a
contiguous slab erased the gain.  The new test admitted a prepared slab only
after observing the same layer-qualified ordered route three times.

On the unrestricted 96-token extraction trace there were 3,456 layer routes and
3,345 unique ordered quartets.  Only one quartet was admitted and only one later
call reused it, a 0.0289\% reuse rate.  The exact-quartet mechanism is therefore
stopped.  Under the adaptive-0.75 trace, 84 slabs required approximately 4.45 GB
but covered only 200 post-admission calls (5.787\%), which is also uneconomic.
Ordered pairs improved nominal reuse, but three-touch admission still required
92 slabs (approximately 2.44 GB) for 172 reuses (4.977\%).  Two-touch admission
required approximately 5.91 GB for 395 reuses.  These storage costs cannot be
repaid by the sub-millisecond per-call arithmetic saving.

The unrestricted quartet and adaptive quartet canonical hashes are
\texttt{22b9ffcded876c34b51588abe5e1bde1}\allowbreak\texttt{6fabbcd4d1bbac4726e42b2ebd37cc41}
and
\texttt{46da48e164de603913ea2192cfa4b4c6}\allowbreak\texttt{faa0689f2841bd1479e73d09ea864f1a}.
The exact ordered-pair three-touch hash is
\texttt{1bfde14305ddf9ef4559665c08f603ec}\allowbreak\texttt{e6b06a861f2f81a30b75604318a3c7b4}.

\subsection{Rank- and projection-selective compact weights}

A new native mixed-representation kernel allowed Q3\_K or Q2\_0 to be applied
independently by route rank and projection.  This revisited the earlier successful
fourth-expert Q3 result without quantising an entire expert.

On 21 unseen reasoning positions at layer 12, Q3\_K on the \emph{up} projection
of routed experts two through four achieved output-relative $L_2$ p95 0.01779,
preserved all 21 argmax values, and reduced route weight by 2.03\%.  Restricting
Q3\_K to the bottom two experts improved p95 to 0.01341 with 1.35\% weight
reduction.  Applying both gate and up to the bottom two narrowly failed at
0.02128.  The corresponding canonical hashes are
\texttt{e039fc57f9d4b6e9609c0c59571f2aa7}\allowbreak\texttt{2aa4127e4f1df8c8a5fbf2fc114c0e43}
and
\texttt{f7167e408f3c4d0e942721769c353dfb}\allowbreak\texttt{209736ef5606c06bcc41c385c8add86c}.

Selective native-width Q2\_0 was also tested as a larger compression step.
Even when applied only to the fourth expert, its best single projection produced
p95 0.04552; gate and down were worse.  Although computation improved roughly
1.12--1.14-fold and a single compact projection reduced total route weight by
4.08\%, no mask passed the 0.02 accuracy gate.  The Q2 branch is stopped.  Its
canonical hash is
\texttt{583ced0885c262689bd1505076b511d74}\allowbreak\texttt{6902a09a5498a0a904049332551941f}.

\subsection{Weight-derived sparse correction bound}

The next experiment tested whether the accuracy-passing Q3\_K up projection
could be paired with a small exact weight-residual cartridge.  Eight real
rank-four layer-12 experts selected by the untouched reasoning trace were
dequantised from the SD warehouse.  Their Q3\_K matrices were dequantised in
the same orientation, and the exact weight-space residual was measured by row
and by $64\times64$ block.  This is an intentionally optimistic offline bound:
if error energy is highly concentrated, a small set of exact blocks could
correct most of it without restoring the complete MXFP4 matrix.

The mechanism failed decisively.  The most energetic 10\% of residual blocks
contained only 11.365\% of residual energy at the median, versus the
predeclared 75\% minimum.  Q3 quantisation error is consequently spread almost
uniformly through these expert matrices.  A sparse exact correction would have
to retain nearly the whole residual and would forfeit the storage advantage.
The branch is recorded as \texttt{STOP\_SPARSE\_Q3\_RESIDUAL}; its canonical
hash is
\texttt{4283f1e00aa29e381a3dd645b1d522bf}\allowbreak\texttt{658b8486b27ba88e7899dec92d828013}.

\subsection{Current conclusion and next admissible move}

No result in this section changes the validated generated-token rate.  The
prepared-slab mechanism lacks sufficient route repetition; activation-only and
generic route-conditioned students still lack the expert-specific nonlinear
structure; selective Q2 is too inaccurate; and Q3 weight error is not sparse.
Selective Q3 is the only new
accuracy-passing weight transformation, but its present padded kernel yields a
small byte reduction and no material complete-model speed claim.

The next admissible student must therefore be expert-function-conditioned rather
than merely route-conditioned: a shared global basis plus small per-layer,
per-expert factors derived from original weights, trained only on authorised
public captures, with certification by expert and activation region.  A route
may use the calculator only when its exact held-out certificate passes; otherwise
it escalates to two, three, or four original experts.  Any speed claim requires
unseen generated-token and semantic validation against the unrestricted teacher.
