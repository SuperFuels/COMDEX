\section{GPT-OSS 120B Output-Head Defect and Quality Ceiling}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-output-head-defect-20260911}

\subsection{Scope and claim boundary}

This section records a material runtime defect, its correction, and the
experiments performed after the correction.  Two tracks remain strictly
separate.  The exact track retains the unrestricted 128-expert router, four
selected experts per layer, the original gates, and the original weights.  The
quality track deliberately changes continuation computation by selecting fewer
experts.  A quality-track rate is therefore a physical/runtime ceiling and not
bit-exact unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B performance.

The verified exact frontier preceding this work remains 1.72455 tokens/s on
the frozen arithmetic family and 1.82858 tokens/s on the frozen extraction
family.  Nothing in this section supersedes that exact frontier.

\subsection{Caught defect: an optional diagnostic became mandatory work}

The runtime uses a layer-range parser with a deliberate default: an absent
adaptive-policy range means ``all 36 layers.''  The early-exit diagnostic
initially reused that parser.  Consequently, absence of
\texttt{--early-exit-diagnostic-layers} also meant all 36 layers.  The runtime
therefore evaluated the 615,329,280-byte Q8\_0 vocabulary projection after
every transformer layer, retained only the final result, and discarded 35
intermediate vocabulary projections per token.

Profiling exposed 216 vocabulary-selection calls in the six-token/two-pass
diagnostic and approximately 0.285--0.300 seconds of otherwise unaccounted
layer time per token.  The repair gives optional diagnostics their own
semantics: an absent optional range is the empty set.  Regression tests now
separately assert that an absent adaptive range means all layers while an
absent diagnostic range means no layers.

\subsection{Measured physical-ceiling change}

The shortest repeatable probe used exact prompt handling followed by a fixed
top-1 continuation inside the existing hash-bound 8~GiB family workbench.  It
is intentionally a changed-model quality track.  The generated token sequence
was unchanged by the defect repair, allowing the runtime cost to be compared
without confusing it with a different trajectory.

The best settled observation is 9.44 times the defective 3.0318-token/s
control.  Six threads outperformed five, seven, and eight; seven threads was
unstable under the measured memory/CPU pressure.  The corrected settled
six-thread token costs were approximately 14--19 ms for attention, 10--14 ms
for expert finish, 6--8 ms for the vocabulary head, and 1--2 ms for routing,
plus small orchestration overhead.

This result is a major runtime-ceiling discovery, but it is not a deployable
quality result.  Fixed top-1 continuation produced repetitive/incoherent text
on the frozen arithmetic probe.  It proves that the complete 36-layer 120B
backbone can physically traverse at nearly 30 evaluations/s when expert
traffic is reduced to one resident expert per layer; it does not prove that
useful unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B generates at that rate.

\subsection{Exact-track control}

The corrected runtime was also exercised on the matched 48-position exact
arithmetic path.  Both passes were route-repeatable and bitwise repeatable in
final hidden states and step logits.  The second pass reached 1.5946 tokens/s
without the promoted overlap configuration.  It did not beat the frozen
1.72455-token/s exact arithmetic control.  This confirms that the defect was
introduced with the later optional diagnostic and did not invalidate or
artificially depress the earlier promoted exact frontier.

\subsection{Stopped narrow optimisations}

Three narrow candidates were preserved rather than promoted:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A persistent native F32 router matched the top-four expert indices on
  all 1,728 measured layer decisions.  Maximum router-logit drift was
  $2.3842\times10^{-5}$ and median projection time was approximately
  0.341 ms/layer, but end-to-end throughput regressed.
  \item Persistent in-process Q5\_0 token embedding reduced embedding lookup to
  approximately 0.015 ms and preserved the token/hidden trajectory, but did
  not materially change total speed because embedding was not the bottleneck.
  \item A persistent Metal vocabulary head uploaded the 615 MB Q8\_0 matrix
  once and subsequently uploaded only the hidden vector.  It was internally
  repeatable, but settled throughput was only 13.356 tokens/s, versus 28.617
  for the CPU output head, and its generated sequence differed.  This Metal
  branch is stopped for both performance and numerical reasons.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Adaptive-quality experiments}

An aggressive preserved-mass 0.65 policy allowing one to four continuation
experts failed.  The unrestricted-fallback version incurred 26--27~GiB of
expert traffic and reached only 0.55--0.74 tokens/s.  The constrained-cartridge
version reached at most 2.22 tokens/s and produced incomplete/repetitive text.
A global preserved-mass 0.70 policy, bounded to two or three experts, increased
two-expert decisions from 101 to 209 of 1,188 continuation layer evaluations,
but diverged into repetition and is also stopped.

A narrower layer-selective policy retained the known-correct 0.75 threshold in
layers 0--27 and used 0.70 only in layers 28--35.  On arithmetic it converted
18 additional calls from three experts to two (119 instead of 101) while
reproducing all 33 continuation token IDs of the correct 0.75 control.  On the
independent extraction family it converted five additional calls (57 instead
of 52) and reproduced all 38 continuation token IDs.  Candidate passes were
internally route- and bitwise-repeatable.  Extraction settled at 5.4082
tokens/s versus 5.3437 for the older control, a 1.21\% observation; arithmetic
timing regressed in the separately executed run.  Because the timing was not a
balanced same-process comparison, this policy is retained as a mechanically
valid quality candidate but is not promoted as a speed improvement.

\subsection{Interpretation and next gate}

The defect repair closes most of the software gap to 30 tokens/s only in the
one-expert physical-ceiling experiment.  The remaining scientific problem is
quality-preserving reduction of expert traffic.  Global thresholds are too
coarse, while the two-family late-layer result shows that selective reductions
can leave the observed answer unchanged.

The next gate is therefore a learned, layer- and confidence-conditioned
dictionary with an exact residual/fallback boundary.  It should predict where
the third or fourth expert contribution is consequential, retain full
computation on uncertain layers, and reduce experts only on independently
validated low-impact layers.  Advancement requires blinded arithmetic,
extraction, reasoning, and business prompts; coherent completion; internal
repeatability; reported traffic and memory; and a balanced control/candidate
timing protocol.  No 30-token/s quality claim is made until those gates pass.
