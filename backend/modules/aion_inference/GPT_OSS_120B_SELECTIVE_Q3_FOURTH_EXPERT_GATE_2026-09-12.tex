\section{Selective Fourth-Expert Q3\_K Accuracy-Recovery Gate}
\label{sec:selective-fourth-expert-q3k}

\subsection{Purpose and predeclared boundary}

The preceding 2.08\,MB AtomSheet residual calculator was extremely fast
(approximately 0.040\,ms) but failed its untouched reasoning gate: none of the
21 held-out positions achieved the required output-relative $L_2$ error of at
most 0.02.  An oracle certification check confirmed that this was a
representation failure, not merely a poor confidence selector: the required
fallback rate was 100\%.  The present experiment therefore retained the three
largest routed expert contributions in their original MXFP4 representation and
changed only selected projections of the fourth, lowest-gate expert to padded
Q3\_K.

This is an explicitly quality-gated representation experiment.  It changes
expert weights and is not bit-exact unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B inference.  The
gate was fixed before the measured run: output-relative $L_2$ p95 no greater
than 0.02, identical layer-output argmax at every measured position, at least
1.005-fold route-weight reduction, no hidden fallback, and separate reporting
for reasoning, arithmetic and extraction.  The experiment was restricted to
real layer-12 routes and does not constitute a generated-token throughput or
semantic-quality claim.

\subsection{Method}

For every captured position the unrestricted router, its four selected expert
identities, all four gate values, all biases, the first three experts and the
reduction order were preserved.  Seven masks were tested over the fourth
expert's gate, up and down matrices.  A set bit replaced only that projection
with a Q3\_K matrix obtained directly from the original MXFP4 weights.  The
Q3\_K matrix was zero-padded internally from width 2880 to 3072; values were
sliced back to the native width before combination with the preserved experts.
The best cross-family mask compacted the fourth expert's gate and up
projections while retaining its down projection in original MXFP4 form.

The candidate reduced the four-expert route's weight bytes by
1.3469\% (a 1.01347-fold reduction).  Across the pooled 76 positions, 75 were
individually below 0.02 output-relative $L_2$; the one arithmetic outlier was
0.02369.  The predeclared family-wise p95 gate nevertheless passed in all
three families, and every measured layer-output argmax remained unchanged.
The pooled median isolated layer-compute speedup was approximately
1.191$\times$.  Because the timing is a layer-12 microbenchmark, it must not be
extrapolated to full-model tokens per second.

Compacting all three fourth-expert projections was rejected as a general
candidate despite passing reasoning: arithmetic p95 was 0.02554 and extraction
p95 was 0.02389.  The projection-isolation experiment therefore improved both
accuracy and compute behaviour by retaining the sensitive down projection.

\subsection{Interpretation and next gate}

This is the first tested compact fourth-expert representation to clear the
declared 0.02 p95 numerical gate across reasoning, arithmetic and extraction.
It replaces the failed proposition ``learn the omitted expert from a tiny
activation-only calculator'' with the narrower proposition ``retain three
original experts and compact only demonstrably tolerant components of the
fourth expert.''  The result is a genuine accuracy advance, but the 1.35\%
traffic reduction is not an orders-of-magnitude speed result.

The candidate may advance only to a bounded multi-layer substitution test with
unseen prompts.  That test must measure accumulated hidden-state and logit
error, generated-token agreement, semantic quality, p50/p95 latency, bytes
moved and fallback behaviour.  Failure at the token or semantic boundary will
stop the branch; success at layer 12 alone does not justify warehouse-wide
conversion.
