\section{Selective Fourth-Expert Q3\_K Accuracy-Recovery Gate}
\label{sec:selective-fourth-expert-q3k}

\subsection{Purpose and predeclared boundary}

The preceding 2.08\,MB AtomSheet residual calculator was extremely fast
(approximately 0.040\,ms) but failed its untouched reasoning gate: none of the
21 held-out positions achieved the required output-relative $L_2$ error of at
most 0.02.  An oracle certification check confirmed that this was a
representation failure, not merely a poor confidence selector: the required
fallback rate was 100\%.  The present experiment therefore retained the three
largest routed expert contributions in their original MXFP4 representation and
changed only selected projections of the fourth, lowest-gate expert to padded
Q3\_K.

This is an explicitly quality-gated representation experiment.  It changes
expert weights and is not bit-exact unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B inference.  The
gate was fixed before the measured run: output-relative $L_2$ p95 no greater
than 0.02, identical layer-output argmax at every measured position, at least
1.005-fold route-weight reduction, no hidden fallback, and separate reporting
for reasoning, arithmetic and extraction.  The experiment was restricted to
real layer-12 routes and does not constitute a generated-token throughput or
semantic-quality claim.

\subsection{Method}

For every captured position the unrestricted router, its four selected expert
identities, all four gate values, all biases, the first three experts and the
reduction order were preserved.  Seven masks were tested over the fourth
expert's gate, up and down matrices.  A set bit replaced only that projection
with a Q3\_K matrix obtained directly from the original MXFP4 weights.  The
Q3\_K matrix was zero-padded internally from width 2880 to 3072; values were
sliced back to the native width before combination with the preserved experts.
The best cross-family mask compacted the fourth expert's gate and up
projections while retaining its down projection in original MXFP4 form.

The candidate reduced the four-expert route's weight bytes by
1.3469\% (a 1.01347-fold reduction).  Across the pooled 76 positions, 75 were
individually below 0.02 output-relative $L_2$; the one arithmetic outlier was
0.02369.  The predeclared family-wise p95 gate nevertheless passed in all
three families, and every measured layer-output argmax remained unchanged.
The pooled median isolated layer-compute speedup was approximately
1.191$\times$.  Because the timing is a layer-12 microbenchmark, it must not be
extrapolated to full-model tokens per second.

Compacting all three fourth-expert projections was rejected as a general
candidate despite passing reasoning: arithmetic p95 was 0.02554 and extraction
p95 was 0.02389.  The projection-isolation experiment therefore improved both
accuracy and compute behaviour by retaining the sensitive down projection.

\subsection{Interpretation and next gate}

This is the first tested compact fourth-expert representation to clear the
declared 0.02 p95 numerical gate across reasoning, arithmetic and extraction.
It replaces the failed proposition ``learn the omitted expert from a tiny
activation-only calculator'' with the narrower proposition ``retain three
original experts and compact only demonstrably tolerant components of the
fourth expert.''  The result is a genuine accuracy advance, but the 1.35\%
traffic reduction is not an orders-of-magnitude speed result.

The candidate may advance only to a bounded multi-layer substitution test with
unseen prompts.  That test must measure accumulated hidden-state and logit
error, generated-token agreement, semantic quality, p50/p95 latency, bytes
moved and fallback behaviour.  Failure at the token or semantic boundary will
stop the branch; success at layer 12 alone does not justify warehouse-wide
conversion.

\subsection{Full-transformer transfer and confidence fallback}

The layer-12 candidate was subsequently integrated into the complete
36-layer runtime.  The implementation accepts an explicit layer allowlist,
caches each derived Q3\_K projection, records conversion cost separately, and
labels the result as a changed-weight quality track.  No converted weight was
written into the source warehouse.  An initial persistent-L2 integration run
caught a Python boundary defect (a zero-copy \texttt{memoryview} was combined
with a byte string); it terminated before emitting an evidence file and was
repaired without changing the numerical or performance gate.

In a two-pass BOS test, both unrestricted control and layer-12 candidate were
internally bitwise repeatable and generated token ID 2167.  The changed layer
did propagate: the first route difference appeared at layer 19 and only 25 of
36 routes matched the separately executed control.  Thus token equality at BOS
was treated as a narrow result, not as proof of semantic equivalence.

The candidate was then evaluated on a 15-token arithmetic prompt followed by
five autoregressive positions.  In the proven 8\,GiB workbench plus 20\,GiB
demand-admitted cartridge configuration, the unconditional candidate matched
all five teacher token IDs
\[
  [35644,,200008,,2167,,1309,,316]
\]
and remained bitwise repeatable across its two candidate runs.  Nevertheless,
its settled five-token rate was 1.32879 tokens/s, below the frozen unrestricted
control's 1.74117 tokens/s.  The unconditional candidate was therefore stopped
as a performance strategy.

A confidence gate was derived from the existing cross-family layer captures:
use the selective Q3\_K gate/up path only when the fourth normalized router
gate is at most 0.20; otherwise execute the original fourth expert.  Every
previously measured admitted position had output-relative $L_2<0.018$.  In the
same 20-position arithmetic execution, this rule applied the candidate on 17
of 20 layer-12 calls, matched all five teacher continuation tokens, and remained
internally repeatable.  It recovered settled continuation to 1.67846 tokens/s
and median token latency to 0.56382\,s, compared with 1.74117 tokens/s and
0.56672\,s for the frozen exact control.  Since aggregate throughput remained
3.60\% lower, the confidence-gated candidate is also
\texttt{NOT\_PROMOTED} for speed.

This experiment establishes a useful but bounded conclusion: selective
projection compaction can survive a real prompt and preserve a short generated
token sequence, while confidence fallback removes most of its route-induced
performance regression.  However, compacting two projections of the fourth
expert in only one layer saves too little physical traffic to beat the complete
exact runtime.  The promoted exact frontier therefore remains approximately
1.72--1.83 tokens/s.  Further multi-layer expansion is prohibited until a new
candidate predicts downstream consequence, not merely local layer error, and
demonstrates a positive balanced end-to-end speed gate.

\subsection{Compact error-correction audit and invalidation}

A subsequent experiment attempted to correct the remaining layer-12 Q3\_K
gate/up error with a compact learned residual.  Three selected experts remained
in original MXFP4 form; only the fourth expert's gate and up projections used
Q3\_K, its down projection remained original, and a 163,536-byte rank-4/rank-4
correction cartridge was fitted on arithmetic captures.  Extraction selected
the capacity and reasoning remained unseen development data.  The correction
itself took 0.015417\,ms at median.

The first report incorrectly returned an advance decision because its gate
tested only the absolute two-percent relative-$L_2$ limit.  This was inadequate:
the uncorrected Q3\_K representation already satisfied that limit on both
held-out families.  The original report is preserved, never overwritten, and
is bound to an \texttt{INVALID} sidecar.  Its stored-file SHA-256 is
\texttt{3b8061e101814c701b491c075a4793238a01cf2b6263f023a9c184936109baea}
and its canonical hash is
\texttt{ca29eb904d34661a5be7d7c79bd2ecab2af3796a3be5dae09a7d4b60b9480786}.

The repaired version-2 gate additionally requires at least a ten-percent p95
improvement over the corresponding uncorrected compact representation on both
selection and unseen-development families.  Under this corrected rule,
extraction p95 changed from 0.0148845 to 0.0148418, only a
$1.00288\times$ improvement.  Unseen reasoning p95 changed from 0.0143191 to
0.0141196, only a $1.01413\times$ improvement, while its maximum error worsened
from 0.0146089 to 0.0148673.  All absolute limits still passed, but both
material-improvement gates failed.  The result is therefore
\texttt{STOP\_COMPACT\_ERROR\_CORRECTION}; it supplies no full-model speed or
quality promotion.

This falsification narrows the next search: a small global correction of an
already accurate Q3\_K fourth expert does not recover enough additional
accuracy to justify its complexity.  Any successor must either condition the
correction on expert identity and activation region or reduce physical expert
work through a different representation, and must beat its uncorrected compact
baseline rather than merely pass an absolute error threshold.  The corrected
report canonical hash is
\texttt{84efe6d3b69263f186c69820f94b100e3034c8c4014785215ee2d2f086f99a9f};
its stored-file SHA-256 is
\texttt{b0902664505b626b1031cc7c9d53a4e574cde75569bd818b26910f9fa985209f}.
