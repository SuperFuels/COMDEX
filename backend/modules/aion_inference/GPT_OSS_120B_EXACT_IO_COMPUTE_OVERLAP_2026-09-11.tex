\section{Exact Adaptive Expert-I/O and Compute Overlap}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-exact-io-compute-overlap}

\subsection{Objective and claim boundary}

The promoted demand-admitted family cartridge stabilised the internal-disk
expert tier but still delivered approximately 45.13~GB of decoded expert data
during each settled 48-position arithmetic pass.  This experiment tested
whether some of that delivery latency could be hidden behind useful neural
arithmetic without increasing the resident expert footprint.  It did not
reduce expert count, constrain the router, alter gate values, change weights,
or weaken exactness requirements.

The candidate starts independent reads for the four unrestricted experts of a
layer.  As each expert becomes available, the original packed MXFP4 expert
calculation produces its gated contribution.  The four contributions are then
combined with the post-attention residual in the original left-associated
order.  This ordering is required: an algebraically equivalent reassociation
would not satisfy the bitwise exact track.

Splitting a fused four-expert call has a fixed orchestration cost.  The runtime
therefore applies overlap only when at least two of the four selected experts
are absent from the RAM workbench.  Layers with zero or one pending expert use
the unchanged fused calculation.  No speculative expert is read: every fetch
is caused by the current unrestricted 120B route.

\subsection{Narrow real-layer gate and invalidated observations}

Sixteen real SD-backed routes, drawn from two positions across eight layers,
were first evaluated.  The initial isolated six-thread candidate was exact but
improved median route time by only 4.03\%, below the fixed 5\% narrow gate, and
was not promoted.  Exploratory four- and eight-thread gates were accidentally
executed concurrently; their timing results were explicitly invalidated and
retained with \texttt{INVALID} sidecars.

The uncontended eight-thread rerun was exact on all 16 routes.  Median route
time fell from 5.1670 to 4.8970~ms, a 5.51\% improvement.  All 16 route medians
improved; per-route speedups ranged from 1.0298 to 1.0861, with median 1.0562.
This passed the narrow gate and authorised complete-model integration.

\subsection{Balanced full-model protocol}

The integration gate used the original GPT-OSS 120B Q4\_K\_M/MXFP4 warehouse
on the 64~GB SD card, the 18~GB M3 Pro host, the 7.9~GiB arithmetic RAM pool,
the 20~GiB demand-admitted family cartridge, the four-slot reusable route
arena, and all 36 attention/MoE layers.  Each pass comprised 15 teacher-forced
prompt positions followed by 33 genuine greedy continuation positions.

To control background load, cache state and temperature within one process,
five passes used the fixed pattern \texttt{00110}: pass A was a non-overlap
warm-up; passes B and E were controls; passes C and D enabled adaptive overlap.
The comparison was therefore control/candidate/candidate/control after warm-up.
All settled passes delivered exactly 45,129,243,480 decoded L2 bytes, recorded
zero SD fallbacks, and followed identical expert routes.

The median control continuation time was 20.744085~s; the median candidate time
was 19.135475~s.  Thus the balanced end-to-end speedup was 1.08406, or 8.4064\%.
The corresponding median throughputs were 1.59081 and 1.72455~tokens/s.
Adaptive overlap was applied to 937 of 1,188 continuation-layer evaluations
(78.87\%); the remaining 251 used the ordinary fused path.

Every pass produced identical unrestricted routes, generated token IDs, final
hidden-state hashes and step-logit hashes.  The improvement therefore belongs
to the exact track.  It is caused by hiding a portion of required L2 delivery
behind required expert arithmetic, not by reducing model work or expert bytes.

\subsection{Decision and limitations}

Decision: \texttt{PROMOTE\_EXACT\_ADAPTIVE\_L2\_READ\_COMPUTE\_OVERLAP} for
the frozen arithmetic-family gate.  The result clears the unchanged 5\%
complete-loop threshold while retaining the lower-memory cartridge control.

This is an incremental exact breakthrough, not the 30--50~tokens/s endpoint.
The promoted measured rate is 1.72455~tokens/s on this gate.  The evidence does
not yet establish the gain on extraction, reasoning, unseen prompt families,
or longer 128--256-token generation.  It also does not reduce the 45.13~GB of
logical L2 delivery.  The next gate is a blinded second-family replication,
followed by longer generation only if exactness and at least a 5\% paired gain
survive.  A larger RAM hotset remains stopped because it previously reduced
traffic while regressing total speed under memory pressure.
