\section{Residual Compiler Second Gate: Real Teacher Captures and Nonlinear Falsification}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-residual-second-gate}

\subsection{Exact real-prompt teacher collection}

Two training families from the previously frozen cohort were executed through
the unrestricted original four-expert GPT-OSS 120B teacher.  The model weights
remained in the verified SD warehouse.  The run used the bounded 8~GiB memory
cache and existing 20~GiB demand-admitted disk cartridge.  Twelve layers
spanning the 36-layer stack were captured.  Each record contains the
post-attention input, router input, exact layer output and combined MoE
contribution, together with independently calculated expert counterfactuals.

\subsection{Compact nonlinear activation-conditioned cartridge}

A nonlinear candidate was then trained under the explicit authorization.  Its
inputs were fixed signed-bin projections of the live post-attention, router and
top-1 contribution vectors, plus layer identity, routed expert identities, gate
masses and entropy.  Candidate hidden widths and regularization were selected by
holding out an entire training family.  The frozen business and writing blind
families were not opened.

The selected 128-unit model occupied 4,130,965 bytes and evaluated in 0.05325~ms
median.  It nevertheless failed strongly: family-held-out layer-output
relative-$L_2$ error was 0.5344 at median and 1.1083 at p95.  The legacy
calibration/numerical/semantic probes also failed.  The result is labelled
\texttt{STOP\_NONLINEAR\_RESIDUAL}; canonical hash
\texttt{06f93f1823ac451123da26d180211da130e54156519f17a426da4ccb56050a85}.

\subsection{Top-3 bridge}

To determine whether the top-1 target was simply too difficult, the same method
was repeated while retaining the original first three experts and predicting
only the fourth expert's gated contribution.  On the real captures, omitting
expert four alone caused layer-output relative-$L_2$ p95 of 0.10046 for
arithmetic and 0.10442 for extraction.  The derived top-3 dataset contains 696
rows, is 36,921,408 bytes, and has canonical manifest hash
\texttt{ac1176f289065ac6c76496d28c6d4f639bc202cb16719aa15b49fcaab842929e}.

The selected top-3 correction occupied 2,079,893 bytes and evaluated in
0.03900~ms median.  Its whole-family-held-out p95 error was 0.09993.  It therefore
did not materially improve over simply dropping the fourth expert and is also
labelled \texttt{STOP\_NONLINEAR\_RESIDUAL}; canonical hash
\texttt{001b53613ca1d21fb0af7e93867c30630e58b0fda501142ddf7c74acc8c41c53}.

\subsection{Technical conclusion}

The experiments falsify generic activation-only residual prediction at both the
aggressive top-1 boundary and the conservative top-3 boundary.  The cartridges
were easily small and fast enough; their failure was informational, not
computational.  The omitted result depends on the identity-specific nonlinear
function stored in the selected expert weights.  A network that receives only
the activation, route label and a small number of examples cannot reconstruct
an expert it has not learned densely.

The next viable residual compiler must therefore derive its compact correction
from the expert weights themselves---for example, an activation-aware compact
factorization or shared expert basis with small expert-specific coefficients.
It must begin with a single-layer, single-omitted-expert gate, compare against
the exact expert contribution on unseen activations, and advance only when it
reduces both error and physical traffic.  The exact four-expert path remains the
fallback.  No full-generation speed or quality improvement is claimed from this
second gate.
