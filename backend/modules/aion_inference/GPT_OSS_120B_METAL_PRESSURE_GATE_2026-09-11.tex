\section{Fused Metal Route Under Production Memory Pressure}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-metal-pressure-20260911}

The earlier fused four-expert Metal microgate was measured with a hot repeated
route and little surrounding memory pressure.  Because the promoted exact
runtime retains an 8~GiB request-specific expert core, the candidate was
retested while the parent process held a real 8~GiB protected pool reconstructed
from the GPT-OSS 120B SD warehouse.  The frozen extraction activation, original
four expert identifiers, original normalized gates, and original MXFP4 weights
were used.  This remains a changed-arithmetic quality-track experiment because
the Metal result is numerically close, but not bitwise identical, to the CPU
reference.

The pressure gate materialised 8,588,436,480 bytes across 648 protected expert
entries.  Pool construction took 91.886 seconds and the recorded warehouse
receipt reports 8,248,768,440 compressed bytes read.  Under this pressure, the
CPU route median was 1.38742~ms and p95 was 1.52171~ms.  Metal warm compute was
1.87546~ms median and 2.06663~ms p95 after a 3.46379~ms route upload.  Even when
upload was excluded, Metal delivered only 0.7398 times CPU throughput: it was
approximately 26.0\% slower.  Relative $L_2$ output error was 0.0058435, maximum
absolute error was 0.040543, and the output argmax agreed.

A second diagnostic tested whether CPU cache warmth had unfairly favoured the
control.  Before each timed CPU calculation, a separate 256~MiB buffer was
touched to disturb cache residency; the disturbance time itself was excluded.
This is explicitly a synthetic cold-cache proxy, not a generated-token
benchmark.  Metal was conservatively charged both route upload and warm
calculation because a changing unrestricted route requires new weights.  CPU
still completed in 0.80375~ms median (2.31038~ms p95), whereas Metal required
5.20004~ms upload plus 1.17754~ms median compute.  The resulting charged speed
ratio was 0.1260 times CPU.  Numerical error was unchanged.

Decision: \texttt{STOP\_FUSED\_METAL\_ROUTE\_UNDER\_PRESSURE}.  A conventional
per-route GGML Metal graph does not remove the exact 120B bottleneck on this M3
Pro: GPU dispatch is slower than the packed CPU route even after realistic
memory pressure, and changing routes add a much larger upload charge.  This
result closes the tempting but incorrect inference that the hot microbenchmark
alone justified moving expert arithmetic to Metal.  Future Metal work must
first eliminate per-route allocation and copying by binding the existing
resident expert buffers directly into a persistent multi-route arena; otherwise
it must not enter the full generation loop.

The production-pressure and synthetic cold-cache receipts have canonical
SHA-256 values
\texttt{667fb8b4fef78bd2128aebabc6cec167af9fc61004429d517e21092fe7d9ad77}
and
\texttt{9ff415bb76a86eee7842146f72694f553ef7454ed986319f7f213d422a61ee38},
respectively.  Neither result changes the promoted exact frontier of 1.725
tokens/s on arithmetic and 1.829 tokens/s on extraction.

\subsection{Direct host-buffer binding follow-up}

The final permitted Metal question was tested directly rather than left as a
hypothesis.  GGML reports that the M3 Pro Metal device supports buffers created
from host pointers.  A page-aligned 53,100,544-byte arena was therefore built,
the real four-expert route was bound into that arena, and Metal consumed the
host-resident weights without a backend tensor upload.  Measured upload time
fell to approximately 0.00004~ms, proving that the extra copy can be removed.
Host-arena packing was measured separately and excluded from the candidate
compute comparison; a production implementation would need decompression to
land directly in those slots.

The mechanism did not pass the speed gate.  In the hot screen, CPU median was
0.70054~ms and zero-copy Metal median was 0.90877~ms, so Metal achieved only
0.7709 times CPU throughput.  Under the actual 8~GiB protected-pool pressure,
CPU median was 1.15854~ms and zero-copy Metal median was 1.85440~ms, or only
0.6248 times CPU throughput.  Relative $L_2$ error remained 0.0058435 and
argmax agreement remained true, but output was not bitwise identical.

Decision: \texttt{STOP\_ZERO\_COPY\_METAL_ROUTE}.  Direct binding successfully
eliminates route upload, but the M3 Pro Metal MXFP4 graph remains slower than
the packed CPU kernel for this batch-one four-expert workload.  The result
closes the current Metal branch: neither copying nor per-route allocation is
the decisive cause of its loss.  A custom kernel would require new evidence and
must first beat the packed CPU microgate before any full-runtime integration.
The hot and pressure receipt hashes are
\texttt{178dd3f85b777fa2af85cac369d422c71c9c48e1bf9bc0c70e87539578262842}
and
\texttt{55ac3ca806db84a6e2ae5e571626f43f723c508b0e885bf0ce5a9bf2fee17389}.
