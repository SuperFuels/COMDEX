\section{Cross-Family Layer-Consequence Omission Gate}

A second first-principles probe asked whether whole selected experts could be
omitted at a layer only after measuring their actual contribution, rather than
using a global router-mass threshold.  For every captured activation, each of
the four original MXFP4 experts was evaluated separately with its original
gate.  The full four-expert layer result was reconstructed in the original
order, and top-1, top-2 and top-3 partial results were compared with it.

The available frozen arithmetic, extraction and unseen-reasoning corpora
provided 681 real observations at layer 12.  A retained count could be selected
only if that layer met all three predeclared limits across the combined corpus:
p95 output-relative $L_2\leq0.02$, maximum output-relative $L_2\leq0.025$, and
p95 expert-contribution-relative $L_2\leq0.15$.  None of top-1, top-2 or top-3
passed.  Layer 12 therefore retained all four experts, yielding no theoretical
work reduction.  Decision: \texttt{STOP\_LAYER\_CONDITIONED\_OMISSION} for
this measured layer.

This result does not prove that every one of the 36 layers is equally
sensitive, because the current cross-family teacher captures contain only
layer 12.  It does show that the layer already used for residual and compact
expert development cannot be safely reduced by a fixed expert count on the
present broad corpus.  A future multi-layer consequence map must collect a
separately frozen cross-family sample at every layer and must survive causal
generation; this layer-12 result provides no justification to weaken the
full-model gate.

The canonical evidence SHA-256 is
\texttt{ba32b0c70ed67298c114231c2feb6bc74e4c16b97221110a1fe1490691007f31}.
This is an offline changed-arithmetic bound, not generated-token speed or
semantic-quality evidence.  The unrestricted exact frontier remains
approximately 1.72--1.83 tokens/s.

\subsection{Impossible best-subset oracle}

The gate was strengthened to remove a possible objection: perhaps the weakest
expert by router order is not the least consequential expert for the actual
activation.  For each of the 681 observations, an impossible oracle enumerated
every one-, two- and three-expert subset and selected the subset having the
smallest error against the already-known four-expert teacher output.  This
oracle cannot be deployed because it sees the answer before choosing, but it
is a strict existence bound for any future consequence predictor.

Even this oracle retained all four experts for 585 observations.  It could use
three experts for 92 observations, two for four observations, and one for
none.  Total expert work fell by only $1.03811\times$.  This is far below the
predeclared $1.50\times$ threshold for constructing a predictor and cannot
plausibly compound toward the missing speed multiplier.  The stronger result
therefore confirms \texttt{STOP\_LAYER\_CONDITIONED\_OMISSION} at layer 12.
The oracle evidence canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{dbd6237a6f43ac6642df887c10233efd43ecc4cac9f64dd939d25c2919ab1869}.

\subsection{Correction to layer coverage}

The preceding prose incorrectly described the 681 observations as layer 12
only.  Inspection of the hash-bound report confirms that the capture glob
covered 12 sampled layers:
\[
  0,3,6,9,12,15,18,21,24,27,30,35.
\]
Layer 12 contributed 76 observations and each of the other sampled layers
contributed 55.  Every one of the 12 layers retained all four experts under
the fixed cross-family limits.  The impossible best-subset oracle counts
($0$ top-1, $4$ top-2, $92$ top-3 and $585$ top-4 observations) and its
$1.03811\times$ total bound already cover this 12-layer sample.  The conclusion
is therefore stronger than first stated: consequence-selected omission failed
across one third of the transformer depth, sampled from early through final
layers.  The remaining 24 unsampled layers are not inferred.
