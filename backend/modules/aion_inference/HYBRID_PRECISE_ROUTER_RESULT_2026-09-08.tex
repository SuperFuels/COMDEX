\section{Precise Metal Routing and Evidence-Guided Hybrid Stabilisation}
\label{sec:precise-metal-hybrid-router}
\providecommand{\aionreporthash}[2]{%
  \par\noindent\texttt{#1}\texttt{#2}\par}

\subsection{Objective and unchanged gates}

This stage attempted to retain the approximately 38-token-per-second advantage
of the changed Metal routing kernel while correcting the repeatability defect
that had prevented promotion.  The experiment did not lower or reinterpret any
previously declared threshold.  The quality-gated track continued to require:
(i) more than 30 generated tokens per second at 64, 128, and 256 generated
tokens; (ii) no more than a 10\% candidate-rate decrease from 128 to 256 tokens;
(iii) candidate self-repeatability no worse than control at 128 and 256 tokens;
(iv) passage of the frozen teacher-forced and twelve-case semantic gates; and
(v) the existing storage-first and 3~GiB bank-integrity conditions.

This is explicitly a \emph{quality-gated changed-kernel track}.  It is not the
bit-exact track.  The earlier exact INT8 dynamic-bank result remains the exact
reference boundary.  No speed result in this section may be described as
bit-identical to the PyTorch-router control unless the individual evidence says
so.

\subsection{Exact prefill grouping}

The first adjustment retained the original PyTorch router linear operation,
\texttt{topk}, and softmax.  Metal performed only expert counting and stable
grouping.  On 32 synthetic 96-token batches through the real Granite layer-16
router, expert indices, per-expert sizes, and gate values were bit-identical on
every batch.  The first implementation improved the isolated routing-chain p50
by 52.7127\% and p95 by 48.0342\%.  Its canonical report SHA-256 is
\aionreporthash{d9519e1984ca96ff020d703772028e30}{e1816a3d7ec70189d2a6ad0117f1669d}

A second stable grouping kernel assigned one Metal thread per expert and scanned
assignments in original order.  It avoided the first kernel's quadratic
per-assignment prior scan.  At 96 tokens it retained exact indices, sizes, and
gates while improving p50 by 47.9913\% and p95 by 29.8930\%.  At 384 tokens it
retained the same exactness while improving p50 by 24.4187\% and p95 by
14.5377\%.  The corresponding canonical report hashes are
\aionreporthash{2ce054e26b1bf1b1dc976e8975b47d85}{7592f881c78551a18f9967d667c4dc1d}
and
\aionreporthash{047ec6ad6979c188c56f94d9cf7696f5}{26e01f50931e2b40ca3af697b1065a70}

The narrow win did not by itself guarantee a full-model win because each of the
32 layers still has dependencies and host-visible expert-size synchronization.
One full semantic run crossed the unchanged 15\% gate at 19.2307\%, with all
12 answers identical and the same 7/12 score.  Its report hash is
\aionreporthash{13f41e05df8c41654062a7d2f45fe0c}{5f8cd9fd4e534fb7112f439ab0aac0f54}
Later repetitions showed that this short workload has material timing variance;
therefore that single passing run was not used alone to claim final promotion.

\subsection{Stopped exact-normalisation variant}

A conservative decode candidate allowed Metal to identify the eight experts
but returned gate gathering, softmax, and sorting to the original PyTorch path.
It achieved exact expert indices and bit-identical gate values on all 256 narrow
samples.  However, its p50 improvement was only 17.3603\% and its p95 improvement
was 10.8799\%, below the frozen 30\% and 15\% narrow speed gates.  It was stopped
rather than scaled.  Canonical report SHA-256:
\aionreporthash{b5cc021a39732d606672ed4d66bcee14}{3869f69a344e64d7100f829e3f3ea9c8}

\subsection{Precise Metal exponential}

The next adjustment retained the single fast Metal selection kernel but replaced
its default exponential with Metal's precise exponential for gate normalisation.
On 256 real-router activation samples, the candidate selected exactly the same
experts and reduced the measured maximum gate error to zero.  It retained a
76.5380\% p50 routing improvement and an 85.1939\% p95 routing improvement.
This passed its frozen narrow gate.  Canonical report SHA-256:
\aionreporthash{7a1f125c50f689f7407dcda58dc39e84}{5c8356c67c7a25f4c75aceb417f54231}

All-layer precise routing sustained 38.5478, 38.2177, and 37.5013 tokens per
second at 64, 128, and 256 generated tokens respectively.  Nevertheless, the
128-token candidate repeated only seven of eight prompt families.  This result
was preserved and was not promoted.  The failure demonstrated that zero error
on a finite synthetic activation sample was not sufficient evidence of full-run
determinism.

\subsection{Full-run route audit}

A diagnostic-only run compared the original PyTorch route against the precise
Metal route at every decode layer and token for the failing document-governance
case.  Diagnostic synchronization was deliberately included, so its throughput
is not performance evidence.  Across 4,064 layer--token route checks, Metal
selected the correct eight experts in every check.  Only layers 19, 21, and 22
showed any gate-value deviation.  Their maximum absolute errors were respectively
$6.1035\times10^{-5}$, $1.2207\times10^{-4}$, and
$6.1035\times10^{-5}$.  The global maximum was therefore only
$1.2207\times10^{-4}$, but accumulation was sufficient to expose a late greedy
generation branch on a sensitive sequence.  Canonical audit SHA-256:
\aionreporthash{09cc5abf27aa102ac9abfb481ffe4229}{bc25d8ad78aff1993a5e7db304f0b479}

\subsection{Evidence-guided hybrid router}

The final candidate uses the precise Metal router on 29 of 32 layers and retains
the original PyTorch decode router only on measured-sensitive layers 19, 21,
and 22.  Exact prefill grouping remains enabled within its bounded 512-token
capacity.  Thus 90.625\% of decode routers retain the fast path, while the three
observed numerical weak points use the reference path.  No new model weights,
duplicate expert banks, model downloads, or relaxed memory ceilings were added.

The exact document-governance case that had diverged at generated token 49 was
first rerun in isolation.  Both candidate runs then repeated exactly, both
matched the control token stream, and candidate throughput was 41.2012 tokens

The changed kernel does not produce bit-identical logits relative to control.
Maximum recorded step-logit differences were 0.8311, 2.1758, and 40.3184 at
64, 128, and 256 tokens.  Candidate streams matched the reference control in
8/8 candidate comparisons at 64 tokens, 16/16 at 128, and 12/16 at 256.  The
candidate nevertheless repeated its own complete 256-token streams 8/8.  These
facts prohibit an exact-output claim and require the quality-gated label.

\subsection{Frozen semantic gate and current decision}

On the final hybrid mechanism, all twelve frozen multiple-choice answers matched
control, all answers were parseable, and both control and candidate retained the
same 7/12 score.  Candidate throughput was 12.4223 tokens per second versus
11.0708 for the dynamic-bank control, an improvement of 12.2075\%.  This missed
the unchanged short-task threshold of 15\%.  The semantic artifact therefore
records \texttt{NOT\_PROMOTED}; canonical report SHA-256:
\aionreporthash{f34951e361b06f99f7b6a99434648a74}{02568a3b69055563c80a287b2ac453ec}

Accordingly, the honest current decision is:
\begin{quote}
The hybrid precise router is a proven 40-token-per-second long-generation
candidate on the measured 18~GB M3 Pro and SD-backed Granite INT8 bank, with
full candidate self-repeatability on the final 64/128/256 cohorts.  It is not
yet globally promoted because the final frozen short-task semantic run achieved
12.21\% rather than the required 15\% speed improvement, and the changed kernel
is not logit-exact relative to control.
\end{quote}

The next admissible step is a replicated, order-balanced short-task evaluation
or a measured prompt-path optimization that leaves answer-quality gates intact.
The 15\% threshold must not be lowered, the stopped exact-normalisation variant
must not be relabelled, and the 40-token-per-second result must not be generalized
to arbitrary models, hardware, prompt distributions, or cold SD streaming.
