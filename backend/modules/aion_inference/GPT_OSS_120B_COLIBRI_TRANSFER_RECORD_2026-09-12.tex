\subsection{Layer--activation top-one continuation frontier}
\label{subsec:gptoss120b-layer-activation-top1-20260912}

The correct public-reasoning quality-track control used exact unrestricted
prefill, the six-position trajectory-protected 8~GiB workbench, and a
cumulative gate-mass threshold of 0.80 during continuation.  Its previously
recorded quiet-system rates were 7.002 and 5.492 tokens/s (6.247 tokens/s
median), and it explicitly concluded that the red box is heavy.  The present
experiments tested whether a layer-conditioned top-one branch could move this
quality-passing control toward the 28.6174-token/s top-one physical ceiling.
All candidates retained the original GPT-OSS weights and used the existing
unrestricted four-expert prefill.  They are nevertheless changed-model
quality-track candidates because some continuation layers calculated fewer
than four selected experts.

A top-one threshold map previously developed only from arithmetic and
extraction counterfactuals was frozen before its first reasoning-family use.
Combined with the 0.80 fallback, the broad map used one expert for 178 of
1,548 continuation layer calls, two for 15, three for 959, and four for 396.
It reached 8.910 and 9.727 tokens/s (9.319 median), remained internally
bitwise repeatable, and did not trigger the structural loop guard.  However,
the fixed 43-token response window restated the problem without reaching the
answer.  An unchanged 59-token extension produced 4.571 and 9.204 tokens/s
and developed a repeated ``ded ded'' fragment without completing the
conclusion.  The branch is therefore
\texttt{STOPPED\_SEMANTIC\_FAIL}; 9.319 tokens/s is retained as a mechanical
speed observation, not a usable quality result.  The 64-token evidence has

These experiments establish a narrower frontier.  Broad top-one execution can
produce 9-token/s-class mechanical throughput but damages reasoning.  A
layer--activation gate can preserve this reasoning answer, but two admitted
layers do not remove enough physical expert work to improve throughput.  The
next justified controller must certify larger layer--activation regions from
authentic held-out trajectories and select them in small frozen groups.  It
must be evaluated with balanced same-process timing because the present cold
and warm pass spread is large.  Neither a global threshold nor a fixed
safe-layer list is promoted.
\subsection{Purpose and claim boundary}

On 12 September 2026, the official Colibri repository was inspected at commit
\texttt{fd93c41aa6ae2c7d1cc1a1e2d6b79dbe6d341708}.  Colibri is relevant because
it executes sparse mixture-of-experts models whose complete weights remain on
storage.  The audit did \emph{not} treat model capacity as evidence of fast
generation.  Colibri's own published figures distinguish approximately
0.05--0.1 tokens/s on a 25~GB CPU-only development machine, approximately
1.8 tokens/s warm on a 128~GB CPU-only system, and higher rates on multi-GPU
resident configurations.  Consequently, the repository supports the
feasibility of storage-streamed inference, but not a claim that a 744-billion
parameter model provides fast laptop chat without a GPU.

The most materially different exact-output mechanism was Colibri's
cross-layer router pilot.  While layer $L$ performs its expert calculation,
the original router for layer $L+1$ is applied early to layer $L$'s
post-attention residual.  The prediction controls prefetch only.  The
authoritative layer-$L+1$ router still selects the experts and exact demand
loading remains available, so correct implementation changes latency but not
the model function.

Primary source: \url{https://github.com/JustVugg/colibri}; tuning description:
\url{https://github.com/JustVugg/colibri/blob/main/docs/tuning.md}.

\subsection{AION offline transfer gate}

A new AION analyzer applied the same stale-state construction to authentic
GPT-OSS 120B captures.  For every transition from layer $L$ to $L+1$, it:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item loaded layer $L$'s captured post-attention residual;
  \item applied layer $L+1$'s original post-attention RMS-normalization weight;
  \item applied layer $L+1$'s original $128\times2880$ router and bias; and
  \item compared the predicted top-$k$ set with the authoritative top-four
        experts captured after layer $L+1$'s real attention calculation.
\end{enumerate}

The gate covered 420 transitions: six token positions, 35 layer transitions,
and two distinct public prompt families (explanation and business).  Before
measuring lookahead, the extracted original router tensors reproduced the
current-layer authoritative top-four routes on all 420 checks, with zero
mismatches.

\subsection{Interpretation and next gate}

This is a genuine prediction breakthrough, but it is not yet a throughput
breakthrough.  The offline analyzer issued no expert reads and therefore
cannot establish that a predicted read completes before demand, that it avoids
evicting a more valuable expert, or that it does not contend with current-layer
calculation.  No tokens/s claim is made from this gate.

The next controlled implementation shall begin with $k=4$, disable the weakest
layers, use a bounded reservation that cannot displace protected entries, and
cancel or discard work when the authoritative layer catches the pilot.  It
shall retain the original router, gates, weights, reduction order and exact
demand fallback.  Promotion requires bit-for-bit token and step-logit
equivalence, lower demand-wait time, lower end-to-end p50 and p95 latency, no
memory-ceiling violation, and a positive result under both cold-SD and warm-L2
conditions.  If speculative traffic saturates storage or lowers throughput,
the runtime branch shall be stopped even though the prediction result remains
valid.

Other Colibri mechanisms were classified as follows.  Contiguous one-read
experts are already approximated by AION's persistent L2 expert files, but not
by the capacity-constrained compressed SD layout.  Unbuffered I/O is
drive-dependent and requires a separate narrow gate.  Persistent compressed
KV state can improve restart and repeated-conversation latency but does not
remove changing expert traffic.  Corpus and grammar drafting can improve
verified workflow throughput but does not establish faster unrestricted novel
generation.  Indiscriminate parallel reads, larger hot pools, ordinary memory
mapping and additional CPU workers were not reopened because AION has already
falsified those unchanged mechanisms under the present hardware pressure.

\subsection{Live rotating-pipeline experiments}

Three implementations were tested in balanced five-pass order before a
candidate was promoted.  All variants retained bit-for-bit routes, tokens,
hidden states and step logits.

First, a copied four-expert pilot loaded predicted L2 experts into a separate
user-space buffer.  Although prediction remained accurate, the following
layer frequently arrived before four sequential reads completed.  The
candidate moved approximately 3.87~GB of additional data per pass and was
40.6559\% slower than control.  It was stopped.  A copied top-one variant
raised prediction precision to 97.38\% and sharply reduced waste, but remained
6.3468\% slower because it split one item from the efficient four-way demand
batch and retained a synchronization wait.  It was also stopped.

Second, top-one lookahead was changed from a user-space read into macOS
\texttt{F\_RDADVISE}: an asynchronous no-copy advisory request.  The kernel was
told which contiguous L2 expert frame would probably be required, while the
unchanged four-way demand loader remained responsible for acquiring and
verifying the authoritative route.  The isolated hint improved median
arithmetic continuation time by 3.5970\%.  This was positive but below the
unchanged 5\% promotion gate, so it was not promoted independently.

Finally, the no-copy cross-layer hint was composed with AION's already-promoted
same-layer expert-read/compute overlap.  The mechanisms operate at different
boundaries: lookahead starts kernel readahead during layer $L$, while the
same-layer mechanism overlaps any remaining layer-$L+1$ demand reads with
available layer-$L+1$ expert arithmetic.  Neither changes neural mathematics.

\subsection{Top-two advice falsification}

\subsection{Stale-state expert arithmetic with compact delta correction}

The Colibri transfer suggested a more ambitious use of early state: calculate
the fourth selected expert from the preceding layer's already available
post-attention state, then apply a very small learned correction once the true
layer input becomes available.  This changed-model quality-track probe was
restricted to layer 12 and route rank four.  Six public explanation positions
formed the development set; six disjoint public business positions were held
out.  The original expert identity, gate and MXFP4 weights remained in use.
Only the input time and the compact correction differed, and every uncertified
case retained an exact fallback requirement.

The uncorrected early expert produced a held-out p95 layer-output-relative
$L_2$ error of 0.0414661.  A 115,280-byte rank-four shared delta cartridge took
0.00822 ms per held-out row, but worsened p95 error to 0.0443818 and certified
only one of six rows under the fixed 0.02 ceiling.  Coverage was therefore
16.67\%, below the predeclared 50\% requirement.  The branch is classified
\texttt{STOP\_STALE\_DELTA}; it supplies no full-model quality or throughput
claim.

This falsification is physically informative.  The following layer's stale
state is accurate enough to predict most expert identities, but is not accurate
enough to evaluate even the lowest-ranked selected expert and repair its output
with a single cross-expert linear delta map.  Router lookahead should therefore
remain a storage-advice mechanism.  Any future early-arithmetic retry must add
expert/activation-region conditioning or a weight-derived local correction and
must beat the raw stale expert on a blinded family.

\subsection{Tail-aware mixed-Q3 all-layer admission breakthrough}

The failed stale-arithmetic branch returned the search to a weight-derived
representation whose layer-12 numerical gate had previously succeeded but was
too narrow to reduce whole-model traffic.  A new screen retained the router,
all four expert identities, all gates, the first three experts, all biases and
the fourth expert's down projection.  Only the gate and up matrices of the
lowest-gate fourth expert were converted directly from their original MXFP4
weights to padded Q3\_K.  Exact fallback was modelled whenever the fourth gate
exceeded a declared threshold.

A seven-layer pilot passed on 84 observations from disjoint public explanation
and business captures.  The screen was therefore expanded, without changing
the rule, to all 36 layers and all 432 available layer-position observations.
At the 0.20 fourth-gate threshold, 243 of 432 calls (56.25\%) were admitted.
Their p50 and p95 layer-output-relative $L_2$ errors were respectively
0.0057072 and 0.0114821; the maximum was 0.0177109.  Every admitted layer-output
argmax matched the original.  This clears the fixed 0.02 p95 gate across the
complete transformer depth and is classified
\texttt{ADVANCE\_TAIL\_AWARE\_Q3}.

Removing the threshold and compacting all 432 fourth experts remained close
but did not pass the stronger boundary: p95 was 0.0125253, maximum error was
0.0213580, and at least one argmax differed.  The 0.20 fallback rule is
therefore substantive rather than decorative.

An initial six-pass full-transformer arithmetic integration retained all five
frozen teacher continuation tokens in every control and candidate pass.  It
also exposed two implementation costs: on-demand conversion occupied
158.955 seconds and retained 870 converted projections, while the underlying
L2 traffic continued warming from 5.329 GB in the first pass to 0.482 GB in
the last.  Timing from that run is consequently marked
\texttt{PERFORMANCE\_INVALID}; it proves a narrow token-agreement result but no
speed improvement.

The next runtime gate must change the physical path, not merely the arithmetic:
construct a hash-bound split sidecar containing Q3\_K gate/up plus the original
down projection, then demand-load that sidecar instead of first fetching the
complete original fourth-expert frame.  Converted matrices must not accumulate
unboundedly in Python memory.  Only after persistent preconversion, bounded
residency and zero-fallback warm balancing may full-model throughput be judged.

\subsection{Verified mixed-Q3 split-sidecar runtime gate}

The proposed physical implementation was completed rather than inferred from
the all-layer numerical screen.  A hash-bound sidecar was built for 435 real
layer/expert pairs observed in the frozen arithmetic trajectory.  Each entry
contained padded Q3\_K gate and up projections, original MXFP4 down projection,
and the corresponding biases.  The sidecar occupied 5,460,394,920 bytes.  Its
manifest bound the complete file and each component independently; a missing
or corrupt entry caused exact fallback to the original fourth expert.  The
runtime did not compile weights on demand and retained no unbounded converted
projection cache.

A six-pass \texttt{010110} experiment treated the first candidate pass as
warm-up and compared the two settled candidates with the two following
controls.  Every pass generated the same five continuation token identifiers.
The candidates recorded zero sidecar misses, zero integrity failures, zero
on-demand conversion seconds and zero retained converted projections.

\subsection{DFlash speculative-decoding eligibility boundary}

DFlash was audited as a distinct mechanism rather than equated with expert
prefetch.  It uses a separate lightweight block-diffusion model to draft
multiple tokens and asks the target model to verify the block.  The public
GPT-OSS 120B drafter contains approximately 0.8 billion parameters, was trained
for the original GPT-OSS 120B target, and is a separate checkpoint.  Its model
card reports GPT-OSS measurements with SGLang on one H200 GPU.  The general
DFlash paper reports large lossless acceleration on its tested accelerator

These results promote the v2 mechanism only as a bounded structural guard.  A
\texttt{CONTINUE} decision means that no declared loop signature was observed;
it does not mean that the answer is factually correct.  General open-ended
answers still require a critic or stronger-model escalation, and the 40.909\%
figure describes eliminated work on a known failed completion rather than raw
model acceleration.  Blinded prompts and live accepted-answer latency remain
the next required gates.

\subsection{Teacher-forced downstream-consequence bound}

The layer-conditioned reduction work was next evaluated against downstream
token consequences rather than immediate layer error alone.  The broad
cross-family top-one threshold plan was executed on the exact 43-position
continuation history produced by the correct reasoning teacher.  Consequently,
each position was an independent next-token counterfactual: a candidate error
at one position could not contaminate the inputs used to label later positions.
The candidate retained the global 0.80 preserved-mass adaptive rule with a
minimum of two experts, added the broad top-one threshold plan, and did not
preserve the dropped top-one gate mass.  Both passes were internally identical
in routes, final hidden states, logits and token IDs.

The candidate selected the same next token as the correct trajectory at 39 of
43 positions, an agreement fraction of 90.698\%, and differed at four
positions.  Agreement alone did not imply useful acceleration.  On this exact
teacher history the candidate measured 2.7138 tokens/s while the correct
trajectory measured 7.0019 tokens/s.  The changed expert routes disturbed the
protected-pool locality sufficiently that moving and admitting the alternative
experts cost more than the reduced arithmetic saved.

Two optimistic bounds were calculated.  A perfect semantic preclassifier that
used the candidate on every token-equal position and the teacher on every
unsafe position would reach only 2.8734 tokens/s.  A stronger oracle that knew
both correctness and the measured cost of both paths, and could therefore keep
the teacher whenever the nominal shortcut was slower, would reach 7.3775
tokens/s.  The latter is only 5.36\% above the measured teacher and remains far
below the 10 tokens/s quality target.  These are oracle bounds, not deployable
controllers or generated-quality results.

This experiment stops confidence-gated switching between the present broad
top-one candidate and the correct controller as a North-Star path.  It does not
falsify layer--activation consequence mapping itself.  Instead it narrows the
next compiler gate to a three-to-four-expert correction: retain three original
experts, approximate only the gated fourth-expert contribution using a local
layer--expert--activation-region cartridge, and certify candidates by later
route, logit, token and semantic stability.  Storage locality may choose among
already certified alternatives but may not certify an otherwise unsafe
shortcut.  The corrected bound report's canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{0edfb1e926c27e118083f65fcf187294399eb93179b0217738cd1b82d7ea2a3d}.

\subsection{Cross-family fourth-expert recurrence and first local correction}

A correction-coverage corpus was frozen before further fitting.  It contains
36 generic public prompts across arithmetic, extraction, reasoning, business,
explanation and writing.  Twenty-four prompts are assigned to training, six to
selection and six to an unopened holdout split.  With sixteen planned
continuation positions and all 36 layers, the complete plan contains 44,064
layer-position observations.  The emitted artifact stores prompt hashes and
token IDs but no personal or customer data.  Its canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{f8d7d407aa548afddce383888ac81bf66abee6d7aaa0357e72c32972e61646a3}.

The first gate remained deliberately narrow at layer 12.  Independent
arithmetic and extraction training captures produced 37 and 41 authentic
four-expert observations.  Both complete model runs were repeatable in routes,
hidden states, logits and token IDs.  Their canonical SHA-256 values are
\texttt{db8b1b2ef18883f4d5e2b39e90308e280e77a19c137c997caef8ffbb252bcfec}
and
\texttt{4f593ea64aab2cae1d6a1a4fa3ac0271ca39a7c6015d3062ce77e3f92a7104ab}.

Repeated expert identity was common but repeated activation geometry was not.
Eighteen of 41 extraction calls, or 43.902\%, selected a fourth-expert identity
also observed in arithmetic.  At the predeclared joint router/FFN cosine
threshold of 0.80, however, only one extraction call remained, a traffic share
of 2.439\%.  This layer-12/expert-22 pair had joint cosine 0.970179.  Its
unmodified three-expert counterfactual incurred 0.0412152 layer-output
relative-$L_2$ error.  The recurrence report's canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{a63c473bbd53d3ecb43eab86423800b46da11d574f75888462376b98c1b80ede}.

The single qualifying region was then subjected to a true cross-family local
correction gate.  The arithmetic expert-22 contribution at position 25 was
stored as an 11,520-byte prototype and scaled only by the extraction call's
original fourth gate.  On the unseen extraction activation at position 29,
the correction reduced relative-$L_2$ error from 0.0412152 to 0.0166035, a
59.715\% reduction which clears the fixed 0.02 local numerical limit.  Median
prototype calculation time was 0.000583~ms versus 0.1775~ms for the original
expert contribution.  Its storage was 0.0829\% of the declared 13.25~MiB expert

advanced this one region to downstream testing.  Its canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{3250630d5fce37c5d44b85f1b776223b563f7df1a8a25dc9afe0899e4775027c}.

This is the first observed cross-family local $3+\widehat{C}_4$ correction to
clear both declared accuracy and physical-cost gates.  It is not yet a runtime
breakthrough: only one region passed, representing 2.439\% of the measured
extraction traffic, and no downstream router, logit, token, semantic or
full-generation speed result has yet been established.  The next gate must
substitute this correction at its authentic layer and position, roll the model
forward, and compare subsequent routes and output logits with the original
four-expert trajectory.  Additional prompt coverage is justified only if that

\subsection{Single-region downstream roll-forward gate}

The passing layer-12/expert-22 prototype was compiled into a hash-bound NPZ
cartridge containing its layer and expert address, frozen 0.80 joint-cosine
threshold, normalized router and FFN anchor vectors, original training gate and
11,520-byte gated contribution.  The complete in-process runtime was extended
with an isolated quality-track path.  It evaluates the cartridge only when the
unrestricted router selects the addressed expert fourth and the joint activation
similarity passes.  A match loads and calculates the first three original
experts, adds the scaled F32 correction, and avoids loading the original fourth
expert.  Every nonmatch retains the original four-expert path.

The 44-position extraction trajectory was repeated twice.  The cartridge fired
once per pass, at position 29 and layer 12, with joint cosine 0.970179.  The two
candidate passes were internally bitwise repeatable.  Against the separately
captured unrestricted teacher, all 44 generated token IDs and all 44 recorded
second-choice token IDs remained identical.  Thus the local perturbation was
token-stable throughout the measured window.

The internal trajectory was not identical.  The first ordered-router change
appeared at position 29, layer 31, where the same four-expert set placed experts
4 and 85 in the opposite order.  The first different expert set appeared at
position 30, layer 26.  Across the complete trace there were 53 ordered-route
differences, 28 expert-set differences and five top-expert differences.  The
maximum absolute chosen-logit drift was 0.19080 and its p95 was 0.12763; maximum
margin drift was 0.25230.  The final token remained identical and its margin
changed from 2.31447 to 2.33242.

This result is classified as \texttt{TOKEN-STABLE-DOWNSTREAM-DRIFT}.  It
proves that the first compact correction can survive the complete downstream
model without changing any next-token choice in this 44-position extraction
window, but it also proves that the 0.02 local-output gate does not guarantee
router identity.  After the substitution, the candidate's recorded top-two
logit perturbation divided by the teacher's top-one/top-two margin had median
0.06298, p95 0.22704 and maximum 0.23778 at position 42.  The unchanged tokens
therefore retained measurable margin, but the worst position consumed nearly
one quarter of the teacher's decision separation.  The bound downstream
analysis has canonical SHA-256
\texttt{fcede9b07008bdc2220c6e208f70daa9b3c7f979fb48571e049bcccd5559a0b0}.

No speed improvement is claimed: the cartridge covered only
one of 1,584 layer calls per pass and the two complete runs include different
cache histories and instrumentation.  Coverage expansion now requires either a
more accurate local correction or a predeclared bounded downstream certificate,
followed by untouched selection and holdout prompts.

\subsection{Addition-order isolation and frozen affine C4 refinement}

The first local correction integration computed
\(\operatorname{fl}(\operatorname{fl}(x+((E_1+E_2)+E_3))+\widehat C_4)\),
whereas the original graph adds the left-associated expert sum to the residual
only after all four contributions are combined.  These expressions are not
generally identical in floating-point arithmetic.  A real layer-12 quartet
audit therefore substituted the \emph{exact} original fourth contribution,
isolating integration rounding from learned approximation error.

The original integration differed in 838 of 2,880 output elements, with maximum
absolute error \(1.220703125\times10^{-4}\).  A native three-expert graph that
appends the already-gated correction before residual addition matched the
original four-expert graph bit-for-bit in every element.  The active-four
wrapper also matched the unchanged original kernel and the frozen capture.
The audit passed with canonical SHA-256
\texttt{404e5373b8ac4df4fd3af0f91b15fed86b5ea64eacdcf067136c67bf2c1f5a79}.
This removes an integration defect; it does not establish how much of the
previous downstream routing drift arose from that defect, and does not certify
the approximate correction.

A second arithmetic-anchor audit passed all three equivalence checks as well:
the old order differed in 883 elements, maximum absolute error
\(3.0517578125\times10^{-5}\), whereas the corrected reducer differed in zero.
Its canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{c63f803e9f1408282a6c1c61d48bf82d4ed69c580eb6fda5cc29288ea67576e4}.

An explicitly separate refinement fits a rank-one secant to the authentic
arithmetic and previously inspected extraction anchors.  With training
activation \(a\), second fitting activation \(b\), and gated contributions
\(c_a,c_b\) at gates \(g_a,g_b\), define
\[
 d=b-a,\qquad q=d/(d^\top d),\qquad
 \widehat C(x,g)=\frac{g}{g_a}
 \left[c_a+q^\top(x-a)\left(\frac{g_a}{g_b}c_b-c_a\right)\right].
\]
The response and secant payload occupies 46,080 bytes, or 0.332\% of the
declared 13.25 MiB expert.  Router and FFN similarity anchors and cartridge
metadata require additional storage and are charged separately.  The measured
fitting-path median was 0.004625 ms, versus 0.197709 ms for the original E4
calculation in that narrow run.  Neither value is a full-runtime speed claim.
The near-zero error at the second fitting anchor is training reconstruction,
\emph{not} unseen accuracy.  The frozen fitting receipt is classified
\texttt{FROZEN-SECANT-REQUIRES-UNTOUCHED-VALIDATION}, canonical SHA-256
\texttt{2a8db8c0a2554b28ff98d6bb9a7785984f01d491c3310b32c9afb8de173a8574}.

Admission retains the frozen joint cosine threshold 0.80 and additionally
requires the secant coordinate to remain in \([0,1]\), with only a
\(10^{-6}\) endpoint rounding tolerance.  Extrapolation falls back to real E4.
The experiment is isolated from constrained pools, reduced-expert policies,
changed routers and changed output kernels.  Diagnostic captures now retain
hash-bound vectors of all 128 router scores, plus the fourth-versus-fifth
admission margin, at explicitly selected layers and positions.  These scores
support subsequent router-sensitive refinement; they are not a safety proof.

No higher tokens-per-second claim or transferable correction certificate is
made by this fitting and reducer audit.  Exact unrestricted and correct
quality-track speed frontiers remain unchanged pending prospective local,
downstream, sealed-holdout and balanced completed-answer gates.

\paragraph{Continuous, bounded training-coverage collection.}
A family-balanced sequential queue now selects training rows only from the
hash-frozen corpus and captures all 36 layers, never consuming selection or
holdout trajectories.  Before accepting a job it verifies canonical teacher
receipt integrity, exact repeatability, complete observation count and the
router/FFN/output/residual/router-score binary hashes.  An append-only completion
sidecar binds the entire capture file tree to the teacher receipt and corpus.
Verified jobs may be resumed without overwrite; incomplete or invalid jobs stop
the queue for inspection.  Full-model jobs share a nonblocking runtime lock so
independent invocations cannot accidentally overcommit the Mac's memory.
The bounded programme retains the existing six-thread, declared 8 GiB expert
cache and watchdog-governed inference-priority arbitration.  Evidence remains
in local repository results, not on the SD card.  Local disk space was only
approximately 9 GiB, so no new 20 GiB cache allocation is authorized by this
queue launch; a 3 GiB evidence reserve is checked before every job.  Planned
observations and queued jobs are not counted as completed evidence or speed
gains.

Counterfactual teacher captures also retain the exact, already-computed gated
fourth-expert contribution as a separate hash-bound F32 target.  This adds
11,520 bytes per observation without another expert calculation and permits
later correction fitting without refetching the original large E4 frame for
each training observation.  The queue verifies these targets along with the
other captured vectors before sealing a completion sidecar.

When layer 35 is selected for diagnostic capture, the existing full vocabulary
output is retained once per position: 201,088 F32 logits, or 804,352 bytes.
The queue compares each binary hash directly with the teacher receipt's
position-level logit hash.  This requires no additional vocabulary calculation
and enables future all-competitor perturbation checks, rather than treating
the previously recorded winning/runner-up scalar drift as a safety proof.
