\section{GPT-OSS 120B Consolidated Performance Frontier and Falsification Record}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-frontier-consolidation-20260911}

\subsection{Scope and claim boundary}

This section consolidates the experiments completed after promotion of the
exact unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B SD-backed runtime.  Every exact-track test used
the existing 18~GB M3 Pro, the mounted AION 120B SD warehouse, the original
Q4\_K\_M/MXFP4 weights, the unrestricted 128-expert router, four selected
experts per layer, all 36 transformer layers, and authoritative fallback to the
verified SD representation.  Candidate mechanisms were promoted only when the
declared numerical, memory, integrity, and transfer gates passed.  Changed
arithmetic and reduced-expert experiments remain separately labelled quality
tracks and are not represented as bit-exact unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B.

The promoted exact runtime retains an 8~GiB protected request-specific expert
core, a demand-admitted 20~GiB internal-disk L2 cartridge, three-touch exception
admission, and exact read/compute overlap.  Its current verified short-window
frontier is 1.72455 tokens/s on arithmetic and 1.82858 tokens/s on extraction,
equivalent to approximately 0.58 and 0.55 seconds per generated token.  The
original unrestricted SD-streamed path was approximately 0.07--0.10 tokens/s;
the promoted architecture therefore represents an approximately 18--24-fold
improvement while retaining exact token, final-hidden-state, and step-logit
equivalence.  The 30-token/s North Star remains 16.41 times above the present
1.82858-token/s frontier and has not been achieved.

\subsection{Activation-sparse expert-column gate}

Activation sparsity was tested as a changed-model traffic-reduction mechanism
on sixteen frozen real expert activations.  The unrestricted router, selected
experts, original residual, original packed MXFP4 weights, and dense down
projection were retained.  Gate/up input coordinates were preserved according
to cumulative absolute activation mass, and the resulting expert contribution
and complete layer output were compared with the original calculation.

At 75\% retained activation mass, the projected weight-column traffic reduction
was 1.60 times, but p95 relative $L_2$ error reached 0.0991 for layer output and
0.3203 for expert contribution.  At 90\% retained mass, the corresponding
errors fell to 0.0401 and 0.1349, but projected traffic reduction fell to only
1.3187 times.  Retaining 99\% of activation mass passed the numerical gates,
but yielded only approximately 1.0842 times projected traffic reduction.
Consequently no sparse kernel or compact column warehouse was constructed.
Decision: \texttt{STOP\_ACTIVATION\_SPARSE\_COLUMNS}.  The evidence was
preserved in isolated commit \texttt{c9c0c274}.

\subsection{Protected-core shrink and cross-family transfer}

The inverse memory-pressure hypothesis was then tested: reduce the protected
core from 8~GiB to 6~GiB, freeing unified memory for packed calculation while
accepting additional exact L2 delivery.  On matched warm 24-position arithmetic
runs, the 6~GiB candidate reached 1.35598 tokens/s versus 1.27873 tokens/s for
the 8~GiB control, a 6.04\% improvement.  However, its median L2 delivery rose
from 15.441~GB to 20.146~GB per pass, an increase of 30.47\%.  Routes, tokens,
final hidden states, and logits remained bitwise equivalent.

The unchanged candidate was transferred to extraction using independently
fresh, identically constructed temporary L2 cartridges.  There the result
reversed: 6~GiB reached only 0.19713 tokens/s versus 0.26809 tokens/s for 8~GiB,
a 26.47\% regression.  Median L2/SD delivery rose to 31.418/6.138~GB from
24.877/2.893~GB.  Exactness again passed, proving that the failure was physical
performance rather than correctness.  Decision:
\texttt{STOP\_CORE\_SHRINK\_NOT\_GENERAL}.  The 8~GiB protected core remains
the cross-family control.  Reproducible experiment caches were removed only
after evidence capture, recovering approximately 30~GiB of local storage; no
evidence was deleted.  The result, checksums, and transfer record were preserved
in commit \texttt{6fa61028}.

\subsection{Fused Metal under production memory pressure}

The packed four-expert Metal graph was retested while the parent process held a
real 8~GiB protected pool containing 8,588,436,480 resident bytes across 648
protected expert entries.  The frozen extraction activation, original route,
original gates, and original weights were retained.  Pool construction took
91.886 seconds and read 8,248,768,440 compressed warehouse bytes.

Under this pressure, packed CPU execution measured 1.38742~ms median and
1.52171~ms p95.  Metal measured 1.87546~ms median and 2.06663~ms p95 after a
3.46379~ms route upload.  Even excluding upload, Metal achieved only 0.7398
times CPU throughput and was approximately 26\% slower.  Relative $L_2$ output
error was 0.0058435, maximum absolute error was 0.040543, and output argmax
agreed, but the output was not bitwise identical.  A separately labelled
256~MiB synthetic CPU cache-disturbance diagnostic also failed to justify Metal
once route upload was charged.  Decision:
\texttt{STOP\_FUSED\_METAL\_ROUTE\_UNDER\_PRESSURE}.  The evidence was
preserved in commit \texttt{cc1fccc4}.

\subsection{Direct zero-copy Metal binding}

The remaining Metal hypothesis was that per-route copying, rather than compute,
caused the loss.  GGML reported host-pointer buffer support on the M3 Pro.  A
page-aligned 53,100,544-byte host arena was therefore constructed and bound
directly to the Metal backend.  This worked mechanically: measured backend
upload fell to approximately 0.00004~ms.  Host-arena packing was recorded
separately and excluded from compute timing, because a production version would
require verified decompression to land directly in persistent slots.

Zero-copy did not pass the performance gate.  In the hot screen, CPU median was
0.70054~ms and Metal median was 0.90877~ms, giving Metal 0.7709 times CPU
throughput.  Under the actual 8~GiB protected-pool pressure, CPU median was
1.15854~ms and zero-copy Metal median was 1.85440~ms, giving Metal only 0.6248
times CPU throughput.  Relative $L_2$ error remained 0.0058435 and argmax
agreement remained true, but bitwise equivalence did not hold.  This proves
that route upload can be removed, but also proves that upload was not the
decisive reason the batch-one MXFP4 Metal route lost.  Decision:
\texttt{STOP\_ZERO\_COPY\_METAL\_ROUTE}.  The current GGML Metal branch is
closed unless a genuinely different custom kernel first beats packed CPU in a
narrow gate.  Evidence and LaTeX were preserved in commit \texttt{887817f8}.

\subsection{Adaptive expert count plus multi-position reuse bound}

The best quality-passing preserved-mass adaptive traces were next combined
offline with the previously exact multi-position expert-union mechanism.  For
every non-overlapping response window, the analysis counted individual active
expert demands and the unique layer--expert union that could be fetched once
for a known teacher block.  This deliberately measured the best physical reuse
opportunity before constructing a costly speculative runtime.

For arithmetic, median expert-delivery reductions were 1.1169, 1.3605, and
1.6718 times for two-, four-, and eight-position blocks.  Extraction produced
1.1494, 1.3484, and 1.6047 times.  The best individual window reached 1.9943
times.  Under the intentionally over-generous assumption that all exact runtime
time scales linearly with expert delivery, the 1.82858-token/s frontier would
rise only to 3.6467 tokens/s.  Real improvement must be lower because attention,
routing, output calculation, draft generation, verification, rejection, and
recovery remain.

The available top-two arithmetic draft and preserved-mass target shared only
their first five greedy response tokens before diverging.  This is not a corpus
acceptance rate, but it confirms that teacher tokens cannot be treated as free
and that autoregressive causality remains.  Decision:
\texttt{DO\_NOT\_BUILD\_FULL\_COMPOSITE\_FROM\_ROUTE\_REUSE\_ALONE}.
Multi-position reuse remains a supporting optimization for any future cheap,
high-acceptance compatible draft, but cannot independently close the 16.41-fold
North-Star gap.  The canonical evidence SHA-256 is
\texttt{aace38fa5d0bb09ef94549aa0f89a49171843490cde855f8adc2ea1aa59a1aba},
and the implementation record is commit \texttt{e940cf71}.

\subsection{Current physical interpretation}

The experiment has established that the complete 120B representation can
reside in an addressable, verified SD warehouse and execute on a machine whose
18~GB unified memory cannot conventionally contain the model.  The promoted
runtime is exact and dramatically faster than the original SD-streamed path.
However, it has also established that the current system remains dominated by
movement of the selected expert data through the memory hierarchy.  Neither
more RAM residency, less RAM residency, ordinary Metal offload, removal of the
Metal upload copy, activation-column omission, nor teacher-block reuse provides
the missing order-of-magnitude improvement under the tested constraints.

For completeness, component-pipelined expert execution had already been tested
before this consolidation.  That exact candidate read and calculated gate/up
components first while fetching the down projection concurrently.  Across
eight captured four-expert routes and 48 balanced observations per mode, every
output remained bitwise identical.  Whole-expert overlap required 4.9073~ms
median per route, whereas staged projection required 7.3853~ms, giving a
candidate/control speed ratio of only 0.6645.  Extra file operations, graph
execution, and synchronization exceeded the short gate/up interval available
for hiding the down read.  Decision:
\texttt{STOP\_STAGED\_PROJECTION\_IO}.  This branch is preserved in commit
\texttt{962e2c35} and must not be repeated without a fundamentally different
single-dispatch implementation.

The resulting frontier changes the next research abstraction.  The exact
engine remains the oracle and fallback, while the primary order-of-magnitude
candidate becomes an explicitly quality-gated transformed expert
representation.  Its first gate must operate on genuine original matrices and
unseen real activations, require no more than 20\% of the original physical
expert traffic, and measure complete expert and layer-output error.  It must not
repeat previously stopped magnitude pruning, activation-column omission,
global residual dictionaries, simple low-rank output imitation, or fixed
lower-bit MXFP4 codebooks.  No full-model implementation is justified until a
multi-family microgate demonstrates both the required traffic reduction and a
predeclared numerical acceptance region with exact fallback for rejected
states.

\subsection{Consolidated conclusion}

The honest verified GPT-OSS 120B frontier on the existing 18~GB M3 Pro is
therefore 1.72455--1.82858 tokens/s.  AION has proved viable bounded execution
of the SD-resident 120B-class model and delivered an approximately 18--24-fold
improvement over the original unrestricted SD path.  It has not yet produced a
30--50-token/s unrestricted 120B runtime.  The principal remaining requirement
is a mechanism that reduces or eliminates most physical active-expert movement,
not another small adjustment to eviction policy or conventional GPU dispatch.
