\section{First Router-Driven Four-Expert 120B Route}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-router-driven-route}

The first multi-expert execution gate used the real layer-0
\texttt{ffn\_gate\_inp} weights and bias from the verified GPT-OSS 120B
warehouse.  A deterministic 2,880-element hidden activation was evaluated by
the 128-way router.  Its top-four route was experts 25, 112, 32 and 127, with
normalised gates 0.26327852, 0.25436795, 0.24231303 and 0.24004049.
This deliberately non-adjacent route prevents the result from depending on a
convenient contiguous expert selection.

AION demanded the six components of all four experts from the compressed SD
warehouse.  The route represented 53,015,040 raw bytes and required
50,506,171 compressed bytes.  Initial sequential demand loading took
0.672565~s under a 256~MiB cache ceiling, with four faults, no eviction and
exact verification of every compressed and decoded frame.

The four packed MXFP4 expert networks were evaluated and combined with their
router gates.  Two independent eight-repetition runs produced the same finite
checksum, 88.9221.  Their resident-compute p50 values were 1.28996~ms and
0.959583~ms.  This passes the first real router-driven four-expert computation
gate.  It excludes attention, residual and normalisation operations and is
therefore not yet a complete transformer layer or token.  The canonical
evidence SHA-256 is
\texttt{98ab3e9b23679fa5e635e8fa10462cc6}%
\allowbreak\texttt{fa31da2c26f3d02fa2a94903ba8a9d2a}.

\subsection{Bounded parallel-loading candidate}

The exact selected route was subsequently tested in a rotating sequential /
parallel / parallel / sequential order.  Both conditions requested macOS
\texttt{F\_NOCACHE}; this reduces ordinary caching effects but does not prove
that all pages were absent from every operating-system cache.  Four bounded
workers loaded and decoded the 24 independent component frames.  All outputs
were hash-identical across all 16 trials.

Sequential loading measured 0.683740~s p50 and 0.692560~s p95.  Parallel
loading measured 0.553441~s p50 and 0.557982~s p95, a p50 improvement of
19.0568\%.  Because the candidate passed its integrity and timing gate, a
bounded parallel route-fault method was integrated into the expert store.
The integrated implementation reproduced the route in 0.552086~s and remained
within the declared 256~MiB cache ceiling.  Six focused store/reader tests
passed 6/6.  The mechanism evidence canonical SHA-256 is
\texttt{660a27b4cca4a78ef3840515dae778b71}%
\allowbreak\texttt{c9e6318dd8f15076e0ca867a39b967c}.

The result proves a real reduction in exact expert-route preparation, not an
end-to-end token improvement.  Initial route preparation is still more than
500 times slower than the approximately 1~ms resident four-expert arithmetic.
The next gate must add the real layer-0 attention, normalisation and residual
tensors and execute one complete transformer block before any token claim.
