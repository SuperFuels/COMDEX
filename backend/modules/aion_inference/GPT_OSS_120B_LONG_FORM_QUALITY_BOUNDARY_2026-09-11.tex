\section{Long-Form Quality Boundary and the Route to the Physical Ceiling}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-long-quality-boundary}

\subsection{Purpose and claim discipline}

This section records the attempt to approach the measured fixed-top-1 physical
ceiling of $28.6174$ generated tokens per second while retaining the useful
behaviour of the unrestricted GPT-OSS 120B model.  Three quantities must remain
separate:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item the unrestricted exact track, which evaluates all four router-selected
        experts using the original weights and gates;
  \item reduced-expert quality candidates, which intentionally change the
        neural calculation and therefore require independent semantic gates;
  \item the fixed-top-1 physical ceiling, which measures achievable execution
        rate but has already failed quality.
\end{enumerate}

The $28.6174$ tokens/s observation therefore proves that the surrounding host,
attention, router, vocabulary projection, and resident-expert machinery can
operate near the 30 tokens/s target when only one expert is calculated.  It does
\emph{not} prove that a useful GPT-OSS 120B completion can currently be produced
at that rate.

\subsection{Direct packed MXFP4 Metal experiment}

A new direct Metal path was implemented for a real four-expert route.  The
17-byte GGUF MXFP4 blocks were losslessly separated into their scale and value
streams, then consumed without first expanding the weights to floating-point
matrices.  One Metal command buffer fused the eight gate/up projections,
SwiGLU, four down projections, and gate-weighted residual combination.

Under an equal 512 MiB cache-displacement charge, the candidate achieved a
speed ratio of only $0.6060\times$ relative to the hot CPU route---that is, it
was approximately $1.65\times$ slower---and its output relative $L_2$ error was
$0.01346$.  The declared gates required at least $1.15\times$ speed and at most
$0.01$ relative error.  The branch is consequently preserved as
\texttt{NOT\_PROMOTED}.  This falsifies the proposition that repacking alone,
followed by an ordinary fused Metal dispatch, removes the dominant four-expert
cost on this M3 Pro.

\subsection{Layer-conditioned reduced-expert probes}

Development traces from arithmetic and extraction prompts were used to test
whether expert reduction could be confined to apparently insensitive layers.
The runtime gained two controlled mechanisms: a per-layer top-1 confidence-gap
plan and a temporal top-1/full-policy cycle.  All tests retained exact
unrestricted prefill; only continuation expert calculation was changed.

The temporal experiment is especially informative.  Periodically inserting a
fuller ``teacher'' step did not repair the preceding approximate hidden state
and KV history.  Once an approximate token changes the causal path, later exact
expert evaluation cannot retroactively restore the original trajectory.

\subsection{Matched-length correction to the earlier quality claim}

The preserved-mass $0.75$ candidate had appeared correct in a shorter window.
On the matched 96-position test it produced 23 exact continuation tokens and
then diverged, eventually entering a repeated ``Actually'' pattern.  Therefore
the earlier short-window observation remains valid as a narrow result but is
withdrawn as evidence of general long-form quality.  This is a test-depth
correction, not a numerical failure hidden by a changed acceptance threshold.

\subsection{Authoritative unrestricted teacher}

The unrestricted four-expert model was then run twice through the same
96-position protocol using the verified SD warehouse, 20 GiB persistent L2
cartridge, and bounded 8 GiB memory workbench.  Expert routes, final hidden
states, logits, and token IDs repeated bit-for-bit across both passes.  The
completion correctly identified invoice \texttt{A-104}, calculated
\texttt{EUR 287.50}, and began the requested JSON output.

The corresponding long-generation rate was only $0.1766$ and $0.1892$
settled tokens/s.  This supersedes neither of the previously measured exact
short-window rates of $1.72455$ and $1.82858$ tokens/s; it establishes that
those short-window rates do not generalize to this changing 70-token route.

\subsection{What is now proved}

The experiment narrows the problem sharply:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Mac runtime has a measured near-30 tokens/s physical path when one
        resident expert is used per layer.
  \item A reduced-expert path can traverse the full 120B backbone at more than
        12 tokens/s, but the tested policies do not preserve long-form quality.
  \item The unrestricted model remains coherent and bitwise repeatable, but a
        changing long route can require roughly 35--37 GiB of compressed SD
        traffic per pass and only reaches roughly 0.18 tokens/s.
  \item A local confidence score, a layer label, or an occasional full step is
        insufficient to predict whether omitted expert contributions will alter
        the later causal trajectory.
\end{itemize}

It is consequently not honest to describe the present system as a usable
120B model at either 12 or 28 tokens/s.  The quality-preserving frontier and
the physical ceiling are both real, but they have not yet been joined.

\subsection{Teacher-forced confidence-fallback bound}

A pre-commit confidence gate was then tested as a possible bridge.  The
preserved-mass $0.75$ candidate was evaluated at every continuation position
while being forced to consume the exact teacher's preceding tokens.  This
removed autoregressive contamination: every comparison asked independently
whether the reduced-expert model would choose the same next token from the
correct causal history.

The candidate agreed at 51 of 70 positions, or 72.86\%, and disagreed at 19.
Its teacher-forced traversal itself measured 11.093 tokens/s, compared with
0.17697 tokens/s for the exact teacher over the same positions.  However, even
an impossible perfect classifier that knew all unsafe positions before
calculation would reach only 0.6525 tokens/s when measured candidate and exact
position costs were composed.  A post-candidate oracle would reach 0.6444
tokens/s.

Output margin did not cleanly separate safe and unsafe decisions.  Catching
all 19 unsafe positions required rejecting every candidate whose top-two logit
margin was at most 1.4021.  That rule rejected 34 of 70 positions, including 15
safe ones, and its optimistic measured upper bound was only 0.3574 tokens/s.
Decision: \texttt{STOP\_CONFIDENCE\_FALLBACK\_AS\_NORTH\_STAR\_PATH}.  A
confidence gate may still be a useful integrity mechanism, but it cannot
approach the 28-token/s ceiling while exact fallback remains this expensive.
The canonical evidence digest is
\texttt{b74277faa908c958e65eb866ae8e82c28d78324fff20cee1bc6b148d95a27c2f}.

\subsection{Required next architecture}

Closing the gap now requires reducing the physical work while restoring the
information carried by experts two through four.  The next candidate should be
a \emph{verified residual expert compiler}: one normal resident expert supplies
the main path, while a compact, directly computed correction represents only
the consequential contribution of the omitted three experts.  A confidence
gate must fall back to the unrestricted four-expert calculation whenever the
correction cannot satisfy a frozen error bound.

This is deliberately different from simply dropping experts.  It attempts to
preserve their effect with much less weight traffic.  It must advance in three
steps: real-layer output error, teacher-logit agreement over causal blocks, and
only then blinded long-form semantic evaluation.  Any trained or injected
neural correction remains a separately authorized quality track; no such
training is claimed in this record.
