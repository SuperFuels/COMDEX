\section{Exact Demand-Admitted Family Cartridges for GPT-OSS 120B}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-exact-family-cartridges}

\subsection{Problem and claim boundary}

The preceding 20~GiB persistent-L2 experiment promoted a task-aligned
arithmetic session but falsified rapid mixed-family switching: extraction,
when executed after arithmetic, incurred 690, 230 and 51 SD fallbacks over
three passes.  This section records the next exact architecture.  It changes
only which verified internal-disk expert frames are protected from eviction.
It does not constrain the router, change top-$k$, alter gates, replace weights,
reduce active experts, quantize further, or modify any neural calculation.
An absent expert is still fetched and hash-verified from the authoritative SD
warehouse.  Consequently, a wrong family prediction can reduce performance
but cannot change the model result.

Only two complete semantic route families were available: arithmetic and
invoice extraction.  Six additional traces were synthetic route-diversity
stress probes.  They are not described as six further business or semantic
families.  The result is therefore a measured two-family exact result, not a
general multi-domain production claim.

\subsection{Offline working-set analysis}

The analyzer removed each semantic family's separately protected 7.9--8~GiB
RAM pool, then examined the residual unrestricted L2 demand.  Arithmetic used
1,283 distinct residual layer-experts occupying 15.8367~GiB; extraction used
1,315 occupying 16.2317~GiB.  Their residual intersection contained 667
layer-experts occupying 8.2331~GiB, while their combined residual union occupied
23.8354~GiB.  The union over both semantic traces and all six stress traces was
31.5994~GiB.  These measurements explain why one undifferentiated 20~GiB LRU
could thrash after a family change.

Candidate layouts at 20, 25 and 30~GiB were evaluated.  The selected bounded
layout was:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Component & Decoded capacity \\
\midrule
Semantic-prioritised shared core & 10~GiB \\
Active family segment & up to approximately 8~GiB \\
Unprotected exception halo & 2~GiB \\
Total internal-disk L2 ceiling & 20~GiB \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This configuration covered 100\% of observed residual accesses in both
semantic traces and all six stress traces in offline replay.  That figure is a
trace-coverage result, not unseen-family proof and not a runtime speed result.
The plan records exact layer/expert addresses, decoded and compressed byte
counts, the source warehouse-manifest hash, every source-trace hash and a
canonical SHA-256.

An eager switch would have staged 14.0134~GiB of compressed SD data for
arithmetic and 14.4524~GiB for extraction after including the active RAM pool.
The implementation therefore rejected eager cartridge loading.  It protects a
planned expert only after unrestricted inference genuinely requests it.  This
``demand admission'' preserves the shared core and family segment while
leaving the 2~GiB halo available for exact exceptions.

\subsection{Live alternating-family gate}

The live gate used the original GPT-OSS 120B Q4\_K\_M/MXFP4 warehouse on the
64~GB SD card, the 18~GB M3 Pro host, an 8~GiB-class protected RAM pool, the
four-slot reusable route arena and the same 20~GiB internal-disk L2.  Each
family executed 48 positions in three passes.  Arithmetic was run from an
extraction-shaped cache; extraction was then run from the resulting
arithmetic-shaped cache.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Family/pass & Continuation t/s & p50 latency (s) & p95 latency (s) & SD fallbacks \\
\midrule
Arithmetic switch pass & 0.50019 & 1.9251 & 3.9907 & 311 \\
Arithmetic settled pass 2 & 1.54384 & 0.6609 & 0.7797 & 0 \\
Arithmetic settled pass 3 & 1.69828 & 0.5822 & 0.7323 & 0 \\
Extraction switch pass & 1.24913 & 0.7786 & 0.9306 & 311 \\
Extraction settled pass 2 & 1.57676 & 0.6354 & 0.7543 & 0 \\
Extraction settled pass 3 & 1.61736 & 0.6235 & 0.7332 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Each hostile switch read approximately 3.67~GiB of compressed expert data on
demand, roughly 74\% less than the corresponding eager-switch estimate.  Both
families then reached zero SD fallbacks and zero L2 evictions on the two
settled passes.  The settled medians were 1.62106~t/s for arithmetic and
1.59706~t/s for extraction.  Arithmetic improved 1.69\% over its preceding
1.59411~t/s task-aligned arena median.  Extraction improved 13.24\% over the
stable 1.41036~t/s verified bulk-copy control and 86.33\% over the preceding
0.85713~t/s settled median from the thrashing mixed-family reusable-arena run.

All six live passes retained repeatable token IDs, complete unrestricted
four-expert routes, layer outputs, final hidden states and logits.  The
runtime's family-cartridge receipt explicitly records that eager prefetch was
disabled.  The focused cache and analysis suite passed 19/19 tests, including
a test proving that a protected exact cartridge entry cannot be displaced by
an unprotected halo admission.

\subsection{Stopped RAM-hotset extension}

The settled profile assigned 43--48\% of continuation time to internal expert
delivery, 32--34\% to native expert calculation, 16--19\% to attention and
approximately 1.6\% to output.  Offline replay predicted that a 1.5~GiB
family-specific RAM hotset could retain 121 repeatedly used arithmetic experts
and avoid 10.5537~GiB of later decoded L2 traffic.  A capacity-only experiment
was first invalidated as an optimisation: the reusable arena intentionally
kept transient misses out of durable L1, so resident expert memory remained
7.8999~GiB and settled throughput regressed 2.66\%.

A second implementation explicitly copied only those 121 hash-bound hot
experts into durable L1 on their first genuine unrestricted use.  It behaved
as designed: resident expert memory reached 9.3934~GiB, all 121 experts were
admitted once, and settled L2 delivery fell from approximately 42.03 to
29.98~GiB per pass, a 28.67\% reduction.  Nevertheless, arithmetic settled at
only 1.35679 and 1.28251~t/s, median 1.31965~t/s.  This was 18.59\% below the
1.62106~t/s cartridge control.  Native expert-finish time rose to 11.41 and
12.67~s from roughly 6--7~s in the lower-pressure control.  The added residency
therefore reduced weight delivery but created enough memory/cache pressure to
make calculation materially slower.  Exact tokens, routes, hidden states and
logits still passed.  Decision:
\texttt{NOT\_PROMOTED\_STOP\_L1\_HOTSET\_ON\_18GB\_HOST}.

\subsection{Interpretation and next gate}

This is a genuine cross-family cache-stability breakthrough, not the requested
30--50~t/s endpoint.  It converts repeated mixed-family LRU churn into one
bounded on-demand transition followed by stable zero-SD-fallback execution.
It also shows that the family cartridge should be a retention policy, not a
large speculative SD read.

The remaining settled ceiling is dominated by repeatedly delivering decoded
expert bytes from internal storage into the reusable route arena and by native
expert/attention computation.  The next speed experiment must profile those
two costs under the now-stable cartridge.  A fused packed expert kernel should
advance only if it wins on real four-expert routes; compact or reduced-expert
representations must remain in a separately labelled quality track with a
frozen semantic evaluation.  No exact-track claim may extrapolate trace
coverage to unseen families.

The RAM-hotset falsification narrows that next gate further: it is not enough
to reduce bytes if doing so increases the resident working set.  The next
exact experiment should retain the promoted memory footprint and test a
single-pass fused native four-expert kernel or a lower-copy internal-delivery
path.  Both delivery bytes and native compute time must improve together.
