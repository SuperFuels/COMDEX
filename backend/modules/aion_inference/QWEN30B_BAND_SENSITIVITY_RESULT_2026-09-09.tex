\section{Qwen3-30B-A3B Expert-Precision Band Sensitivity}
\label{sec:qwen30b-band-sensitivity}

\subsection{Purpose and predeclaration}

The unsuccessful edge-layer heuristic in
Section~\ref{sec:qwen30b-q2q3-edge} showed that mixed precision could improve
one quality instrument while regressing another.  A second experiment therefore
screened the 48 transformer layers as six independent eight-layer bands.  In
each candidate, exactly one band's gate, up, and down expert tensors used
Q3\_K; the remaining expert tensors used Q2\_K; and non-expert tensors retained
the Q4\_K\_M base policy.

The plan, band order, gates, and selection rule were fixed before screening.
The plan's SHA-256 is
\texttt{90ddbde0c57add79285989d2d7b34562d5e8d4b23f719d9b00a9b9b08f3b5e7c}.
A band was sensitivity-positive only if it achieved at least 7/8 strict and
7/8 semantic on the frozen structured cohort, 8/8 parseable and repeatable,
at least 11/12 on the frozen multiple-choice cohort, full Metal residency, and
at least 50 tokens/s on the smoke test.  Gates were not changed after results
were observed.

B0, B1, B2, and B5 produced parseable, repeated structured outputs.  B0's
narrow smoke rate was 59.10 generated tokens/s.  B2 and B5 improved the
structured instrument to 7/8 strict and 8/8 semantic, but they regressed the
multiple-choice control to 10/12 and 9/12 respectively.  B1 preserved the Q2
control's 11/12 multiple-choice result but failed to improve the structured
strict score.  Thus no tested band passed both quality instruments.

B3 and B4 encountered Metal command-buffer out-of-memory errors during model
initialisation.  B4 failed repeatedly, including with one service slot, a
512-token context, batch 128, and microbatch 64.  These failures are valid for
the measured conditions, but the ordered screen was itself memory-intensive;
temporal memory pressure is therefore a confound and the failures must not be
attributed solely to layer position.

\subsection{Decision}

No band was promoted and no combined mixed-precision model was authorised.
The result demonstrates that expert precision has non-monotonic,
task-dependent effects: the same allocation can repair structured behaviour
while damaging reasoning questions.  Combining individually failing bands
would not be sensitivity-guided; it would be a new speculative search.

The proven selective-Q2 candidate consequently remains the resident model.
Further changed-weight work should use finer tensor-family or per-layer
sensitivity only if it can be screened inside the physical memory boundary.
Otherwise, the higher-value path is system-level AION routing, verified
AtomSheets, and cold-start reduction, whose gains do not depend on pretending
that a small frozen quality set proves general capability.
