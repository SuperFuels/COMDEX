\section{Balanced Direct-I/O Startup Replication}
\label{sec:qwen30b-dio-abba}

The initial direct-I/O result was followed by a predeclared balanced
auto/DIO/DIO/auto replication.  Before every arm, the complete preserved
17.28~GiB Q4 control was read sequentially from SD to \texttt{/dev/null}; the
four displacement passes each required approximately 194.1~s.  Every server
used the same Q2-expert model, all 48 Metal layers, context 4096, four slots,
eight orchestration threads, poll 50, and automatic attention.

Median readiness was 226.543~s for automatic mmap and 116.873~s for direct
I/O.  Direct I/O therefore reduced median readiness by 48.410\% and was
1.9384 times as fast.  Both DIO arms were faster than both automatic arms, so
the direction survived the balanced order.  All four arms became healthy,
none reported Metal out-of-memory, and every completion produced identical
text and an identical top-10 log-probability payload with canonical SHA-256
\texttt{1fe300c9fb23f5314920617a1a61980a8d27934aa4bc860752f3300ff445919b}.

The audit also caught a predeclared-gate defect in the experimental design.
The plan required \emph{every arm}, including the control arms, to deliver at
least 50 tokens/s on the first post-ready probe.  Both automatic arms delivered
approximately 24 tokens/s, whereas both DIO arms exceeded 58 tokens/s.  The
literal all-arm gate therefore failed and was not relaxed.  Formal promotion
under this specific ABBA plan is withheld even though the DIO mechanism and
direction replicated cleanly.

The control failure is itself informative: mmap reported service readiness
while first-use page work apparently remained, whereas DIO provided both
earlier readiness and immediately fast inference.  This is an inference from
the measurements, not yet a proven causal decomposition.  The next experiment
must separately predeclare process-start-to-first-response latency and
second-response warm performance, with candidate acceptance distinct from
control characterization.
