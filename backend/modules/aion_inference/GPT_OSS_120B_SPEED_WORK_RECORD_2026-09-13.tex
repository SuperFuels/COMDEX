\subsection{All-layer correction probes}

36 layers. Fitting/probe prompts were from different families, both in the
declared training split. No sealed holdout was read and no accuracy-policy
retuning occurred.

The policy uses at least four and at most 32 fitting calls per rank/expert,
regularization $10^{-3}$, and maximum-anchor activation cosine at least
0.80. For omitted contribution $c$, prediction $\widehat c$ and full teacher
MoE output $y$, the local metric is
\[
 e=\frac{\lVert\widehat c-c\rVert_2}{\max(\lVert y\rVert_2,10^{-30})}.

plus the existing payload check. That check uses one quarter of an
approximate 13.25\,MiB expert budget, not a measured compute-cost ratio.

\subsection{Joined gate/up projection kernel}
The existing native implementation already uses caller-owned weight buffers,
avoiding roughly 53\,MiB of copying per four-expert layer call. Earlier
reusable-graph microbenchmarks reported approximately 1--5\% gains without
meeting their promotion criterion; these are not new successes today.

The new candidate combines two projections of the same input:
\[
 g=W_gx+b_g,\quad u=W_ux+b_u,\quad
 z=\begin{bmatrix}W_g\\W_u\end{bmatrix}x,
 \quad g=z_{1:d}+b_g,\quad u=z_{d+1:2d}+b_u,
 \qquad d=2880.
\]
SwiGLU, down projection, float32 biases, original MXFP4 bytes, gate values,
left-associated expert summation and residual addition are retained.
SwiGLU constants remain 1.702 and 7.0. No experts are dropped and no weights
are requantized. Reduced duplicate input preparation/dispatch is the
hypothesis; its components were not separately timed.

One writable joined buffer exposes adjacent gate/up views whose lengths sum
to the original lengths. Cache accounting is unchanged. Packing and byte
checks happen on admission, not every resident call. Two unit tests passed
for unchanged bytes/size, native adjacency and incompatible-length rejection.
The native library compiled and loaded.

Real business inputs at (position, layer) $(20,0)$, $(24,12)$ and $(32,24)$
were tested with 2/3/4 active experts. Weights were resident before timing.
Each case had four warmup rounds and 24 measured ABBA rounds. Both kernels
used the same joined storage layout. All repeated outputs were bitwise
identical; four-expert outputs also matched captured teacher outputs.

\section{Later 13 September update: verified speed boundaries and public benchmark}
\label{sec:gptoss-speed-later-20260913}

\subsection{System and representation}
The evaluated system is GPT-OSS 120B in the verified Q4\_K\_M/MXFP4 GGUF
warehouse on removable SD storage, running locally on an 18\,GB M3 Pro Mac.
The runtime keeps attention, router, embedding, output and the current active
expert material in unified memory; it does not place the complete 120B model
in RAM. The normal full-model experiment used an 8\,GiB transient expert cache
and a 40\,GiB internal persistent cache. The adaptive experiment additionally
uses a predeclared 8\,GiB selected-expert pool. These are distinct operating
modes and must never be collapsed into one advertised rate.

\subsection{Joined-template CPU kernel}
The promoted experimental implementation combines adjacent gate/up MXFP4
projections, retains a CPU worker pool, and reuses one GGML graph template per
active-expert count while rebinding every caller-owned input, expert component
and gate pointer before execution. This avoids graph construction and worker
startup on each expert call without retaining stale model buffers. Nine
real layer/route/count microcases were bitwise equal to their controls and
had a median 1.10535 speed ratio. The conditional 64-position adaptive ABBA
then produced a warm median of 10.65340 tokens/s for the original path and
11.55811 tokens/s for the joined-template path, a ratio of 1.08492.

Longer one-prompt extensions remained bitwise equal: the 128-position ratio
was 1.07069 and the 256-position ratio was 1.07417. These results establish
a repeatable approximately 7--8.5\% implementation gain for the specified
warm adaptive benchmark. They do not establish normal unrestricted throughput
or completed-answer quality: the 256-position response did not reach a valid
assistant completion marker. The 11.56 tokens/s value is consequently a
\emph{benchmark-specific warm adaptive rate}, not a general public claim.
Evidence roots are
\path{results/aion_joined_template_projection_gate_20260913},
\path{results/aion_template_128_generation_gate_20260913}, and
\path{results/aion_template_256_generation_gate_20260913}.

\subsection{Persistent-cache delivery improvements}
On a storage-heavy exact trace, a 40\,GiB persistent cache was tested with
the original copied-frame reader and with a reusable four-slot route arena.
The arena performs direct process-verified \texttt{preadv} reads into reusable
buffers, avoiding a whole-frame allocation and subsequent component copies.
An ABBA experiment retained exact generated tokens, routes, layer outputs,
final hidden states and logits in all four runs. The copied reader's median
was approximately 0.97 tokens/s; the reusable-arena median was approximately
2.44 tokens/s, a 2.5$\times$ improvement for this warm, loading-heavy trace.

An additional exact ABBA experiment overlapped the later cached-expert read
with the earlier calculation. It raised the median from approximately 2.26 to
2.39 tokens/s, about 6\%. This result applies to the full-model delivery path,
not to the selected-expert adaptive benchmark where the relevant experts are
already resident. Evidence is in
\path{results/aion_reusable_arena_l2_40gib_20260913}.
