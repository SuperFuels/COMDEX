\section{Expert Atlas as a Consequence Selector}
\label{sec:gptoss120b-atlas-consequence-selector}

The stopped local Taylor Atlas was given one final, distinct role.  Its
approximate vectors were not injected into GPT-OSS 120B.  Instead, AION used
them only to estimate whether omitting a suffix of the unrestricted top-four
route would be harmless.  Every retained contribution was still calculated by
the corresponding original MXFP4 expert, and the unrestricted four-expert path
remained the fallback.

Threshold selection used 22 frozen extraction positions at layer 12.  The
selected threshold was then applied once to 21 unseen reasoning positions.
Business and writing remained blind.  The required bound was relative
layer-output error no greater than 0.02.

An initial report was invalidated because its p95-only selection rule hid one
failure in the small 22-position cohort.  That rule selected 0.05 even though
one extraction position had 0.038425 relative error.  The invalid report is
preserved with an \texttt{INVALID} sidecar and is not evidence.  The corrected
protocol requires the maximum selection-cohort error to pass the 0.02 limit.

Under the corrected rule, the selected threshold was 0.0025.  It retained all
four original experts at all 22 extraction positions and all 21 unseen
reasoning positions.  The outputs were consequently exact, but estimated
expert-traffic reduction was exactly $1.00\times$.  A threshold of 0.05 would
have removed one expert at six of 21 reasoning positions, but its reasoning
p95 error was 0.073344 and its maximum error was 0.077207, for only a
$1.0769\times$ estimated traffic reduction.

The decision is \texttt{STOP\_ATLAS\_CONSEQUENCE\_SELECTOR}.  The compact atlas
cannot reliably certify its own approximation error outside the family on
which its threshold is selected.  It must therefore not control expert
omission in the runtime.  This result closes the current activation-function
Atlas family without weakening the exact fallback or opening the final blind
cohorts.
