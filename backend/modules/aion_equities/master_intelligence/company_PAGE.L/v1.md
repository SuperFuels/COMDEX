1) How The Business Makes Money
Revenue drivers:
PageGroup is a pure-play specialist recruitment business. Revenue is called gross profit — the fee earned on each placement, not total billings. Gross profit equals the placement fee on permanent roles or the margin on temporary contractor day rates. Volume of placements times average fee rate. No product, no inventory, no capital intensity. The business is entirely human capital and relationships.
Two placement types with structurally different economics. Permanent placement — one-off fee typically 15-20% of candidate annual salary, recognised immediately on acceptance of offer. Temporary/contract placement — ongoing margin on daily rate for duration of contract, more recurring but lower margin per head. Current mix is 70% permanent, 30% temporary. Permanent is higher margin and higher volatility. Temporary is lower margin and more defensive.
Margin drivers:
Operating margin is almost entirely a function of gross profit per fee earner (productivity) versus the cost of employing those fee earners (salary plus bonus plus overhead). When markets are good, productivity rises and margin expands dramatically due to operational leverage — the fee earner base is relatively fixed in the short term. When markets turn, productivity falls but the cost base is sticky — margin collapses quickly. This is the defining financial characteristic of the business and the primary source of analytical edge.
Fee earner headcount is management's primary lever. They cut headcount when markets deteriorate and rebuild when markets recover. The timing of headcount decisions versus market recovery is the critical management quality question — cut too early and you miss the recovery; cut too late and you destroy capital.
Cash conversion drivers:
Highly cash generative in normal times. Working capital is minimal — debtors are placement fees owed, creditors are salaries owed. Net cash position of £95m at end of Q4 2024 despite a severe revenue downturn. No debt. This balance sheet strength is a strategic asset — allows them to hold headcount through cycles rather than being forced to cut.
Capital allocation drivers:
Dividend is the primary return mechanism. PageGroup pays a special dividend when cash exceeds operating requirements. In downturns the special dividend is suspended — ordinary dividend maintained. Share buybacks are used opportunistically. No acquisitions of scale — organic growth is the model. Cost base restructuring during downturns — SSC closure in Q4 2024 is an example.

3) Exposure Map
3.1 FX Exposure
Reporting currency: GBP (Sterling)
PageGroup reports in GBP but generates revenue across 37 countries. The FX exposure is less complex than Unilever but still material — in Q4 2024 FX movements reduced reported gross profit by 4.2 percentage points or £9.8m on a £196.7m reported number. That is significant.
EUR/GBP — EMEA approximately 55% of group
EMEA reports primarily in EUR. France 15% of group, Germany 12%, Benelux, Southern Europe, Middle East. EUR strength against GBP is a tailwind on reported numbers — EMEA gross profit translates into more GBP. EUR weakness is a headwind. Given the Brexit period history and current EUR/GBP volatility this is the primary FX variable.
USD/GBP — Americas approximately 18% of group
North America 10%, Latin America 8%. USD revenues translate at average quarterly rate. USD strength against GBP is a tailwind.
AUD/GBP — Asia Pacific approximately 15% of group
Australia is a meaningful part of APAC. AUD weakness against GBP is a headwind — demonstrated in the -20.8% reported versus -16.9% constant currency for FY2024 APAC.
Composite FX basket:
Unlike Unilever the FX exposure is simpler and less dominant as a driver. The primary driver of PageGroup results is market activity, not currency translation. FX is secondary. However it can swing reported numbers by 3-5 percentage points in volatile periods as demonstrated in Q4 2024.
3.2 Commodity and Input Cost Exposure
None material. This is a people business. The only meaningful cost is people — salaries, bonuses, office space. No commodity inputs. No supply chain. This makes the analytical model simpler and cleaner than Unilever.
Office costs are relatively fixed. Technology investment is increasing — Customer Connect platform, Page Insights data platform, AI tools referenced in Q4 2024 CEO statement. These are investments not variable costs.
3.3 Geographic Concentration
EMEA — 55% of group (the critical region)
France 15% of group — the single most important market. Has been the primary drag in 2024 declining 17% in Q4. France is highly cyclical in professional recruitment, heavily influenced by business confidence and labour market regulation. Recovery in France is the single most powerful positive catalyst for group results.
Germany 12% of group — declined 23% in Q4 2024, the worst performing major market. Germany's industrial recession is the driver. Recovery requires either German domestic demand improvement or export market recovery.
Leading indicators for EMEA: France INSEE Business Confidence (monthly), Germany IFO Business Climate (monthly), Eurozone PMI Services Employment component (monthly), REC equivalent data where available for European markets.
Americas — 18% of group
US 10% — the bright spot, grew 3% in Q4 2024. Engineering, Accounting, Financial Services sectors positive. US market is more resilient because labour market remains tighter and professional hiring is less discretionary than in Europe.
Latin America 8% — volatile, Brazil and Mexico the key markets. Excluding Argentina hyperinflation distortion, broadly declining but Mexico showing sequential improvement.
Leading indicators: US Conference Board Employment Trends Index (monthly), US job openings (JOLTS monthly).
Asia Pacific — 15% of group
Greater China 4% of group — declined 23% in Q4. China property sector and consumer confidence weakness the driver. Recovery requires Chinese domestic demand improvement.
Australia declining 25% in Q4 — significant underperformer. Resource sector and financial services softness.
Japan and SE Asia more resilient — Japan -6%, SE Asia -4%.
Leading indicators: China NBS PMI Employment component (monthly), ANZ job advertisements Australia (monthly).
UK — 12% of group
Michael Page brand -9%, Page Personnel brand -22% in Q4. Page Personnel is the volume lower-end brand and is suffering disproportionately — consistent with a market where lower-skilled permanent hiring freezes first.
Leading indicators: REC/KPMG UK Report on Jobs — permanent placements index (monthly, published first Monday of each month, approximately 3-4 weeks after the reference month).

6) What Good Looks Like
The recovery signal sequence (based on Q4 2024 starting point):
Stage 1 — stabilisation: Gross profit declines narrow to single digits year on year. France and Germany stop accelerating downward. Productivity flat versus prior year.
Stage 2 — inflection: REC UK Report on Jobs permanent placement index moves above 50 (expansion). France INSEE confidence recovers above -20. Fee earner headcount stabilises — no further cuts. Gross profit per fee earner turns positive year on year for first time.
Stage 3 — recovery confirmation: Group gross profit positive year on year in constant currency for first time. France positive. Germany stabilising. US growing 5%+. Permanent mix holding at 70%+.
Numeric thresholds for "good":

Group gross profit growth: positive in constant currency — breaking the trend of consecutive quarterly declines
France gross profit growth: better than -10% — France recovery is the most powerful group lever
Productivity (gross profit per fee earner): positive year on year — the key operating leverage indicator
Fee earner headcount: stable or growing — management confidence signal
REC permanent placements index: above 50 for 2+ consecutive months

Re-rating condition:
Two consecutive quarters of group gross profit growth in constant currency, France recovering, productivity positive, headcount stable. At that point the market re-rates from "distressed cyclical" to "recovering cyclical" and the multiple expansion is rapid and significant. PageGroup has re-rated 40-60% from trough in previous cycles within 12 months of this confirmation.

7) What Bad Looks Like
Thesis-breaking conditions:

France accelerating downward beyond -25% — signals structural not cyclical problem
Fee earner headcount cut more than 5% in a single quarter — management has lost confidence in near-term recovery, capacity destruction that will limit upside capture
Productivity declining more than 15% year on year — operating leverage working in reverse at maximum intensity
Temporary recruitment declining faster than permanent for multiple quarters — defensive segment also failing means market-wide freeze not sector rotation
Net cash declining — balance sheet strength deteriorating, financial flexibility reducing
Operating profit guidance withdrawn entirely — management cannot see forward with any confidence

The structural risk:
AI displacement of professional recruitment is the long-term existential risk management reference in Q4 2024 — "deploy AI and automation tools into our working environment." This is double-edged. It could reduce PageGroup's own costs, or it could reduce client demand for recruitment services. The AOT score needs monitoring here. Currently this is a medium-term risk not an immediate thesis-breaker but it requires explicit tracking.

8) Sensitivity Coefficients
France gross profit — 1 percentage point improvement in growth rate:
France is 15% of group gross profit. A 1pp improvement in France constant currency growth rate is approximately 0.15pp improvement in group gross profit growth rate. Because France is the largest single market and has the highest operational leverage, the actual impact is larger than the revenue weight suggests — France fee earner costs are largely fixed so revenue recovery falls almost entirely to operating profit.
Fee earner productivity — 1% improvement versus prior year:
With approximately 5,370 fee earners and average gross profit per fee earner of approximately £157k per year (£842.5m / 5,370 headcount), a 1% improvement in productivity is approximately £8.4m of incremental gross profit — at near-100% incremental margin given the fixed cost base.
Headcount — 100 additional fee earners:
100 additional fee earners at £157k average productivity is approximately £15.7m of incremental annual gross profit capacity. But headcount takes 6-12 months to become fully productive so the in-year benefit is partial.
EUR/GBP — 1% EUR strengthening against GBP:
EMEA is 55% of gross profit. A 1% EUR appreciation against GBP adds approximately 0.55% to reported group gross profit. In Q4 2024 FX was a -4.2pp drag — that represents approximately 4% average EUR/GBP move against PageGroup.

The pre-announcement edge window:
REC Report on Jobs is the most powerful leading indicator. Published the first Monday of each month, it covers the prior month. PageGroup's quarterly trading update covers a 3-month period and is published approximately 2-3 weeks after the quarter ends. This means you have 2-3 REC reports covering the quarter before PageGroup reports. If REC permanent placements index is recovering, PageGroup UK revenue is recovering. France and Germany are not covered by REC but INSEE and IFO serve an equivalent function.
